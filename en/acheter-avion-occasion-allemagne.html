The German used-aircraft market

Germany is one of the most active light aviation markets in Europe, driven by a dense network of airfields and a strong general aviation tradition (flying clubs, microlights, touring single-engine aircraft). The most commonly listed used models are the classics of the European market: Cessna 172, Piper PA-28, Diamond DA40, along with a substantial supply of two-seaters and microlights. The density of supply often allows several similar aircraft to be compared before deciding, a real advantage for negotiation.

Listings can be found on specialist marketplaces, through regional flying clubs or by word of mouth on the airfields. As everywhere in Europe, the asking price remains a starting point for negotiation, especially for an aircraft that has been for sale for a long time.

Average prices by aircraft type

Price ranges in Germany remain broadly aligned with those seen elsewhere in the European Union for comparable aircraft, sometimes with a premium for aircraft well equipped with recent avionics (glass cockpit), which are highly sought after in this market.

Tip — An aircraft's price depends above all on engine history (hours remaining before TBO), airframe condition and installed avionics far more than on the build year alone. Always compare at equivalent engine potential.

Registration with the LBA

Every aircraft based and operated in Germany must be registered with the Luftfahrt-Bundesamt (LBA), the German civil aviation authority, under the D- prefix. For a purchase between private individuals where the aircraft is already registered in Germany, the transfer consists of a simple update of the registered owner with the LBA. For an aircraft imported from another country, deregistration from the register of origin is required before the new registration, as with any registration transfer in Europe.

  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and ARC — check this even before signing; the expiry date determines the first flight under your name.
  • Bill of sale — dated, signed by both parties, stating the serial number.
  • Liability insurance certificate — required before first use under the new registration.
Good to know — Common EASA rules govern the transfer between member states and make it simpler than an import from outside the European Union. Generally allow 2 to 8 weeks depending on how complete the file is, a timeline comparable to that observed for any registration in Europe.

VAT and taxation

A sale between private individuals in Germany is generally not subject to German VAT (Umsatzsteuer). However, a sale carried out by a professional or a company may be subject to it, and an aircraft imported from a country outside the European Union remains subject to import VAT calculated on its customs value. It is recommended to have your tax situation confirmed before acquisition, particularly if the aircraft has already changed country of registration several times.

SituationTax treatment
Sale between EU resident private individualsGenerally VAT-exempt
Sale by a professionalVAT potentially applicable
Import from outside the EU (e.g. USA)Import VAT, standard national rate

Pre-purchase inspection and documents

Before signing anything, a pre-purchase inspection by a certified mechanic independent of the seller remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises. It covers the condition of the airframe, the consistency of the engine logbook, maintenance history and the compliance of the avionics with current European requirements (transponder, 8.33 kHz radio).

  • Up-to-date airframe, engine and propeller logbooks, with no chronological gaps.
  • History of maintenance invoices from the last three years.
  • No undisclosed structural damage (verifiable via the maintenance file and a thorough visual inspection).
Point of caution — Never finalise a purchase without having seen the original logbooks and having their consistency checked by a professional: it is the best indicator of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained in the past.

Pitfalls to avoid

The most common mistakes when buying in Germany mirror those seen everywhere in Europe: neglecting to check deregistration from the previous register for an imported aircraft, underestimating certificate of airworthiness transfer fees, or signing without having the exact condition of the optional equipment listed in the technical sheet confirmed in writing.
