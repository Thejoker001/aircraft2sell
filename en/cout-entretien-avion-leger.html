How much does it cost to maintain a light aircraft per year

Real annual budget to maintain a light aircraft: maintenance, insurance, hangar, fuel. Detailed cost estimates by aircraft type.

The main expense categories

Owning a light aircraft involves a budget that goes well beyond the purchase price alone. Beyond mandatory maintenance, you need to factor in insurance, parking, fuel and a reserve for major upcoming costs (engine overhaul, avionics). The rule of thumb most often quoted in the industry is to budget between 8% and 15% of the aircraft's purchase value every year, excluding flight hours.

Maintenance and mandatory inspections

The annual inspection (mandatory to maintain airworthiness) is the most predictable expense: generally budget between €800 and €2,500 for a light single-engine aircraft depending on the workshop and the condition of the aircraft, excluding spare parts if defects are found.

  • Annual inspection (100h/annual) — €800 to €2,500 depending on the aircraft and the approved workshop.
  • Engine overhaul reserve (TBO) — to be spread over the remaining hours before overhaul: budget €15,000 to €40,000 for a complete overhaul depending on the engine, divided by the hours remaining until due.
  • Consumables and small parts — tires, filters, spark plugs, battery: €300 to €800 per year with regular use.
  • Avionics and instruments — GPS database updates, calibrations: €100 to €400 per year.

Fixed costs: hangar and insurance

These two items are due whether the aircraft flies or not, and vary significantly depending on the region and the type of coverage chosen.

ItemAnnual range
Enclosed hangar€1,500 to €4,000
Outdoor parking spot€400 to €1,200
Liability + hull insurance€1,000 to €3,500 depending on the insured value and the pilot's experience
Flying club membership (if club-based)€150 to €500
Tip — The cost of hull insurance depends directly on your declared annual flight hours and your experience: an up-to-date logbook with a recent rating can significantly reduce the premium.

Variable costs: fuel and flight hours

Fuel remains the variable most dependent on your actual usage. A piston single-engine aircraft consumes on average between 25 and 40 liters/hour; at the price of avgas in Europe (often €2.20 to €2.80/L in 2026), one flight hour therefore represents €60 to €110 in fuel alone, on top of landing and parking fees at stopovers.

Indicative annual budget by aircraft type

These estimates cover maintenance, insurance and hangar for moderate private use (50 to 100 hours/year), excluding fuel and excluding the engine overhaul reserve.

Aircraft typeIndicative annual budget
Microlight€2,000 to €4,000
Light single-engine (e.g. Cessna 172, Piper PA-28)€5,000 to €9,000
High-performance single-engine (e.g. Cirrus SR22)€9,000 to €16,000
Light twin-engine€15,000 to €30,000
Turboprop€40,000 and up

How to keep your budget under control

Co-ownership (sharing the aircraft between several pilots) remains the most effective way to reduce your individual budget while flying an aircraft more capable than you could afford alone. Choosing a maintenance workshop away from major airport hubs (often more expensive) and setting aside a monthly reserve for major upcoming costs also help avoid cash-flow surprises.

Point of caution — Before buying, always ask the seller for the complete maintenance invoice history of the last three years: it is the best indicator of the real maintenance cost of this specific aircraft, far more reliable than a general average.
