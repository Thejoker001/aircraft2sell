Used Beechcraft Bonanza: buying guide

Why the Bonanza remains a benchmark

The Beechcraft Bonanza is one of the most iconic single-engine piston aircraft in light aviation, produced without interruption since the late 1940s across its various evolutions. Its reputation rests on a sturdy all-metal construction, a cabin comfortable for four to six occupants depending on the version, and cruise performance that makes it a popular aircraft for family or professional trips over medium distances.

On the used market, the Bonanza comes in many model years and variants produced over several decades, from the historic V-tail silhouette to more recent conventional-tail versions. This long production run explains an abundant supply but also a wide disparity in prices depending on age, condition and the equipment level of each aircraft.

What budget to expect for the purchase

The price of a used Bonanza varies significantly depending on the year of manufacture, total airframe and engine hours, the remaining potential before engine overhaul, and the level of avionics installed. The oldest aircraft, with reduced engine potential and original avionics, trade at a significantly more accessible entry level than the more recent, well-equipped and better-powered versions, which represent a much larger investment.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget must include additional costs: a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance, and possibly an avionics or cabin interior upgrade depending on the condition of the aircraft in question.

Points to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For a Bonanza, some examples of which can be quite old, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Full engine history: hours elapsed since the last overhaul, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance programme, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Condition of the structure and flight controls, especially on older V-tail examples, some of whose specific parts can be harder to find.
  • Presence and compliance with airworthiness directives (technical bulletins) specific to the model, to be confirmed by the maintenance shop.
  • Condition and updates of the installed avionics, as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements (transponder, traffic surveillance).
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no grey areas regarding the aircraft's history and any previous changes of ownership.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about the general condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against an unpleasant surprise after purchase, especially on an aircraft whose history spans several decades.

The main Bonanza families on the market

The Bonanza range actually includes several families of aircraft produced over a long period, with cabin, engine and equipment evolutions from one generation to the next. Before setting a budget, it is worth clearly identifying the variant sought and its place in the range, as price gaps between the earliest generations and the most recent, best-equipped versions can be very substantial.

Some variants also stand out for a different payload capacity or engine from the base model, which directly influences the intended use (family trips, professional missions, payload) and therefore the profile of buyer being sought.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a Bonanza remains broadly comparable to other high-performance single-engine aircraft in its category, with particular attention to be paid to parts availability on the oldest examples. The main expense items remain the mandatory annual inspection, insurance, parking and, in the longer term, the engine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, documented maintenance tracking is a strong argument when it comes to a future resale, all the more so on an older aircraft where a fully traceable technical file makes a real difference in value.

Tip — Always ask for the last three maintenance invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The Bonanza benefits from an active used market in Europe as well as in the United States, driven by a very large installed fleet and generally good parts availability, despite the wide range of model years in circulation. A well-maintained aircraft with a transparent history and up-to-date avionics generally resells more easily and on better terms than an aircraft whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models, of the same model year and the same equipment level, remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
