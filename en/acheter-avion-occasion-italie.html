The Italian used aircraft market

Italy has a dense network of flying clubs and private aerodromes, particularly in the north (Lombardy, Veneto, Piedmont), where light aviation remains very active. The used market there has historically been driven by classic touring single-engines (Cessna 172, Piper PA-28) and by a notable supply of locally built aircraft, such as Tecnam models, which benefit from a good maintenance network across the country.

Listings circulate through specialist marketplaces, flying clubs affiliated with the Aero Club d'Italia (AeCI) and the network of approved maintenance workshops. As everywhere in Europe, the listed price remains a starting point for negotiation, especially for an aircraft that has been on the market for a long time.

Average prices by aircraft type

Price ranges observed in Italy remain broadly close to those in the rest of the European Union for comparable aircraft. The overall condition of the airframe and the remaining engine potential before overhaul (TBO) weigh much more heavily on price than the year of manufacture alone.

Tip — Always compare at an equivalent engine potential (hours remaining before TBO): this is the factor that most influences an aircraft's real price, far more than its year of manufacture.

Registration with ENAC

Any aircraft based and operated in Italy must be registered with ENAC (Ente Nazionale per l'Aviazione Civile), under the I- prefix. For a purchase between private individuals of an aircraft already registered in Italy, the transfer consists of updating the holder in the national aeronautical register (RAN). For an aircraft imported from another country, prior de-registration in the register of origin is required, as with any intra-European transfer.

  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and ARC — to be checked before signing, the expiry date determines the first flight under your name.
  • Bill of sale — dated, signed by both parties, stating the serial number.
  • Liability insurance certificate — required before first use under the new registration.
Good to know — Common EASA rules govern the transfer between member states and simplify it compared with an import from outside the European Union. Generally expect 2 to 8 weeks depending on how complete the file submitted to ENAC is.

IVA and taxation

A sale between private individuals in Italy is generally not subject to IVA (Italian VAT, standard rate of 22%). However, a sale made by a professional or a company may be subject to it, and an aircraft imported from a country outside the European Union remains subject to import IVA calculated on its customs value. It is recommended to have your tax situation confirmed before acquisition, particularly if the aircraft has already changed registration country several times.

SituationTax treatment
Sale between EU resident private individualsGenerally exempt from IVA
Sale by a professionalIVA potentially applicable (22%)
Import from outside the EU (e.g. USA)Import IVA, standard rate

Pre-purchase inspection and documents

Before any signature, a pre-purchase inspection by a licensed mechanic independent of the seller remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises. It covers the condition of the airframe, the consistency of the engine logbook, maintenance history and the compliance of the avionics with current European requirements (transponder, 8.33 kHz radio).

  • Up-to-date airframe, engine and propeller logbooks, with no chronological gaps.
  • Maintenance invoice history for the last three years.
  • No undeclared structural damage (verifiable via the maintenance file and a thorough visual inspection).
Point of caution — Never finalise a purchase without having seen the original logbooks and had their consistency checked by a professional: this is the best indicator of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Pitfalls to avoid

The most common mistakes when buying in Italy mirror those seen throughout Europe: neglecting to check the de-registration from the previous register for an imported aircraft, underestimating the delays and costs of the transfer with ENAC, or signing without having the exact condition of the optional equipment listed in the technical sheet confirmed in writing.
