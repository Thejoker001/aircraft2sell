What is a weight-shift microlight (trike)

The weight-shift microlight (or "trike") is an ultralight aircraft made up of two distinct elements: a flexible delta wing fitted above a powered carriage that carries the pilot, any passenger, and the engine. Control is through shifting the body's weight via a control bar, a principle very different from the stick of a conventional aircraft. It is historically one of the most accessible categories of light aviation, both to buy and to maintain.

The used market is active in France, Belgium and other French-speaking countries, with aircraft ranging from leisure two-seaters to school machines and trikes equipped for aerial photography or cross-country flying. Before buying, it is worth clearly distinguishing the wing (fabric, to be replaced periodically) from the carriage (structure, engine, instruments), since their condition and age do not necessarily follow the same schedule.

Purchase budget by age and condition

The price of a used weight-shift microlight depends above all on the age of the wing, the number of engine hours and the level of equipment (instruments, emergency parachute, one- or two-seater). An older carriage with a worn wing will sell for significantly less than a recent aircraft equipped for navigation and safety.

Older carriage, worn wing, little equipmentEntry level of the used market

Beyond the purchase price, plan for replacing the wing if its condition or age requires it, engine overhaul, routine maintenance (propeller, landing gear, wiring) and hangar or parking fees at an airfield. These items can make up a significant share of the overall budget over the ownership period.

What to check before buying

A weight-shift microlight is inspected differently from an aircraft: the wing and the carriage must each be inspected separately, ideally with the help of a mechanic or an instructor experienced with this type of machine.

  • Condition of the wing fabric: discolouration, porosity, signs of prolonged UV or moisture exposure, wear on seams and rigging cables.
  • Carriage history: engine hours, maintenance carried out, parts replaced, availability of tracking documents.
  • Engine condition: leaks, abnormal vibration, maintenance logbook and dates of the last overhauls.
  • Landing gear, wheels, brakes and carriage structure: absence of corrosion or deformation.
  • Presence and validity of an emergency parachute, if the aircraft is fitted with one.
  • A test flight with the seller or an instructor before finalising, if at all possible.
Good to know — The wing of a weight-shift microlight has a limited lifespan over time and with use, independently of the carriage's condition. Always ask for its manufacturing date and its history of UV exposure.

Regulation and registration

In France, microlights fall under specific regulations, separate from those for certified aircraft: simplified registration, no conventional certificate of airworthiness, but declaration obligations and a technical check under the rules in force in the country of operation. Rules vary from one European country to another (France, Belgium, Switzerland and Germany each have their own frameworks), so it is essential to check the applicable regulations before a cross-border purchase.

When ownership changes, remember to transfer the declaration or registration with the relevant authority and check that the aircraft is up to date with any required inspections. An aircraft sold without up-to-date documents can delay, or even complicate, getting it back in the air.

Insurance and training

Flying a weight-shift microlight requires a licence and a rating specific to this class of aircraft, separate from a standard private pilot licence. If you are new to this type of flying, additional training with a qualified trike instructor is generally required before taking the controls alone, even if you already hold a licence for another type of aircraft.

Third-party liability insurance is mandatory to operate a microlight. Hull cover (for the aircraft itself) remains optional but recommended, particularly for a recent or valuable aircraft. The cost of insurance depends on the pilot's experience, the use (leisure, school) and the cover chosen.

Where to find a used weight-shift microlight

The market is split between private listings, microlight clubs and schools renewing their fleet. Favouring a seller able to provide a complete, transparent history — tracking logbook, maintenance invoices, wing manufacturing date — significantly reduces the risk of a bad surprise after purchase. Comparing several similar listings also helps form a realistic picture of prevailing prices at the time of purchase.

Negotiating and completing the purchase

Once the inspection has been carried out, the main points to use in negotiation are the age of the wing, the condition of the engine and the completeness of the tracking documents. An aircraft without a clear history should be negotiated down, or passed over, if doubt remains over key safety points.

At the point of sale, draft a contract specifying the identity of the parties, a description of the aircraft (carriage and wing), the price, any warranties and the date of transfer of ownership. Keep a copy of all documents handed over by the previous owner: they will be useful for future maintenance and for a later resale.
