Why the DR400 remains a benchmark

Built by Robin Aviation (now Robin Aircraft) since the late 1960s, the DR400 is one of the most common light aircraft in France and French-speaking Europe. Its mixed wood-fabric-metal construction, its cantilevered low wing with no struts or bracing wires, and its sliding canopy make it an easily recognisable aircraft, used both by flying clubs for flight training and by private owners for touring. Several thousand examples have been produced, which guarantees good parts availability and a network of maintenance workshops familiar with the type in France, Belgium and Switzerland.

The main versions

The DR400 has gone through many variants over the decades, differing mainly in engine and intended use (training, touring, glider towing).

Purchase budget by age and airframe hours

The used market for the DR400 is active, with a wide price range depending on year of construction, airframe and engine hours, and installed avionics. A 1970s-80s example with a well-maintained airframe and an engine close to its hourly limit (TBO) sells for noticeably less than a more recent model with glass cockpit avionics.

  • Total airframe hours and hours since the last major overhaul.
  • Hours remaining before the next engine overhaul (Lycoming TBO generally around 2,000 hours).
  • Condition of the fabric covering on fabric-winged versions, and of the wood structure (moisture, delamination).

Maintenance and recurring costs

Like most light aircraft registered in Europe, the DR400 is maintained under a Part-M/Part-ML programme with a mandatory annual inspection. The mixed wood-fabric construction requires particular attention to moisture and ageing of the covering, which must be recovered periodically depending on its condition.

Good to know — Before any purchase, ask for the complete airframe and engine logbooks as well as the latest annual inspection report. A workshop approved on the type can estimate the work needed on the covering and the engine.

Points to watch before buying

Beyond the standard technical inspection (see our guide on the pre-purchase inspection), a few points are specific to the DR400:

  • History of corrosion on metal fittings, especially after prolonged outdoor parking.
  • Compliance with applied modifications and service bulletins (EASA airworthiness directives).
  • Compatibility of the installed avionics with current requirements (Mode S transponder, 8.33 kHz radio).

Suited for which use

The DR400 suits both initial flight training (many French clubs use it as a training aircraft) and family touring on the more powerful 4-seat versions. The towing versions remain sought after by gliding clubs. Before deciding, compare several listings to refine your budget according to your intended use.
