Used Cirrus SR22: buying guide and budget

Budget to plan for, inspection points to watch, routine maintenance and resale outlook to help you buy a used Cirrus SR22 with full knowledge of the facts.

Why the SR22 appeals to so many buyers

The Cirrus SR22 is one of the best-selling single-engine piston aircraft in the world since its launch in the early 2000s. Its composite structure, built-in emergency parachute and modern avionics have made it a preferred choice for owner-pilots looking for a fast, comfortable and well-equipped aircraft for family or business trips over long distances.

On the used market, the SR22 comes in several successive generations, with notable developments in avionics, engine (turbocharged or not) and standard equipment. This diversity of model years explains a wide price range, which makes it essential to target your budget carefully before starting a search.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used SR22 depends above all on the age of the aircraft, total airframe and engine hours, engine status (remaining potential before overhaul), and the level of avionics fitted. The oldest models, with reduced engine potential, trade at a noticeably more accessible entry level than well-equipped recent versions, which can represent a far larger investment.

Age of the aircraftMore recent generations trade noticeably higher

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: an independent mechanic's pre-purchase inspection, registration and transfer fees, insurance, and possibly an avionics or cabin interior refresh if the aircraft needs one.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For an SR22, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Condition of the composite structure: look for any impacts, delamination or previous repairs, which can be hard to detect without a specialist inspection.
  • Complete engine history: hours elapsed since the last overhaul, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Operation and expiry date of the parachute system's rockets, which must be replaced at regular intervals.
  • Condition and updates of the integrated avionics, as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements (transponder, traffic-monitoring systems).
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

The CAPS parachute system: an asset but also a cost

The emergency parachute system built into the SR22 is one of its best-known features. It's a valuable safety argument, but it also requires specific maintenance: the pyrotechnic components and the parachute itself must be overhauled or replaced according to a schedule set by the manufacturer, which represents an expense to plan for over the life of ownership.

Before buying, it's worth asking the seller for the date of the last intervention on this system and the date of the next one, so you can factor this cost into your financing plan if the deadline is approaching.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of an SR22 remains comparable to that of other high-performance single-engine aircraft in its category, with a few specifics related to its composite structure and equipment. The main expense items remain the mandatory annual inspection, insurance, parking and, in the longer term, engine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records are a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the last three maintenance invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The SR22 benefits from an active used market in Europe, supported by a large installed fleet and good parts availability. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and up-to-date avionics, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same model year are almost always explained by remaining engine potential and the quality of the avionics fitted.
