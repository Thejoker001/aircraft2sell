The Finnish used aircraft market

Finland has a solid aeronautical tradition, supported by a dense network of flying clubs (ilmailukerho) spread across the country, from the Helsinki-Malmi area to airfields in the far north. General aviation is particularly dynamic there in summer, with very long flying days above the Arctic Circle, while winter imposes specific operating conditions (snow-covered runways, skis or wheels depending on the season, engine pre-heating before start-up).

The used aircraft market remains modest in size compared with Central Europe, but it is active and well organised around the Finnish Aeronautical Association (Suomen Ilmailuliitto) and small specialist listings. Many Finnish buyers also look at Swedish, Baltic and continental European listings to widen their choice, as Finland is a member of the Schengen area and the European Union, which simplifies the circulation of aircraft between these markets.

Maintenance networks are concentrated around the major airfields (Helsinki-Malmi, Jyväskylä, Tampere-Pirkkala, Oulu, Rovaniemi), with recognised expertise in aircraft adapted to winter operations (de-icing, engine pre-heating, tyres and skis). The availability of certain specific parts can require a longer lead time than in the major Central European markets, something worth anticipating especially during winter.

A point to bear in mind throughout this guide: Finland is a member of the European Union and uses the euro as its currency, which directly simplifies comparing prices with listings from other eurozone countries.

Average prices by aircraft type

Prices in Finland for an equivalent aircraft in terms of condition, airframe hours and remaining engine potential are broadly comparable to those seen in other Nordic countries (Sweden, Norway) and in Northern Europe. Aircraft that have operated in harsh winter conditions deserve particular attention during inspection, especially regarding the condition of flight controls, seals and corrosion linked to runway de-icing salt.

Registration with Traficom

Any aircraft based and operated in Finland must be registered with Traficom (the Finnish Transport and Communications Agency, Finland's civil aviation authority), under the OH- prefix. For an aircraft already registered in Finland, a private purchase results in an update of the holder of the certificate of registration with this authority. For an aircraft coming from abroad, prior deregistration from the register of origin is generally required before any new Finnish registration.

  • Valid, up-to-date certificate of airworthiness, recognised by Traficom.
  • Previous certificate of registration and proof of deregistration if the aircraft comes from another country.
  • Certificate of liability insurance covering operation in Finland, including winter conditions where relevant.

VAT and taxation within the European Union

As Finland is an EU member state, buying an aircraft from another member country generally falls under the intra-EU acquisition regime rather than an import, which simplifies customs procedures compared with a purchase outside the EU (for example from Norway, a Nordic country that is not an EU member).

Finland's standard VAT rate is generally quoted at around 25.5% — this general figure should always be confirmed for the specific case of an aircraft, as exemption or calculation rules can vary depending on whether the transaction is between private individuals, via a professional, or involves an aircraft already cleared through customs and taxed within the Union. It is essential to have the exact tax treatment confirmed by the Finnish tax administration (Verohallinto) before signing, rather than relying on unverified rates for this particular case.

Worth remembering — Always ask for a written assessment of the applicable VAT treatment from a professional before committing, particularly if the aircraft comes from a non-EU Nordic country such as Norway.

Pre-purchase inspection and documents

As with any used aircraft purchase, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by a certified workshop independent of the seller remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises. In Finland, harsh winter conditions (snow, ice, extreme cold) warrant particular attention to corrosion of the airframe and flight controls, to the condition of the engine pre-heating system and any de-icing equipment, as well as to the aircraft's actual storage conditions (a heated hangar is preferable to prolonged outdoor parking in winter).

Documents to gather and check systematically:

  • Complete aircraft logbook, with no gaps in the history.
  • Report of the last annual inspection (Part-M / Part-ML) and any major engine overhauls.
  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and history of airworthiness directives (AD) applied.
  • No disputes or liens on the aircraft, to be checked with Traficom.
  • Storage conditions (heated hangar or outdoors) and any signs of corrosion linked to de-icing salt or winter humidity.

Pitfalls to avoid

The more limited number of aircraft available locally can encourage buyers to rush a purchase decision: take the time nonetheless to compare with Swedish, Baltic and continental European listings before committing. Be wary of incomplete logbooks, particularly regarding winter operating history (whether the engine was systematically pre-heated or not), and of prolonged outdoor parking in a cold, humid climate without suitable corrosion protection.

Worth remembering — A price noticeably below market should always raise questions: reduced engine potential, corrosion linked to winter conditions, work needed on the airframe, or incomplete documentation are the most common causes.
