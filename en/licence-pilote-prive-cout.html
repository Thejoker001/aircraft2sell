Private Pilot Licence (PPL): cost and duration

Budget, duration, steps and advice for obtaining a private pilot licence in Europe.

What is the PPL(A)

The PPL(A) (Private Pilot Licence - Aeroplane) is the basic licence allowing you to fly a light aircraft in a non-remunerated capacity, in Europe and in most countries that recognise EASA licences. It is the mandatory starting point before considering additional ratings (night flying, IFR, multi-engine).

The real budget to plan for

The total cost of a PPL(A) in Europe generally ranges between €8,000 and €15,000, depending on the country, the flying club chosen and the pace of training. This budget includes flight hours, theoretical training, exams and study materials.

Expense itemIndicative budget
Flight hours (minimum 45h required)€6,000 - €11,000
Theoretical training and exams€500 - €1,500
Class 2 medical examination€150 - €300
Equipment (headset, charts, documentation)€300 - €800
Point of caution — The minimum regulatory number of hours (45h) is rarely enough in practice: budget for 55 to 65 hours on average to be ready for solo flight and the final exam.

How long it takes to obtain

With intensive training (several flights per week), a PPL(A) can be obtained in 3 to 6 months. With standard training, at a rate of one or two flights per week at a flying club, expect 12 to 18 months instead. The regularity of sessions matters more than their total number for effective progress.

The steps of training

  • Introductory flights — first dual-control flights with an instructor to discover flying.
  • Theoretical training — 9 subjects (regulations, weather, navigation, flight mechanics...) validated by a multiple-choice exam.
  • First solo — first solo flight, generally after 15 to 20 hours of dual-control flying.
  • Navigation — solo and dual-control flights to other airfields, with full flight-planning preparation.
  • Final flight test — practical test with an approved examiner, validating the award of the licence.

After the PPL: owning your own aircraft

Once the licence is obtained, many pilots consider buying a light aircraft, alone or in co-ownership, to fly more freely than with club rental. This is often more economical from 50 to 80 annual flight hours, depending on the type of aircraft.

Tip — Before buying, calculate the real cost per flight hour (depreciation, maintenance, hangar, insurance) and compare it to your actual expected usage, not just your aspirations.
