Insuring your aircraft in Europe: the complete guide

Liability, hull cover, essential guarantees: how to properly insure your aircraft, helicopter or microlight in Europe.

Essential coverage

Insuring an aircraft in Europe rests on two pillars: liability insurance, compulsory in all EU countries under Regulation (EC) No 785/2004, and hull insurance for the aircraft itself, optional but strongly recommended once the aircraft's value exceeds a few tens of thousands of euros.

Additional options are often added to these two coverages: personal accident cover for pilot and passengers, legal protection, search and rescue cost cover, or "loss of licence" cover for professional pilots.

Aviation liability insurance

This covers damage caused to third parties (on the ground and passengers) in the event of an accident. The minimum coverage amount is set by EU regulation according to the aircraft's maximum take-off weight (MTOW) — the heavier the aircraft, the higher the minimum required cover.

Legal obligation — Flying without valid aviation liability insurance exposes the owner to criminal penalties and grounding of the aircraft. Check the expiry date before each ARC renewal.

Hull insurance

This covers physical damage to the aircraft itself: accident, fire, hail, theft. The premium amount depends on the insured value, the type of aircraft, the pilot's experience and the aircraft's usual parking location.

Two formulas exist: "ground and flight" cover (comprehensive) and "ground only" cover, which is cheaper and useful during a period when the aircraft is grounded or for sale.

What affects the premium

  • Pilot experience — total hours, hours on type, ratings (IFR, multi-engine).
  • Declared use — private, instruction, rental, aerial work: each use has its own rate.
  • Parking — enclosed hangar or outdoor parking, controlled or uncontrolled airfield.
  • Geographic area — Europe, Mediterranean basin or worldwide.
  • Deductible — a higher deductible reduces the premium, provided you can afford it.

Choosing the right insurer

Favour a broker specialising in general aviation, able to compare several insurers (AIG Aviation, Allianz Aviation, and similar specialist providers in your country). Check that the policy properly covers your actual use (private, instruction, rental) and your flying area (Europe, worldwide).

Tip — Always request a quote before finalising a purchase. Insurance cost can be a decisive factor between two similar models.
