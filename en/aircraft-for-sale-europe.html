The European used aircraft market

Europe is home to one of the world's most active general aviation markets, spanning flying clubs renewing their fleets, private owners upgrading, and professional dealers moving business jets and turboprops. Because certified aircraft fall under common EASA airworthiness standards, buying an aircraft for sale in one European country and registering it in another is a well-established, if administrative, process: de-registration in the country of origin, then re-registration once the file — proof of ownership, certificate of airworthiness, airworthiness directive compliance — is complete with the relevant national authority.

Demand varies by category. Light single-engine aircraft (Cessna 172, Piper PA-28, Robin DR400) remain the backbone of flying club fleets. Turboprops and light jets (Pilatus PC-12, TBM, Citation, Phenom) see steady demand from owner-pilots and small operators seeking lower trip costs than larger jets. Helicopters (Robinson R44/R66, Airbus H125) serve training, tourism and utility operators. Ultralights and microlights attract first-time owners drawn by lower acquisition and running costs.

On tax, a private-to-private sale is generally VAT-exempt, while a purchase from a professional may fall under VAT or a margin scheme depending on the aircraft's prior status — always worth clarifying with the seller before committing. Frequently asked questions

What buyers of aircraft for sale in Europe most often ask.

Can I buy an aircraft in one European country and register it in another?
Yes. EASA rules harmonise airworthiness standards across member states, which makes cross-border purchases straightforward for certified aircraft. The seller's aircraft is de-registered in its home country, then re-registered in yours once the file (proof of ownership, certificate of airworthiness, compliance with airworthiness directives) is complete. Expect a few weeks of administrative processing with the relevant national authority.
What should I check before buying a used aircraft for sale in Europe?
Airframe total hours, engine hours since overhaul (SMOH), logbooks and maintenance history, current Airworthiness Review Certificate (ARC), avionics fit, and any outstanding airworthiness directives. An independent pre-purchase inspection remains the best protection before signing, especially for a cross-border transaction.
Does VAT apply when buying an aircraft for sale in Europe?
A private-to-private sale is generally exempt from VAT. A purchase from a professional seller or dealer may be subject to VAT or a margin scheme depending on the country and the aircraft's prior tax status. Always ask the seller to clarify the tax position before committing to a transaction.
