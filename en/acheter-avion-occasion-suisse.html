Buying a used aircraft in Switzerland: prices and registration

Market, prices, registration with FOCA and VAT: the particularities of a purchase in Switzerland, a country outside the European Union but fully integrated into the European airspace.

The Swiss used aircraft market

Switzerland has an active community of private aviators, spread across numerous regional airfields and a few larger ones such as Sion, Grenchen or Bex. The light aircraft fleet there is generally newer and better maintained than the European average, supported by high purchasing power and a sufficient number of approved maintenance workshops across the country.

Listings circulate through specialist marketplaces, flying clubs affiliated with the Swiss Aero Club (AeCS) and word of mouth within clubs. As elsewhere in Europe, the overall condition of the airframe and the remaining engine potential weigh far more heavily on the price than the year of manufacture.

Average prices by aircraft type

Prices in Switzerland generally sit at the upper end of what is seen in Western Europe, without a major gap for a comparable aircraft in similar condition and with similar hours.

Registration with FOCA

Any aircraft based and operated in Switzerland must be registered with the Federal Office of Civil Aviation (FOCA), under the HB- prefix. For a purchase between private individuals of an aircraft already registered in Switzerland, the transfer consists of updating the holder in the Swiss aircraft register. For an aircraft imported from an EU member state or a third country, prior deregistration in the register of origin is required.

  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and ARC — Switzerland recognises certificates issued under the common European rules (EASA) through bilateral agreements.
  • Bill of sale — dated, signed by both parties, stating the serial number.
  • Liability insurance certificate — required before the first use under the new registration.
Good to know — Switzerland is not an EU member but participates in the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) through a bilateral agreement: the airworthiness standards applied remain very close to those of the rest of Europe, which simplifies cross-border transfers. Generally allow 2 to 6 weeks depending on how complete the file submitted to FOCA is.

VAT and taxation

Switzerland applies its own VAT (standard rate of 8.1% in 2026), separate from the European Union's VAT regime. A sale between Swiss resident private individuals is generally not subject to VAT, but an aircraft imported from abroad (including from an EU country) is in principle subject to import VAT, calculated on its customs value when crossing the Swiss border. It is recommended to have your tax position validated by the Federal Customs Administration (FCA) before the purchase.

SituationTax treatment
Sale between Swiss resident private individualsGenerally VAT-exempt
Sale by a Swiss professionalVAT potentially applicable (8.1%)
Import from the EU or a third countrySwiss import VAT, standard rate

Particularities of a purchase outside the EU

Since Switzerland is not a member of the European Union or the EU customs area, any movement of an aircraft between Switzerland and an EU country is formally an import-export operation, with the associated customs formalities — even though, in practice, Schengen free movement considerably eases the process for a private pilot. A buyer based in the EU acquiring an aircraft in Switzerland must anticipate these formalities and, where applicable, the payment of VAT in their own destination country.

Point of caution — Always check whether VAT has already been paid in the EU or in Switzerland for the aircraft in question: an aircraft that has already circulated between the two zones without correct tax regularisation can expose the buyer to a subsequent reassessment.

Pitfalls to avoid

The most common mistakes when buying in Switzerland echo those seen elsewhere in Europe, with an added customs dimension: neglecting to check the aircraft's VAT status, underestimating the transfer delays with FOCA for an imported aircraft, or signing without having the exact condition of the optional equipment listed in the technical sheet confirmed in writing.
