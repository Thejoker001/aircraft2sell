The general aviation market in Germany

Germany has one of the largest general aviation fleets in Europe. The airworthiness of certified aircraft falls under EASA, while the national authority, the Luftfahrt-Bundesamt (LBA), manages aircraft registration under the D- prefix and administrative matters. Buying a used aircraft involves checking the certificate of airworthiness, the ARC and the logbooks, then transferring the registration if the aircraft comes from another country.

German tax law applies a VAT regime (Umsatzsteuer) to aircraft that depends on the seller's status: a sale between private individuals is generally not subject to VAT, whereas a sale by a professional may be. It is advisable to confirm your situation before acquisition. German flying clubs (Luftsportvereine) are highly organised and maintain steady demand for touring single-engine aircraft, from classic Cessnas and Pipers to locally built Diamonds. Flight schools are numerous, and training provides a regular outlet for used aircraft.

The trend in the German market is toward modernisation: glass-cockpit avionics, ADS-B and fuel-injected engines are valued. Buyers scrutinise engine hours, inspections and documentation closely, and transferring registration to another European country is made easier by EASA's common rules. Frequently asked questions

What buyers of aircraft in Germany most often ask.

What are the steps to buy a used aircraft in Germany?
Registration is issued by the Luftfahrt-Bundesamt (LBA) under the D- prefix, after de-registration in the country of origin for an imported aircraft. Check the validity of the certificate of airworthiness, the ARC and the logbooks before purchase. Transferring registration between EASA states follows the standard procedure of de-registration followed by re-registration.
Does German VAT apply to used aircraft?
A sale between private individuals is generally not subject to German VAT (Umsatzsteuer). However, a sale made by a professional may be subject to it. It is recommended to have your tax situation confirmed before the acquisition, particularly for an imported aircraft.
How do you transfer an aircraft registered in Germany to another country?
De-registration is requested from the LBA, then the receiving country issues a new registration and an Airworthiness Review Certificate. EASA's common rules govern transfers between member states and make it simpler than outside Europe.
