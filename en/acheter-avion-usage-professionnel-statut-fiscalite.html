Buying an aircraft for professional use: status and tax

Company, leasing, professional co-ownership: which legal structure to choose to acquire an aircraft for professional use, and the tax implications depending on the activity carried out.

Why the acquisition status changes everything

An aircraft bought for professional use (aerial work, flying school, charter, business transport) is not acquired under the same conditions as a leisure aircraft. The choice of legal status — direct purchase through a company, leasing, finance leasing or professional co-ownership — determines VAT recovery, accounting depreciation, liability in the event of a claim, and future resale. This is a decision to make before searching for the aircraft, not after signing a preliminary agreement.

On Aircraft2Sell, many listings for turboprops, jets and helicopters are specifically aimed at this professional audience: operators, schools, aerial work companies. Both the seller and the buyer therefore have every interest in clarifying this status from the very first exchanges.

Key point — The buyer's legal status (private individual, company, leasing structure) should be settled before price negotiation: it determines the applicable VAT and the clauses of the sale contract.

Buying through an operating company

The most direct structure is to have the aircraft acquired by the company that will operate it (aerial work company, flying school, charter operator). Several points require attention:

  • VAT on the purchase is in principle recoverable if the aircraft is used for a taxable economic activity — see our dedicated guide on VAT and tax on buying an aircraft in Europe for the detail of the intra-EU rules.
  • Accounting depreciation of the aircraft is generally spread over several years according to local rules, with a direct impact on the company's taxable result.
  • The company must hold, or arrange to hold, the necessary operating approvals (air operator's certificate, school approval) before commercial entry into service — a point often underestimated when buying.

Leasing and professional finance leasing

For high-value aircraft (jets, turboprops), finance leasing (leasing) remains a common option to preserve the operator's cash flow:

  • The leasing company remains the legal owner for the duration of the contract; the operator has a right of use in exchange for rent payments, with a purchase option at the end of the period.
  • Rent payments are generally deductible from the operator's taxable result, unlike standard depreciation — a trade-off to discuss with your accountant depending on the balance sheet structure.
  • The leasing contract must specify who bears maintenance, insurance and end-of-contract reinstatement costs — clauses that vary significantly from one lessor to another.

For a detailed comparison of financing structures (standard bank loan, finance leasing, co-ownership), see our guide on financing a business jet.

Professional co-ownership and partnership

When no single operator justifies full-time use of an expensive aircraft, a group of operators or a professional co-ownership (several co-owning companies sharing the same aircraft) allows the investment to be pooled:

  • A dedicated co-ownership agreement or shareholders' pact must precisely set out the rules on use, cost-sharing and the exit of a co-owner.
  • Operational management (scheduling, maintenance, piloting) is most often delegated to a third-party manager (aircraft management), whose fees add to the fixed costs.
  • Reselling a share is generally more complex than reselling an aircraft owned outright: plan for pre-emption clauses between co-owners from the outset.

The case of mixed private/professional use

Many business owners use their company's aircraft both for professional travel and personal trips. This common situation is also one of the most closely scrutinised by the tax authorities:

Good to know — Undocumented mixed use exposes the company to tax reclassification (benefit in kind, challenge to VAT or expense deductions). A detailed flight logbook, clearly distinguishing professional flights from private flights, is the best protection in the event of a tax audit.

Common practice is to establish an annual professional/private use ratio, reviewed each financial year, and where applicable to invoice the business owner for the private-use portion at market price — a compliance step that must be anticipated from the time of purchase, not corrected afterwards.

Tax implications depending on the activity

The precise rules vary by country and type of activity carried out; they must be confirmed with a tax adviser specialised in aviation before any acquisition. Some general benchmarks observed on the European market:

ActivityMain tax point to watch
Flying schoolVAT regime on training services, often specific depending on the country (partial exemptions possible).
Aerial work / charterVAT recovery conditional on actual, documented use for a taxable activity.
Business transport (business owner)Risk of reclassification as a benefit in kind if private use is not isolated and valued.
Resale after professional useThe VAT status of the aircraft on resale depends on its acquisition history — to be documented for the next buyer.

These points remain general benchmarks: each case must be validated with an accountant or tax specialist familiar with the aviation sector, the amounts involved amply justifying this advice.

Documents to prepare before buying

To secure a professional-use acquisition, a file should be put together even before signing:

  • Articles of association and operating approvals of the acquiring company (air operator's certificate, school approval, aerial work authorisation depending on the activity).
  • Detailed financing plan (direct purchase, leasing or co-ownership) validated by the structure's accountant.
  • Projected annual utilisation of the aircraft, useful both for financing and to justify the professional allocation to the tax authorities.
  • Tax history and VAT status of the aircraft in question, to be systematically requested from the seller before final negotiation.
Tip — Before contacting a seller on Aircraft2Sell for a professional purchase, prepare this file in advance: it speeds up the negotiation and avoids unpleasant tax surprises after signing.
