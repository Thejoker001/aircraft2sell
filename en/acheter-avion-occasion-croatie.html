The Croatian used aircraft market

Croatia has a solid aeronautical tradition, supported by a network of flying clubs active along the entire Adriatic coast and inland, federated in particular around the Hrvatski aeroklub savez (Croatian federation of flying clubs). General aviation, gliding and air tourism are particularly dynamic there, with active airfields around Zagreb (Lučko), Split, Osijek, Zadar and Lošinj, the latter benefiting from strong general aviation traffic linked to tourism.

The used aircraft market remains modest in size compared with Germany, France or Italy, but it benefits from a pivotal geographic position between Central Europe and the Mediterranean, which facilitates the circulation of aircraft with neighbouring markets (Slovenia, Italy, Austria, Hungary). Many Croatian buyers naturally extend their search to these neighbouring markets to access a wider choice of aircraft, with maintenance networks and parts supply chains well connected to the rest of Central Europe and the Adriatic.

A point to bear in mind throughout this guide: Croatia has been a member of the European Union since 2013 and joined the eurozone on 1 January 2023, replacing the kuna. Listings are now therefore denominated in euros, which greatly simplifies comparing prices with the rest of Europe compared with the situation before 2023.

Average prices by aircraft type

Prices in Croatia for an equivalent aircraft in terms of condition, airframe hours and remaining engine potential generally remain comparable to those seen in Southern and Central Europe, sometimes slightly lower on entry-level aircraft. This does not remove the need to check the quality and traceability of work carried out, whatever the aircraft's country of origin.

Registration with the CCAA

Any aircraft based and operated in Croatia must be registered with the Hrvatska agencija za civilno zrakoplovstvo (CCAA, the Croatian Civil Aviation Agency), under the 9A- prefix. For an aircraft already registered in Croatia, a private purchase results in an update of the holder of the certificate of registration with this agency. For an aircraft coming from abroad, prior deregistration from the register of origin is generally required before any new Croatian registration.

  • Valid, up-to-date certificate of airworthiness, recognised by the CCAA.
  • Previous certificate of registration and proof of deregistration if the aircraft comes from another country.
  • Certificate of liability insurance covering operation in Croatia.

VAT and taxation since adopting the euro

As Croatia is an EU member state, buying an aircraft from another member country generally falls under the intra-EU acquisition regime rather than an import, which simplifies customs procedures compared with a purchase from a country outside the EU. This does not, however, remove the need to clarify the VAT treatment applicable to the transaction, which depends on the seller's status (private individual or VAT-registered professional) and the aircraft's intended use.

Croatia's standard VAT rate is generally quoted at around 25% — one of the highest in the European Union — but this general figure should always be confirmed for the specific case of an aircraft, as exemption or calculation rules can vary depending on whether the transaction is between private individuals, via a professional, or involves an aircraft already cleared through customs and taxed within the Union. It is essential to have the exact tax treatment confirmed by the Croatian tax administration (Porezna uprava) before signing, rather than relying on unverified rates for this particular case.

Worth remembering — Always ask for a written assessment of the applicable VAT treatment from a professional before committing, particularly if the aircraft changes country as part of the transaction.

Pre-purchase inspection and documents

As with any used aircraft purchase, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by a certified workshop independent of the seller remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises. In Croatia, the Adriatic climate (high salt humidity along the coast, hot summers) warrants particular attention to corrosion linked to proximity to the sea, especially for aircraft based at coastal airfields (Split, Zadar, Dubrovnik, Pula), as well as the condition of anti-corrosion protection and the electrical system.

Documents to gather and check systematically:

  • Complete aircraft logbook, with no gaps in the history.
  • Report of the last annual inspection (Part-M / Part-ML) and any major engine overhauls.
  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and history of airworthiness directives (AD) applied.
  • No disputes or liens on the aircraft, to be checked with the CCAA.
  • Storage conditions (closed hangar or outdoors) and any signs of corrosion linked to sea air, particularly for an aircraft based on the coast.

Pitfalls to avoid

An aircraft coming from a club or an associative co-ownership arrangement should be subject to careful verification of ownership status before any commitment: transfer procedures can differ significantly from a private sale in full ownership. Also be wary of incomplete logbooks, particularly for aircraft that have moved between several Central European and Adriatic countries, and of prolonged outdoor storage on the coast without suitable anti-corrosion protection.

Worth remembering — A price noticeably below market should always raise questions: reduced engine potential, corrosion linked to sea exposure, work needed on the airframe, or incomplete documentation are the most common causes.
