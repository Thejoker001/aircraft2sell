Used helicopters: a buying guide for individuals

Buying guide for individuals looking to buy a used helicopter: budget, categories, maintenance costs, training and administrative procedures.

The main categories of helicopters for individuals

Unlike light aircraft, the used helicopter market for private use is concentrated on a limited number of categories, mainly single-engine turbine or piston models. The models most sought after by individuals in Europe are light single-turbine helicopters such as the Robinson R22/R44/R66, as well as a few older piston models for an entry-level budget.

The choice depends above all on the intended use: occasional leisure flying, ongoing training, or regular trips. A light single-turbine helicopter remains the most common entry point for private owners, with good parts availability and an established maintenance network in Europe.

What budget to plan for

The price of a used helicopter varies significantly depending on age, airframe and engine hours, and onboard equipment (avionics, landing gear, comfort options). As a rough guide, and broadly consistent with other estimates already published for the light aircraft market:

CategoryUsed price rangeBuyer profile
Light single-engine piston, olderAround 50,000 to 150,000 €First purchase, controlled budget
Recent light single-turbineAround 200,000 to 600,000 €Regular use, easier resale
High-end single-turbine / light twin-engineSeveral hundred thousand to over a million eurosProfessional or intensive use

These figures remain rough orders of magnitude: each aircraft must be assessed individually based on its remaining engine potential, maintenance history and airworthiness status.

Maintenance and running costs

Helicopters differ from light aircraft in generally higher operating costs, due to the mechanical complexity of the rotor and more frequent maintenance intervals.

ItemOrder of magnitude
Liability insurance (+ hull)1,500 to 6,000 € / year depending on the model
Annual inspection / periodic check1,000 to 4,000 €
Fuel (avgas or Jet A1 depending on engine type)Variable depending on the model's hourly consumption
Dedicated hangar or parking area150 to 500 € / month
Major engine / rotor blade overhaul (depending on potential)High amounts, to be budgeted over the ownership period
Good to know — The hourly operating cost of a helicopter is structurally higher than that of a comparable light aircraft, notably due to rotor monitoring and potential limits on certain dynamic parts. Factor this into your overall budget before buying, not just the purchase price.

Training and licence required

Flying a helicopter requires a specific licence (PPL(H) in Europe), separate from the fixed-wing licence: flight hours and ratings are not interchangeable between the two aircraft categories. If you already hold a fixed-wing licence, dedicated helicopter training remains essential.

  • Specific helicopter theoretical and practical training, generally delivered by an approved school.
  • A possible type rating if the intended model requires it (above a certain weight or engine type).
  • Maintaining proficiency through a regular volume of flight hours, often recommended by insurers.

Registration and administrative procedures

As with an aircraft, the helicopter must be registered with the civil aviation authority of the chosen country, with a valid certificate of airworthiness. The ownership change process follows a similar logic to that of light aircraft: transfer of ownership, update of the registration certificate, and verification of maintenance history with the relevant authority.

A complete and up-to-date maintenance file (engine, airframe and, where applicable, rotor logbooks) is an even more important point of attention than for an aircraft, given the critical role of certain dynamic parts in flight safety.

What to check before buying

Before finalising a purchase, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by a mechanic or maintenance centre specifically approved for helicopters (not only for aircraft) remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises.

  • Complete maintenance history and compliance with mandatory intervals (airframe, engine, rotor).
  • Remaining potential before the next major overhauls, often more costly than on a light aircraft.
  • Compliance with applicable airworthiness directives (AD) for the model.
  • Verification of the avionics and optional equipment advertised in the listing.
