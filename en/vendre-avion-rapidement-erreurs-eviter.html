Selling Your Aircraft Quickly: Mistakes to Avoid

A mispriced listing, insufficient photos, an incomplete ad: the most common mistakes that slow down the sale of an aircraft, and how to avoid them.

Mistake #1: misjudging the starting price

This is the most common mistake, and the most costly in terms of time. A price set too high discourages serious buyers from the very first budget-range search — the listing stays invisible to those who could actually buy, and attracts only out-of-budget enquiries. Conversely, too low a price gives the impression of a hidden problem and needlessly loses value. Good practice is to compare several listings of similar aircraft (same model, similar year, comparable flight hours) and adjust according to the actual condition: avionics, hours remaining before major overhaul, maintenance history. A price that is slightly negotiable, stated transparently, attracts more enquiries than a fixed price presented as non-negotiable.

Mistake #2: neglecting photos and presentation

A buyer searching for an aircraft filters on photos first, before reading the description. A listing with 3 blurry photos taken on a phone, in a dark hangar, is systematically outmatched by listings with a complete, well-lit photo set. You should include at minimum: several exterior angles (front, rear, side profile), the cabin and cockpit interior, the instrument panel, the engine with the cowling open if possible, and specific selling points (recent avionics, refurbished interior). Photos taken outdoors, in clear weather, with a clean aircraft, showcase the listing far better than archive photos several years old.

Tip — A listing with 8 to 12 quality photos generally receives far more enquiries than a listing with 2 or 3 photos, for an equivalent aircraft and price. It's often the simplest lever to improve before reposting a stagnant listing.

Mistake #3: an incomplete or vague listing

Aircraft buyers are demanding and well-informed: they compare listings on precise criteria. Omitting total flight hours, engine type, the date of the last overhaul, the number of previous owners or the country of registration forces the potential buyer to write in and ask these basic questions — and many will simply move on to the next listing rather than wait for a reply. The more a listing answers by itself the questions a serious buyer has, the more qualified enquiries it generates and the fewer low-value messages it attracts.

  • Total flight hours and hours remaining before engine overhaul — the number-one piece of data every serious buyer looks for.
  • Maintenance history — up-to-date logbooks, tracked by an approved workshop, manufacturer's programme where applicable.
  • Avionics and equipment — GPS, ADS-B transponder, autopilot, interior fit-out.
  • Registration and location — country, home airport, VAT status where applicable.

Mistake #4: slow responsiveness to buyers

The used aircraft market remains a niche market where serious buyers often track several listings in parallel. A seller who takes several days to reply frequently loses the enquiry to a more responsive seller offering an equivalent aircraft. Replying quickly, even just to arrange a phone call later, demonstrates the seller's seriousness and keeps the buyer's interest alive during their comparison phase.

Mistake #5: not preparing documents in advance

Waiting until a buyer has decided to gather logbooks, the certificate of airworthiness, maintenance invoices and ownership history wastes precious time at the critical moment — when the buyer wants to move quickly. Preparing this file in advance, even before publishing the listing, means you can respond instantly to a pre-purchase inspection request and reassures the buyer about the transparency of the transaction.

Tip — A complete sales file prepared in advance (logbooks, invoices, certificates) is often seen by the buyer as a sign of the seller's seriousness, and helps secure a more favourable price negotiation.

Where to post your listing to sell faster

The visibility of the listing matters as much as its quality. Posting only on a general classifieds site dilutes visibility among listings unrelated to aviation. A specialised site, visited by an already-qualified audience (pilots, brokers, operators), generates more relevant enquiries and reduces the number of off-topic messages.
