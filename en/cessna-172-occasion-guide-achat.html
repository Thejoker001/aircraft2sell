The Cessna 172 in brief

The Cessna 172 Skyhawk is one of the most produced and most widely flown light aircraft in the world. A high-wing, fixed-gear single-engine aircraft seating four, it appeals to flight schools as much as to private owners looking for a versatile machine for leisure flying or short trips. Its popularity rests on easy handling, wide spare-parts availability, and a dense network of qualified mechanics across Europe and beyond.

The used market is especially broad: decades of production have produced many variants, with significant price differences depending on age, installed avionics, and the overall condition of the airframe and engine.

Budget by year and variant

The price of a used Cessna 172 depends above all on the year it was built, airframe and engine hours, and the level of avionics fitted. The ranges below are indicative, consistent with other light aviation segments already covered in our guides.

These ranges remain approximate: an aircraft with an engine close to its overhaul limit will sell for noticeably less than a recently overhauled example, even with the same year of manufacture. Always compare several similar listings before settling on a price.

The main variants to know

Over the decades, the Cessna 172 has gone through several notable changes that directly affect its value and its intended use:

  • Early generations, mechanically simpler, suit flight-school or leisure use on an entry-level budget.
  • Intermediate generations often offer the best balance between equipment, reliability and purchase price.
  • The most recent examples, fitted with digital avionics, command a higher price but are easier to maintain avionics-wise.

Typical uses: flight school, leisure, short hops

The Cessna 172 remains a benchmark for flight training, thanks to its stability and forgiving handling. It's also widely used for leisure flying, whether through a club or private ownership, and is well suited to short hops between regional airfields with its four seats. Its versatility makes it a frequent choice for a first light aircraft purchase, before moving on to higher-performance aircraft if needed.

Checklist before buying

Before committing, several checks are essential, ideally carried out with the help of an approved mechanic during a pre-purchase inspection:

  • Complete logbook history: airframe hours, engine hours since the last overhaul, any recorded incidents.
  • Airframe condition: corrosion, signs of impact, quality of any previous repairs.
  • Airworthiness status and compliance with applicable airworthiness directives (ADs).
  • Consistency of the installed avionics with current European regulatory requirements (transponder, traffic-monitoring systems).
Good to know — An attractive price paired with an engine close to its overhaul limit can translate into a significant expense in the short term. Always factor this into your negotiation.

As with any single-engine light aircraft, the purchase price is only part of the overall budget. You need to plan for the fixed and variable costs of ownership:

Alternatives to consider

Other four-seat single-engine aircraft occupy the same market segment and are worth comparing before deciding, particularly depending on spare-parts availability in your region, the local maintenance network, and your specific needs (intensive flight-school use, occasional leisure flying, or regular trips). The final choice will also depend on your overall budget once operating costs are factored in.
