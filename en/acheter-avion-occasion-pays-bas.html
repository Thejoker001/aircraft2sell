Buying a Used Aircraft in the Netherlands: Complete 2026 Guide

Dutch market, prices, registration with the ILT, VAT and pre-purchase inspection: everything you need to know to buy a used light aircraft in the Netherlands with confidence.

The Dutch used aircraft market

The Netherlands has a dense general aviation fabric despite a particularly constrained airspace (heavy commercial traffic density around Schiphol, numerous military zones). Airfields such as Lelystad Airport, Teuge, Hilversum and Seppe (Breda International) concentrate most of the recreational, training and maintenance activity, and are classic touchpoints for buying or selling a light aircraft. The used market there remains modest in size compared with Germany or France, but benefits from close proximity to the Belgian, German and British markets.

Given the country's limited size and the pressure on its airspace, many Dutch buyers naturally widen their search to Belgian, German and British listings, as these markets are well interconnected in terms of spare parts, approved maintenance workshops and cross-border flying clubs. The most common aircraft on the used market remain the general aviation classics: touring single-engines, light sport/ULM aircraft, gliders and motor gliders, plus a fleet of light helicopters used in particular for training and light transport.

Average prices by aircraft type

Prices in the Netherlands broadly align with those seen elsewhere in Western Europe for a comparable aircraft in terms of condition, airframe hours and remaining engine potential. The decisive factor remains the consistency between the asking price and the maintenance history, more than the seller's nationality.

Tip — Before settling on the asking price, always ask for the engine history (hours remaining before TBO) and the airframe condition. These factors, far more than the year of manufacture, determine an aircraft's real value.

Registration with the ILT

Any aircraft based and operated in the Netherlands must be registered with the Inspectie Leefomgeving en Transport (ILT), the Dutch authority responsible among other things for the civil aircraft register, under the PH- prefix. For a private purchase of an aircraft already registered in the Netherlands, the transfer consists of updating the holder of the registration certificate with the ILT. For an aircraft coming from another EU country, prior deregistration from the register of origin is required before the new registration, as with any intra-European transfer.

  • Valid, up-to-date certificate of airworthiness, issued or recognised by the ILT.
  • Previous registration certificate and proof of deregistration if the aircraft comes from abroad.
  • Certificate of liability insurance covering operation in the Netherlands.

VAT and taxation

As elsewhere in the European Union, a private sale involving an aircraft already in free circulation within the EU does not in principle trigger new VAT (BTW), since the tax was already paid when the aircraft first entered circulation in the Union. The situation differs for a purchase from a professional (dealer, VAT-registered broker) or for an aircraft imported from outside the EU, which may trigger import VAT. As the precise rules on registration and taxation change regularly, it is advisable to check directly with the ILT and the Dutch tax authority (Belastingdienst), and to have the aircraft's exact customs and tax status confirmed by a professional before signing, rather than relying on unconfirmed rates or procedures.

Pre-purchase inspection and documents

Before signing, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by an approved workshop independent of the seller remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises. It covers the condition of the airframe, the avionics, the landing gear and, above all, the engine history.

Documents to gather and check systematically:

  • Complete aircraft logbook, with no gaps in the history.
  • Report of the latest annual inspection (Part-M / Part-ML) and any major engine overhauls.
  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and history of applied airworthiness directives (ADs).
  • No dispute or lien on the aircraft, to be checked with the ILT.

Pitfalls to avoid

Pressure on Dutch airspace (military zones, proximity to Schiphol) can encourage some sellers to heavily price up an aircraft based at a "convenient" airfield. Take time to compare with similar listings in Belgium, Germany or the UK before negotiating, and never skip the pre-purchase inspection, even for an aircraft described as "perfectly maintained". Also be wary of incomplete logbooks or aircraft that have changed registration country several times without consistent documentation: these are signals that warrant extra caution, or even a second expert opinion.

Worth remembering — A price noticeably below market should always be questioned: reduced engine potential, work needed on the airframe, or incomplete documentation are the most common causes.
