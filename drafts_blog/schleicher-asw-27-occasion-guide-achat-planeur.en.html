The Schleicher ASW 27: a demanding competition single-seater

The Schleicher ASW 27 is a high-performance single-seat glider designed by Alexander Schleicher, a historic German manufacturer and a long-standing reference in European gliding. Positioned at the top end of competition gliders, the ASW 27 was designed to compete at the highest championship level, thanks to a high-glide-ratio wing and a particularly refined composite structure.

On the used market, the ASW 27 benefits from a significant installed fleet across Europe, particularly in Germany, France and the Alpine countries, which practise gliding competition intensively. This spread guarantees a steady supply of used aircraft and good spare-parts availability through specialist workshops familiar with the type, an important criterion for an owner planning to keep the aircraft long term.

Versions and developments of the model

Over its production run, the ASW 27 went through a notable development with the ASW 27B version, which introduced aerodynamic and structural improvements over the original model, particularly at wing level. Some aircraft have also been subject to later modifications through manufacturer bulletins or airworthiness directives, which should be checked precisely in the technical file of the aircraft you are considering.

Before targeting a specific aircraft, it is worth asking the seller for the exact serial number, the model version and the list of technical bulletins applied, to make sure the aircraft matches the expected configuration and that no applicable airworthiness directive has been left unaddressed.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used ASW 27 depends mainly on the age of the aircraft, its airframe hours, the condition of its composite skin, its equipment (competition instruments, oxygen, trailer) and the documented quality of its maintenance. The oldest aircraft, with minimal equipment, trade at a noticeably more accessible entry level than well-equipped examples regularly maintained at an approved workshop, which represent a far larger investment given the model's high-end positioning.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: an independent mechanic's or approved workshop's pre-purchase inspection, registration and transfer fees, insurance, and hangar or tie-down space depending on how you plan to operate the aircraft.

What to check before buying

A pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For an ASW 27, a few points deserve particular attention, given its positioning as a demanding competition glider.

  • Condition of the composite structure: look for any impacts, delamination or previous repairs on the leading edges and fuselage, sometimes hard to detect without a specialist inspection.
  • Total airframe hours and history of periodic inspections (ARC), with a complete, transparent logbook.
  • Compliance with manufacturer bulletins and airworthiness directives applicable to the model and serial number concerned.
  • Condition of flight controls, cables, bearings, airbrakes and canopy, as well as the proper operation of the retractable gear.
  • Past storage conditions (hangar or trailer): a glider regularly kept under cover keeps its skin in noticeably better condition.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report, ideally carried out by an approved workshop on the type, remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

Flying profile and required experience level

The ASW 27 is primarily intended for a pilot already confirmed on high-performance single-seaters, not for a pilot new to private ownership. Its higher stall speed compared with a classic club glider and its demanding behaviour at speed require solid prior experience, ideally gained on intermediate performance models before considering the ASW 27 as a first high-performance machine.

Its high glide ratio and demanding flight characteristics make it a glider particularly favoured by regional, national or international competition pilots, who look for a machine capable of holding its own against the newest models on the market.

Many owner-pilots choose the ASW 27 once significant experience has been gained on intermediate performance gliders, because of the good balance it offers between availability on the used market and top-level competition performance. This positioning partly explains the loyalty of a community of owners who readily share maintenance advice and experience on specialist forums.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of an ASW 27 remains comparable to that of other single-seat performance gliders in its class. The main expense items remain the periodic airworthiness inspection, insurance, storage (hangar or trailer) and, occasionally, composite structure repair or the replacement of wear parts.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved workshop familiar with the type and with composite materials helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records are a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the complete logbook and the latest periodic inspection reports before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The ASW 27 benefits from an active used market in Europe, supported by a significant installed fleet among competition pilots and good parts availability through specialist workshops. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and skin in good condition, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar aircraft remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two examples are almost always explained by airframe hours, the condition of the skin and the documented quality of maintenance.
