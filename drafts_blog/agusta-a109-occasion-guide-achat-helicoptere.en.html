The Agusta A109, an Italian benchmark in light twins

The Agusta A109 (now marketed under the Leonardo Helicopters brand, successor to Agusta and then AgustaWestland) is a light twin-engine helicopter built in Italy since the late 1970s. Recognisable by its sleek profile and retractable landing gear — a rare feature on a helicopter — the A109 has established itself as a benchmark for missions requiring speed, twin-engine redundancy and cabin comfort all at once.

On the used market, the A109 stands apart from light single-engine piston helicopters (such as the Robinson R44 or R66) by category: two turbines provide greater safety margin in the event of an engine failure and superior performance, at the cost of a notably higher acquisition and operating budget. It sits on a segment close to other professional light twins, popular with both private operators and institutional fleets.

Typical uses: VIP, EMS, surveillance and aerial work

A used A109's prior operational history varies considerably from one airframe to another, which has a direct impact on its overall condition and resale value. The main uses found on the European market are:

  • VIP and corporate transport: a comfortable cabin for a few passengers, often with a high-end interior fit-out and up-to-date avionics.
  • Air ambulance missions (EMS/HEMS): a dedicated configuration with medical equipment, used by public or private helicopter rescue operators.
  • Surveillance, police and institutional missions: aircraft fitted with specific systems, used by government or quasi-public fleets.
  • Aerial work and various utility missions, to a lesser extent, depending on the variant and the original operator.

The same model can therefore have had a very different operational life from one airframe to another: an aircraft used in private VIP transport does not have the same wear profile as one that has seen intensive use in medical missions or surveillance. A complete operational history is one of the first things to request before any viewing.

The main variants found on the used market

The A109 family has undergone several successive updates since launch, with notable differences in powerplant, avionics and cabin capacity. On the used market, you will mostly find older A109A/A109C-generation airframes alongside more recent versions such as the A109 Power and A109 Grand, fitted with upgraded avionics and more capable turbines.

Good to know — Before making an offer, identify precisely the variant and engine/avionics configuration of the aircraft in question: performance, maintenance costs and resale value can vary significantly between A109 generations.

Inspection points to watch

Beyond the standard checks that apply to any used helicopter (complete logbook, compliance with airworthiness directives, overall airframe condition), a few points deserve particular attention on an A109.

  • Precise operational history (VIP, EMS, surveillance, aerial work) and consistency with the declared flight hours and logbook.
  • Condition and operation of the retractable landing gear, a mechanical feature specific to the A109 that requires dedicated maintenance.
  • Remaining potential on both turbines before the next overhaul, and consistency with the engine logbooks and maintenance invoices.
  • Condition of the main rotor blades, rotor head and transmission components, which have limited service lives and whose replacement represents a significant expense.
  • Signs of corrosion, especially for aircraft that have operated in coastal, offshore or otherwise demanding environments.
  • History of any incidents (hard landing, transmission damage, engine FOD) recorded in the logbook or with the registration authority.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection by a workshop approved on the type is strongly recommended before any firm offer, particularly to check the condition of the transmission, the retractable gear and the complete file for both turbines — three costly items should their service limits be close.

Maintenance, component life and overhaul

Like any twin-engine turbine helicopter, the A109 requires a sustained maintenance schedule, with life-limited components (blades, rotor head, certain transmission parts) that must be replaced after a set number of hours or a calendar period defined by the manufacturer, regardless of their apparent condition.

Overhauling both turbines is the heaviest maintenance item to plan for: its cost and timing must be clearly documented in the aircraft's technical file before any purchase, since an airframe close to this deadline will logically trade at a discount reflecting the cost of the upcoming overhaul. Also check the availability of an approved maintenance workshop near the intended base of operations, an important criterion for a professional aircraft in frequent use.

Price range and operating budget

The A109 sits in a price bracket well above that of light single-engine helicopters: on the European used market, prices typically range from a few hundred thousand euros for the oldest, most heavily used examples, up to several million euros for recent, well-equipped versions with low hours remaining. This places it in a budget category comparable to small business jets and turboprops featured elsewhere on Aircraft2Sell (see our guide to average private jet prices), rather than light single-engine helicopters.

Before negotiating, compare several listings with comparable configurations (variant, hours, cabin equipment, prior use) to gauge the asking price against the real market, rather than relying on a single isolated reference.

Market and resale

The A109 benefits from an active resale market, supported by the aircraft's reputation in the VIP and medical segments as well as sustained demand from certain institutional and private operators. Resale is generally made easier by a complete, transparent maintenance file, a clearly documented operational history and life-limited components that have been recently replaced or still have a comfortable margin before their next deadline.

Tip — Always keep maintenance invoices and inspection reports: they are a strong argument at resale time, particularly for the turbines and life-limited components.
