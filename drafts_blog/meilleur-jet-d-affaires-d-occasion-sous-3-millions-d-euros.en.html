Best used business jet under 3 million euros

Between 1 and 3 million euros, the used market opens the door to well-equipped light and midsize jets: the Citation CJ and Excel, Learjet 40/45, Hawker 800. Here is how to choose based on your mission and real operating budget.

What 3 million euros actually buys you

Under the one-million-euro mark, the used market is mostly limited to older very light jets or ones with heavily consumed engine potential. Between 1 and 3 million euros, the picture changes: buyers gain access to well-equipped recent light jets and, more importantly, to roomier midsize jets such as certain Hawker 800s or Learjet 45s, provided they accept an older airframe and more advanced engine time than they would find on the new-aircraft market.

This budget still forces a trade-off: it almost always means balancing cabin size, aircraft age and remaining engine hours before the next major overhaul. Three aircraft families dominate this segment on the European used market: the Citation CJ/Excel, the Learjet 40/45 and the Hawker 800.

Cessna Citation CJ and Citation Excel: the light-to-midsize benchmark

The Citation CJ family (CJ1, CJ2, CJ3) sits at the top of the light-jet segment, with a reputation for reliability and a particularly dense maintenance network in Europe, which makes both resale and routine upkeep easier. The larger Citation Excel sits at the border between light and midsize, offering a noticeably roomier cabin while keeping operating costs reasonable for its class.

On the used market, a decade-old CJ2 or CJ3 typically trades in the lower-to-middle part of the 3-million-euro budget, while a well-equipped Excel can absorb nearly all of it depending on year and hours. Upgraded avionics (Garmin, modern integrations) remain a decisive value factor, both when buying and when reselling.

Learjet 40 and 45: speed and brand prestige

The Learjet 45, and its shortened variant the Learjet 40, carry forward the brand's speed-focused DNA with its distinctive oval cabin cross-section and high cruise performance for the class. They remain popular for trips where flight time matters more than cabin space, particularly on regular medium-range missions.

Their cabin, narrower than an Excel's or a Hawker's of comparable price, positions them more as performance-oriented business jets than as comfort tools for long flights with several passengers. The European used market offers decent availability within the 3-million-euro range, especially among the earlier Learjet 45 model years.

Hawker 800: the roomiest midsize cabin in the segment

The Hawker 800 (and its 800XP evolution) remains one of the most accessible ways to get a genuine midsize cabin within this budget: generous interior volume, comfortable headroom and good storage capacity — strengths that matter for flights with several passengers or longer trips.

That budget accessibility comes with a trade-off: examples available under 3 million euros tend to be older aircraft, which calls for particular attention to remaining engine potential (SMOH) and major-overhaul history before treating the purchase price as the only decision criterion.

Beyond this table, two aircraft of the same model can carry very different operating budgets depending on installed avionics, remaining hours before the next engine overhaul, and the documented quality of the logbook.

It is also worth looking beyond the purchase price to compare these three families on a fair basis. Fixed costs (hangarage, insurance, crew where applicable) and variable costs (fuel, scheduled maintenance, airport fees) vary noticeably from one model to another and, over several years of operation, can represent a bigger gap than the initial purchase-price difference between two aircraft in this segment.

Financing and insurance in this price segment

Between 1 and 3 million euros, most buyers use partial financing rather than paying the full amount in cash, whether through a finance lease, an aviation-specific bank loan, or private financing for the simplest arrangements. The aircraft's age directly affects the terms on offer: an older Hawker 800 may be offered a shorter financing term or require a larger down payment than a newer Citation CJ, since lenders factor in the accelerated depreciation risk tied to remaining engine potential.

Insurance is another cost to plan for before signing a purchase offer. Premiums depend on the aircraft's insured value, the experience of the pilot(s), and sometimes the intended type of operation (strictly private use versus partial charter availability). It is worth requesting a preliminary insurance quote from several specialist brokers before committing, since some older or less common models in Europe can carry noticeably higher premiums.

Which jet for which mission

Choosing between these three families depends primarily on the intended mission rather than on budget alone.

  • Frequent trips with a small party, priority on controlled operating cost: Citation CJ.
  • Trips where speed matters more than cabin comfort: Learjet 40/45.
  • Flights with several passengers or longer trips where cabin comfort becomes the priority: Hawker 800 or Citation Excel.
  • Planning a mid-term resale: favour an aircraft with a dense maintenance network and modern avionics.

Points to watch when buying used in this segment

In this price bracket, the price gap between two examples of the same model is almost always explained by engine condition and avionics, far more than by simple cabin layout differences. Before making any offer, always request detailed engine hours and SMOH for each engine, along with the complete history recorded in the logbook.

Word of caution — An aircraft close to a major engine overhaul may look attractive on purchase price alone, but the cost of that overhaul must always be factored into the overall budget before comparing two offers.

As with any used aircraft, an independent pre-purchase inspection and verification of the certificate of airworthiness (CofA) remain essential before signing, whichever model you choose.
