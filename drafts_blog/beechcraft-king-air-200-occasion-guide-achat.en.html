Why the King Air 200 remains a twin-turboprop benchmark

The Beechcraft King Air 200 is one of the most widely used twin-engine turboprops in the world, flown for business transport as well as for specialised missions such as air ambulance, surveillance and light freight. Its well-proven ruggedness, pressurised cabin and all-round capability make it a versatile aircraft that has spanned several decades of production while remaining actively flown and traded on the used market.

On the secondary market, the King Air 200 comes in several model years and sub-variants, with successive developments in avionics, engines and cabin configuration. This long production run explains a wide price range, which makes it essential to target your budget and intended use carefully before starting a search.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used King Air 200 depends above all on the age of the aircraft, total airframe hours, the status of both engines (remaining potential before overhaul), the level of avionics fitted, and the chosen cabin configuration. The oldest examples, with reduced potential on both powerplants, trade at a noticeably more accessible entry level than well-equipped recent versions, which represent a far larger investment.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: an independent mechanic's pre-purchase inspection by someone familiar with the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance suited to a twin-engine turboprop, and possibly an avionics or cabin interior refresh if the aircraft needs one.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For a King Air 200, a few points deserve particular attention given its twin-engine, pressurised complexity.

  • Condition of the airframe and pressurised structure: look for signs of corrosion or fatigue at the most heavily loaded points.
  • Complete history of both engines: hours elapsed since the last overhaul, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts on each powerplant.
  • Condition of the propellers and compliance with their respective overhaul programs.
  • Operation of the cabin pressurisation system and the history of interventions on it.
  • Condition and updates of the installed avionics, as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements in the intended airspace.
  • Complete logbooks and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report covering both engines and the airframe remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

Engines, propellers and overhaul programs

Managing engine potential is a central concern on a twin-engine turboprop like the King Air 200: each engine follows its own overhaul schedule, and overhaul costs rank among the most significant expenses over the aircraft's ownership period. A buyer must carefully compare the remaining potential of both engines, since a large gap between the two powerplants can mean one of them needs attention in the short term.

The propellers also follow an overhaul program separate from the engines. Before buying, it's worth asking the seller for details of the last interventions on both the engines and propellers, along with upcoming deadlines, so you can factor these costs into your financing plan if one of the deadlines is approaching.

Avionics, cabin and compliance upgrades

Many King Air 200s have been progressively modernised over the decades with newer avionics suites replacing the original equipment. It's important to check precisely which avionics standard equips the aircraft you're considering, as well as compliance with regulatory requirements applicable in the intended area of operation, before treating the purchase as finalised.

Cabin configuration (seat count, executive versus utility layout, comfort equipment) also varies widely from one aircraft to another and should be assessed against the intended use, whether business transport, specialised missions, or mixed use.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a King Air 200 is noticeably higher than that of a single-engine piston aircraft, owing to its two turboprop engines, pressurised airframe and greater technical complexity. The main expense items remain the mandatory annual inspection, insurance, parking, fuel and, in the longer term, overhauling both engines once their hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records, engine by engine, remain a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the latest maintenance invoices for both engines before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously each powerplant has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The King Air 200 benefits from an active, international used market, supported by a very large installed fleet and good parts availability. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history on both engines and up-to-date avionics, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar aircraft remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same model year are almost always explained by the remaining engine potential on both powerplants and the quality of the avionics and cabin fit.
