The Airbus H135, a compact and versatile twin

The Airbus H135 (formerly the Eurocopter EC135) is a light twin-engine helicopter widely used across Europe, in particular in helicopter emergency medical service (EMS/HEMS) fleets. Its design combines two turbines for power redundancy, a rigid, hingeless main rotor and a Fenestron (shrouded tail rotor) that makes it particularly quiet and discreet in flight — a real advantage for operations over urban areas.

On the used market, the H135 is set apart from single-engine helicopters such as the H125 by its safety category: having two engines allows operations over populated areas and medical missions that require engine redundancy. This versatility makes it a sought-after aircraft for institutional operators and aerial-work companies, and, to a lesser extent, for private owners in a VIP configuration.

Typical missions: EMS, VIP, offshore and law enforcement

The operational history of a used H135 varies considerably depending on its previous career, which has a direct impact on its overall condition and resale value. The most common profiles found on the European market are:

  • Helicopter emergency medical service (HEMS): intensive use, frequent start/stop cycles, onboard medical equipment, often with very rigorous maintenance tracking at institutional operators.
  • VIP and corporate transport: cabin fitted with sound insulation and high-end finishes, generally a less intensive usage profile than EMS.
  • Police, surveillance and public-safety missions: specific equipment (camera, searchlight, hoist depending on the version), usage varies by unit.
  • Offshore and industry-support work, in certain fleets, with stricter maintenance requirements linked to the marine environment.

An aircraft that served in a HEMS fleet with rigorous institutional maintenance can have a very complete technical file despite a high number of hours, while a low-hour VIP aircraft is not automatically in better condition if its maintenance follow-up was less strict. A complete operating history therefore remains the first thing to request before any viewing.

Main versions and evolutions of the type

The type has gone through several evolutions since its launch as the Eurocopter EC135, with successive improvements in powerplant, avionics and maximum take-off weight across the various series. On the used market, it is essential to precisely identify the version and standard of the aircraft being viewed, since parts, performance and resale value differ significantly from one series to another.

Inspection checkpoints

Beyond the standard checks that apply to any used helicopter (complete logbook, compliance with airworthiness directives, overall airframe condition), a few points deserve particular attention on an H135.

  • Precise usage history (EMS, VIP, offshore, police) and consistency with declared flight hours and the logbook.
  • Condition and remaining life of the main rotor blades, rigid rotor head and transmission components — life-limited parts whose replacement represents a significant cost item.
  • Condition of the Fenestron and its controls, a point specific to the H135/EC135 family compared with conventional tail-rotor helicopters.
  • Remaining time before the next overhaul of each of the two turbines and consistency with the engine logbooks and maintenance invoices.
  • Signs of corrosion or accelerated wear, particularly on aircraft that have operated in a marine environment (offshore) or with frequent start cycles (EMS).
  • History of any incidents (hard landing, transmission damage, engine FOD) recorded in the logbook or with the registration authority.
  • Compliance of onboard equipment (medical, VIP, camera) with the declared configuration and with the buyer's intended use.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection carried out by an Airbus Helicopters-approved workshop is strongly recommended before any firm offer, particularly to check the condition of the transmission, the rotor head and the complete file for both turbines — three costly items if potential has been exceeded.

Maintenance, Fenestron and engine overhaul

Like any turbine helicopter, the H135 requires a sustained periodic maintenance rhythm, with life-limited components (blades, rotor head, certain transmission parts) that must be replaced after a number of hours or a calendar duration set by the manufacturer, regardless of their apparent condition. Having two turbines effectively doubles this tracking item, with two overhaul schedules to monitor instead of one.

The engine overhaul is the heaviest maintenance item to plan for: its cost and due date for each of the two engines must be clearly documented in the aircraft's technical file before any purchase, since an aircraft approaching this milestone is logically negotiated with a discount reflecting the cost of the upcoming overhaul. Also check the availability of an approved maintenance workshop near the planned operating base, an important criterion for a professional aircraft in frequent use.

Tip — Ask for the separate maintenance file of each turbine: it is not unusual for the two engines on the same aircraft to show very different remaining potential, which should be reflected in the price negotiation.

Price range and operating budget

The H135 sits in a price bracket well above light single-engine helicopters: on the European used market, prices generally range from a few hundred thousand euros for the oldest, most used examples to several million euros for recent, low-hour models. This order of magnitude puts the aircraft in a budget category comparable to turboprops and small business jets featured elsewhere on Aircraft2Sell (see our guide on the average price of a private jet), rather than light single-engine helicopters.

Before any negotiation, compare several listings with comparable configurations (year, hours, cabin equipment, previous use) to position the asking price against the real market, rather than relying on a single isolated reference.

Market and resale

The H135 benefits from an active, international resale market, driven by the type's reputation and by sustained demand from institutional operators as well as some private companies. Resale is generally made easier by a complete and transparent maintenance file for both turbines, a clearly documented usage history and life-limited components recently replaced or with a comfortable margin before their due date.

Tip — Always keep maintenance invoices and inspection reports: they are a strong argument at resale time, particularly for the two turbines and the life-limited components.
