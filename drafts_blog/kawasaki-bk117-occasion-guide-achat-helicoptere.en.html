Used Kawasaki BK117: buying guide

Medical and utility roles, inspection points to watch, twin-turbine maintenance and the budget to plan for when buying a used Kawasaki BK117 with confidence.

Why the BK117 remains a light twin reference

Jointly developed by Kawasaki and MBB (which later became part of Airbus Helicopters after several industrial mergers), the BK117 has stood out since the early 1980s as one of the reference light twins for missions that demand dual-engine redundancy and a roomy cabin. Its combination of two turbines, a rugged airframe and a cabin volume that's generous for its class made it a favourite of air-ambulance and rescue operators worldwide, particularly across Europe and Japan.

On the used market, the active fleet spans several successive generations of the aircraft, with noticeable changes in powerplant, avionics and cabin configuration. This diversity makes it essential to pin down the exact version of a given airframe before entering negotiations, as value and operating-cost gaps between generations can be substantial.

The main versions to know

The BK117 has gone through several major evolutions over the decades, from the original A-series to more recent variants with modernised cockpits. Each generation differs in powerplant, avionics and sometimes structural configuration.

  • The earliest series, now the oldest aircraft in the active fleet, trade at noticeably more accessible entry prices, but generally come with less engine potential and more limited avionics.
  • Intermediate versions benefited from powerplant and performance improvements, with airframes generally well maintained thanks to sustained professional use.
  • The most recent variants with modernised cockpits make up most of the fleet's highest residual value, with digital avionics and more advanced flight aids.

Before any viewing, ask the seller for the exact version designation, the details of the installed powerplant and the history of any modifications or upgrades carried out, so you can compare genuinely equivalent offers.

Typical uses: medical, rescue and utility

The prior operational profile of a used BK117 directly affects its overall condition and value. The most common uses found on the European market are:

  • Air ambulance and HEMS (helicopter emergency medical service): cabin fitted out for one or more stretchers and medical equipment, often with avionics dedicated to night or degraded-weather operations.
  • Search and rescue, particularly in mountainous terrain, with specific equipment (hoist, reinforced floor) that may or may not still be fitted depending on the aircraft's history.
  • Utility work and passenger transport, to a lesser extent, for airframes that have left air-ambulance operators after an initial career.

The same model can therefore show a very different wear profile from one airframe to another: an aircraft flown intensively on HEMS missions doesn't have the same cycle and stress history as one used more occasionally for transport. The complete operational history is one of the first things to request before any viewing.

Inspection points to watch

Beyond the standard checks that apply to any used helicopter (complete logbook, compliance with airworthiness directives, overall airframe condition), several points deserve particular attention on a BK117, an aircraft often flown on intensive twin-engine missions.

  • Precise usage history (HEMS, rescue, utility) and consistency with declared flight hours and the logbook.
  • Condition and remaining potential of the main blades, rotor head and transmission components, life-limited parts that take particular stress on an aircraft flown on intensive missions.
  • Time remaining before the next overhaul of each of the two turbines, and consistency between the two engines: a significant potential gap between the two turbines deserves close attention.
  • Signs of corrosion or accelerated wear, particularly on aircraft that have operated in mountains, coastal areas, or with the frequent take-off/landing cycles typical of air-ambulance missions.
  • History of any incidents (hard landing, transmission damage, engine FOD) recorded in the logbook or with the registration authority.
  • Compliance of the cabin configuration (medical fit-out, hoist, seats) with the buyer's intended use, and the condition of the wiring and specific equipment installed by the previous operator.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection carried out by a shop approved on type is strongly recommended before any firm offer, particularly to check the condition of the transmission, the rotor head and the complete records of both turbines, three costly items if remaining potential is limited.

Maintenance and overhauls of the two turbines

Like any twin-turbine helicopter, the BK117 requires a sustained periodic maintenance rhythm, with life-limited components (blades, rotor head, certain transmission parts) that must be replaced after a number of hours or a calendar period set by the manufacturer, regardless of apparent condition.

Having two separate turbines is an important specific point to factor into the technical file: each engine follows its own overhaul schedule, and it isn't unusual for the two turbines on the same aircraft to come due at different times. The cost and timing of each overhaul must be clearly documented before any purchase, since an airframe with one or both turbines close to their due date logically trades at a discount reflecting the cost of the upcoming overhauls. Also check the availability of an approved maintenance shop near the intended operating base, an important factor for a professional aircraft in frequent use.

Price range and operating budget

The BK117 sits in a price segment clearly above that of light single-engine helicopters: on the European used market, observed prices generally range from a few hundred thousand euros for the oldest, most heavily used airframes, up to several million euros for recent variants with modernised cockpits and low potential. This positioning places the aircraft in a budget category comparable to other professional light twins, rather than that of light single-piston or single-turbine helicopters.

Before negotiating, compare several listings of comparable configurations (version, hours, cabin equipment, prior use) to gauge the asking price against the real market, rather than relying on a single isolated reference.

Market and resale

The BK117 benefits from an active, international resale market, supported by the aircraft's reputation among air-ambulance and rescue operators as well as sustained demand on the utility market. Resale is generally made easier by a complete and transparent maintenance file for each of the two turbines, a clearly documented usage history, and life-limited components either recently replaced or with a comfortable margin before their due date.

Tip — Always keep maintenance invoices and inspection reports for both turbines separately: they're a strong argument at resale time, especially if the two engines don't share the same remaining potential.
