The Skylane RG: the retractable gear version

The Cessna 182RG Skylane RG is the retractable-gear version of the Cessna 182 Skylane, produced from the late 1970s into the early 1980s. It targets pilots looking for a cruise speed gain over the fixed-gear version, while retaining the generous payload and airframe ruggedness that made the Skylane's reputation.

On the used market, the 182RG occupies a narrower niche than the standard Skylane: its production run was shorter and its fleet smaller, making it somewhat less common in listings, but it still has a loyal following drawn by its performance and the appeal of flying a complex single with retractable gear.

What changes compared to the fixed-gear 182

The main difference from the fixed-gear Skylane is of course the hydraulically actuated retractable landing gear, which improves the aircraft's aerodynamics and delivers a noticeable cruise speed gain. This added mechanical complexity comes at a cost, however: more demanding maintenance on the hydraulic system and locking mechanisms, specific training required to fly a complex aircraft (retractable gear and variable-pitch propeller), and generally higher insurance premiums than for a fixed-gear 182.

Before settling on a 182RG rather than a standard 182, it is worth weighing the real speed gain delivered by the retractable gear against the added maintenance and insurance cost over the aircraft's ownership period.

What budget should you plan for

The price of a used 182RG mainly depends on the year of manufacture, total airframe and engine hours, the remaining potential before major overhaul (SMOH), the condition of the retractable gear system and the avionics fit. Since the fleet is smaller than the fixed-gear Skylane's, price differences between two examples can be more pronounced depending on how well the gear has been maintained.

Beyond the purchase price, factor in higher ancillary costs than for a fixed-gear Skylane: a more thorough pre-purchase inspection covering the retractable gear system, generally higher insurance for a complex aircraft, and registration transfer fees.

Good to know — Since the 182RG fleet is smaller, take the time to compare several listings across Europe rather than limiting your search to a single national market: this makes it easier to judge a fair price on a less common model.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential. On a 182RG, the inspection must pay particular attention to the retractable gear system, in addition to the standard Skylane checkpoints.

  • Complete engine history: time since the last major overhaul, compliance with the manufacturer's programme, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Operation of the hydraulic retractable gear system, condition of the actuators, hoses and locking mechanisms, and any history of a gear-up landing.
  • Condition of the variable-pitch propeller and its governor, another component specific to the complex aircraft that is absent on a fixed-gear 182.
  • Any corrosion on the airframe, particularly around the wheel wells and areas exposed to moisture.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with particular attention to the traceability of any work on the gear system.
Good to know — Never rely on the seller's verbal statements about the gear's overall condition: a written independent inspection report, including a full gear cycle test, remains the best protection against an unpleasant surprise after purchase.

The retractable gear: an ongoing point of attention

Owning a 182RG means living with the constraints common to any retractable-gear aircraft: systematic vigilance over confirming the gear is down before every landing, periodic maintenance of the hydraulic system, and replacement of certain components over flight hours or calendar time per the manufacturer's programme. A gear-up landing, even if rare across the fleet, remains the incident most feared by owners of this type and can significantly affect an aircraft's history and resale value.

Before buying, always ask the seller whether there is any history of a gear-related incident, and check the consistency between the gear's cycle count and the aircraft's total hours.

Maintenance and running costs

The running cost of a 182RG is higher than that of a fixed-gear Skylane, mainly due to maintaining the hydraulic gear system and the variable-pitch propeller, plus generally higher insurance for a complex aircraft. Other cost items remain comparable: the mandatory annual inspection, parking and, eventually, the major engine overhaul once the time potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained by an approved shop specifically familiar with the 182RG, not only with the standard Skylane, helps avoid unpleasant surprises related to the gear system and preserves resale value.

Tip — Always ask for the last three maintenance invoices, making sure they include a recent intervention on the gear system, before making an offer.

Resale and the used market

The used market for the 182RG is narrower than for the fixed-gear Skylane, which can slightly lengthen resale times but also benefits buyers willing to compare listings across Europe. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history on the gear system and up-to-date avionics, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one with gaps in its technical file.

Comparing several listings remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same vintage almost always come down to remaining engine potential and the documented condition of the retractable gear system.
