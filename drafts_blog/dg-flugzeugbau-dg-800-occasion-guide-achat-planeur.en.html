The DG-800: a high-performance glider from DG Flugzeugbau

The DG-800 is a high-performance single-seat glider designed by DG Flugzeugbau, a manufacturer based in Bruchsal, Germany, and the successor to the Glaser-Dirks lineage. Launched in the mid-1990s to replace the DG-600, it has established itself as a benchmark in the 15-metre and 18-metre classes, valued both in competition flying and by private owners looking for performance and refined handling.

Its composite airframe, flapped wing and interchangeable wingtip extensions (switching between a 15-metre and an 18-metre span depending on the configuration needed) make it a versatile aircraft, equally at home on a club flying day or in a national championship. On the European used market, the DG-800 remains sought after despite its age, thanks to DG Flugzeugbau's reputation for build quality and a fleet large enough that parts and competent workshops are not hard to find.

The DG-800 variants: S, A and B

Before starting a search, it's essential to understand the three main DG-800 variants, which differ significantly in use and in running costs.

VariantEngineTypical use
DG-800SPure glider, no engineMaximum performance at reduced weight, club flying and competition
DG-800ASustainer turbine (climb-out only, not self-launching)Getting home safely after a marginal or weak thermal flight
DG-800BRetractable piston engine, self-launchingFull independence without a tow plane or winch

The DG-800S, with no powerplant at all, remains the simplest to maintain and generally posts the best pure performance thanks to its lower weight. The A and B variants, heavier and more complex, bring valuable independence for a pilot flying solo or away from their home field, at the cost of higher maintenance and running expenses.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used DG-800 depends above all on the variant (S, A or B), the year of manufacture, the number of flight hours and cycles, the condition of the gelcoat, and whether the 18-metre wingtip kit is included alongside the standard 15-metre configuration. A well-maintained aircraft, with a complete technical file and gelcoat free of major repairs, sells for noticeably more than a comparable example with gaps in its history.

Beyond the aircraft's purchase price, your budget should also cover the trailer if it isn't included in the sale, an independent glider mechanic's pre-purchase inspection, insurance, and, for the A and B variants, a reserve for the specific maintenance of the powerplant.

What to check before buying

As with any composite glider, a pre-purchase inspection by a mechanic qualified on the type remains essential before finalising a transaction. For a DG-800, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Condition of the gelcoat and composite structure: look for any hairline cracks, delamination or signs of previous repair, particularly around the wing roots and flap hinges.
  • Operation of the airbrakes, camber-changing flaps and retractable landing gear, as well as control surface play at the stick.
  • Fit and condition of the wingtip extensions (18-metre kit) if advertised as included in the sale, as well as the condition of the winglets.
  • For the A and B variants, condition of the retractable mast, smooth operation of the engine deployment mechanism, and the engine's maintenance history.
  • Complete logbook, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance programme, and traceability of any major inspections already carried out.
  • Condition of the water ballast system if fitted, and the canopy seal.
Good to know — On a composite glider, a written inspection report, ideally from a workshop familiar with the DG type, remains the best protection against an unpleasant structural surprise after the purchase.

The engine system: an asset but also a commitment

For the DG-800A and DG-800B variants, the retractable powerplant is undeniably an asset in terms of range and safety, but it is also a maintenance item in its own right. The mast extension and retraction mechanism needs to work smoothly, and the engine itself follows its own maintenance schedule, with periodic checks and, in the longer term, a full overhaul in line with the engine manufacturer's recommendations.

Before buying a powered variant, it's worth asking the seller for the complete history of work carried out on the powerplant, the number of engine deployment cycles logged, and the date of the last overhaul, so you can factor this cost into your financing plan. A DG-800S, free of this added complexity, remains the simplest and least expensive option to maintain over time for a pilot who doesn't need the range an engine provides.

Maintenance, major inspections and operating costs

Routine maintenance of a DG-800 follows the programme set out by the manufacturer, with a mandatory annual inspection and, at the interval defined in the maintenance manual, a more thorough major inspection covering the composite structure, flight controls and, where applicable, the powerplant. Having this work carried out at an approved workshop familiar with DG gliders helps preserve the aircraft's resale value and avoid unpleasant surprises at a future sale.

Other expenses to plan for include insurance, storage (hangar or trailer), and tow or winch-launch fees for the unpowered variant. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records, with invoices and inspection reports kept on file, are a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the complete logbook and the latest major-inspection reports before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The used market for a high-performance glider such as the DG-800 remains a niche one, driven by a community of enthusiast pilots and by DG Flugzeugbau's reputation for build quality. Listings are mainly found on marketplaces specialising in leisure aviation and gliding, as well as through gliding clubs and federations.

A well-maintained aircraft, with a gelcoat in good condition, a transparent history and, where applicable, the 18-metre wingtip kit included, generally resells more easily and on better terms than an example with gaps in its technical file. Comparing several similar DG-800 listings, taking the exact variant into account, remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
