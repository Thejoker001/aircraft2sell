The Airbus H130, a single-turbine built for comfort and quiet

The Airbus H130 (previously sold by Eurocopter as the EC130, notably in the B4 and later T2 versions) is a light single-turbine helicopter derived from the Ecureuil (AS350) family. It carries over the family's general ruggedness while standing out with a wider cabin and a shrouded Fenestron tail rotor, which removes exposed tail rotor blades and significantly reduces perceived noise on the ground. This feature has made it one of the go-to aircraft for sightseeing flights and missions over noise-sensitive areas.

On the used market, the H130 sits in the same general category as the H125, but is more geared towards comfortable passenger transport than demanding aerial work missions at high altitude or with heavy payloads. A buyer deciding between the two models should first clarify the aircraft's intended use.

Typical uses: tourism, VIP and aerial work

The usage profile of a used H130 varies significantly depending on its prior history, which directly affects its condition and value. The main uses found on the market are:

  • Sightseeing and scenic flights: comfortable cabin, good passenger visibility and reduced noise levels, a major asset for flights over busy sites or urban areas.
  • VIP and business transport: high-end interior fitting for a few passengers, often with reinforced soundproofing.
  • Surveillance and public safety missions: the aircraft's quietness is valued for certain missions over populated areas.
  • Advanced training and transition to turbine aircraft, to a lesser extent, for pilots qualifying after flying piston helicopters.

The same model can therefore have had a very different operational life from one example to another: an aircraft used for sightseeing with many take-off and landing cycles does not have the same wear profile as one that served in VIP transport with fewer cycles but more cruise hours. The operating history is therefore one of the first things to ask for before any viewing.

Inspection points to watch

Beyond the standard checks that apply to any used helicopter (complete logbook, compliance with airworthiness directives, general airframe condition), a few points deserve particular attention on an H130.

  • Precise usage history (tourism, VIP, surveillance) and consistency with declared flight hours and the logbook.
  • Condition and remaining potential of the main blades, rotor head and transmission components, life-limited parts whose replacement represents a significant expense.
  • Condition of the shrouded (Fenestron) tail rotor and its associated components, a feature specific to this architecture compared with the H125.
  • Time remaining before the next turbine overhaul and consistency with the engine logbook and maintenance invoices.
  • Signs of corrosion or accelerated wear, particularly for aircraft that have operated near the coast or in demanding environmental conditions.
  • History of any incidents (hard landing, transmission damage, engine FOD) recorded in the logbook or with the registration authority.
  • Compliance of the cabin configuration (VIP, sightseeing, seat count) with the buyer's intended use and with the Certificate of Airworthiness.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection by an approved Airbus Helicopters shop is strongly recommended before any firm offer, in particular to check the condition of the transmission, rotor head, Fenestron and the complete turbine file, three costly items if potential has been exceeded.

Maintenance and engine overhaul

Like any turbine helicopter, the H130 requires a demanding periodic maintenance schedule, with life-limited components (blades, rotor head, certain transmission parts) that must be replaced after a number of hours or a calendar period set by the manufacturer, regardless of their apparent condition.

The turbine overhaul is the heaviest maintenance item to plan for: its cost and due date must be clearly documented in the aircraft's technical file before any purchase, since an example close to this deadline logically trades at a discount reflecting the cost of the upcoming overhaul. Also check the availability of an approved maintenance shop near the planned operating base, an important criterion for an aircraft used frequently in commercial rotation.

Price range and operating budget

Like the H125, the H130 sits in a price segment well above light piston helicopters: on the European used market, prices generally range from a few hundred thousand euros for the oldest, most used examples, up to several million euros for recent, low-potential models with high-end VIP fittings. This order of magnitude places the aircraft in a budget category comparable to turboprops and small business jets covered elsewhere on Aircraft2Sell (see our guide on the average price of a private jet), rather than light single-engine piston helicopters.

Before any negotiation, compare several listings of comparable configurations (year, hours, cabin fitting, prior use) to place the asking price against the real market, rather than relying on a single isolated reference.

Market and resale

The H130 benefits from an active resale market, driven by steady demand from sightseeing and VIP transport operators, who value its quietness. Resale is generally made easier by a complete, transparent maintenance file, a clearly documented usage history, and life-limited components recently replaced or with a comfortable margin before their deadline.

Tip — Always keep maintenance invoices and inspection reports: they are a strong argument at resale time, particularly for the turbine, the Fenestron and the life-limited components.
