The Stemme S6: a versatile tandem two-seat motorglider

The Stemme S6 is a tandem two-seat motorglider built by the German manufacturer Stemme, known for its high-end self-launching sailplanes. It stands out with a long-span wing optimized for pure soaring cross-country flight, combined with a retractable engine that folds entirely into the nose of the aircraft once gliding flight begins, reducing aerodynamic drag.

This hybrid architecture lets the S6 take off and climb independently without relying on a tow plane or a winch, while keeping glide performance close to that of a pure glider once the engine is retracted. On the used market, the S6 appeals to experienced glider pilots drawn to this autonomy and versatility, who are also prepared to take on an acquisition and maintenance budget higher than that of a classic pure glider.

What budget to plan for

The price of a used Stemme S6 depends above all on accumulated engine hours, the overall condition of the composite airframe, the onboard avionics fitted, and the maintenance documentation available in the logbook. The cost of a motorglider at this level remains structurally higher than an equivalent pure glider, due to the complexity of the retractable engine system.

Beyond the purchase price, budget for a pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic familiar with the type, registration fees, insurance (generally higher than for a pure glider because of the engine), and hangar storage, recommended to preserve the composite airframe and onboard electronics.

What to check before buying

A motorglider like the S6 requires a pre-purchase inspection covering both the classic aspects of a composite glider and the specific features of its retractable engine system. Engage an independent mechanic experienced with this type of aircraft before making any offer.

  • Condition of the composite airframe: look for any impacts, delamination or previous repairs, especially on the long-span wings.
  • Operation of the engine retraction and extension mechanism: run full cycles to test for excessive play or sticking.
  • Complete engine history: engine hours since the last overhaul, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program.
  • Complete flight log and logbook, with clear traceability of the certificate of airworthiness (CofA) and periodic inspections.
  • Condition and calibration of the soaring instruments (variometer, flight computer, FLARM) essential to this aircraft's main use.
Good to know — On a motorglider, a written independent inspection report must cover the retractable engine system, often the most expensive item to repair in case of an undetected fault.

The retractable engine system: a specific feature to watch

The retractable engine of the Stemme S6 is both the aircraft's main asset and the most specific maintenance item to watch. The mechanism that retracts and extends the engine in the nose relies on precise mechanics, and wear or poor adjustment can lead to significant repair costs.

Before buying, ask the seller for the complete history of work done on this system, the frequency of retraction cycles performed, and the date of the last inspection specific to the mechanism. Rigorous tracking of this item is an important trust argument in a negotiation. Whenever possible, personally witness several complete retraction and extension cycles during the aircraft's test, paying close attention to unusual noises, cycle time, and the overall smoothness of the mechanism.

Routine maintenance and operating costs

Routine maintenance of a Stemme S6 combines the usual items for a composite glider (periodic inspection, condition of control surface covering, soaring instruments) and those specific to its engine system (engine inspection, maintenance of the retraction system). This dual nature explains an overall operating cost higher than that of a classic pure glider.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with Stemme motorgliders helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. A complete and transparent maintenance record remains the most convincing argument for a future sale.

Tip — Always ask for the reports from the most recent periodic inspections before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of the actual condition of the engine mechanism.

Typical use and pilot profile

The Stemme S6 primarily appeals to experienced glider pilots seeking full independence for takeoff and climb, without relying on a tow plane, a winch, or club availability. Its long wingspan and glide performance also make it a well-regarded aircraft for cross-country flying once the engine is retracted.

This type of aircraft requires a higher initial investment and operating budget than a pure glider, making it a relevant choice for a pilot already experienced in soaring and motivated by the versatility offered by the integrated engine. Connecting with a club or association specializing in Stemme motorgliders provides valuable firsthand experience on day-to-day operation, type-specific flying best practices, and the choice of an approved shop for periodic inspections.

Insurance and storage

Insurance for a motorglider like the S6 is generally higher than for an equivalent pure glider, due to the engine and its higher overall insured value. Some insurers offer policies specific to motorgliders, which are worth comparing carefully, particularly regarding clauses related to the retractable engine system and the liability associated with mixed glider/light aircraft use.

Closed hangar storage is strongly recommended to protect the long-span composite airframe and onboard electronics from weather extremes. A suitable transport trailer, if included in the sale, is also a significant plus for an owner wishing to take part in competitions or cross-country flights far from home base.

Resale and the used market

The used market for the Stemme S6 remains a niche market, with a much smaller population of aircraft than pure gliders produced in larger series. This relative rarity can work in favor of a seller with a well-maintained and documented aircraft.

A transparent engine maintenance history, a complete maintenance record and up-to-date soaring instruments remain the best arguments for a good sale. Comparing listings available on the European market helps better assess a fair price for this specific type of motorglider.
