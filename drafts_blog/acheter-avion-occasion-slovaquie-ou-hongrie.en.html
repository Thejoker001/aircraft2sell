Buying a Used Aircraft in Slovakia or Hungary: Which Country to Choose

Market, prices, registration, VAT and pitfalls to avoid: a complete comparison to help you decide where to buy your used light aircraft between Slovakia and Hungary.

Two small markets, two different aviation cultures

Slovakia and Hungary are both European Union members and apply the EASA regulatory framework, but their used light aircraft markets remain noticeably smaller than those of Western Europe. Slovakia concentrates its activity around a handful of key airfields (Bratislava, Piešťany, Nitra, Žilina), with a supply dominated by gliders, microlights and entry-level single-engine aircraft from former aero clubs.

Hungary has a historically dense network of aero clubs, especially around Budapest and across the Hungarian plain, with a strong tradition of gliding and aerobatics inherited from an era when the country trained many glider pilots. Used aircraft supply there is slightly more varied than the Slovak average, but still heavily concentrated on simple types: gliders, motor gliders, microlights and light training single-engines.

In both countries, buyers almost always widen their search to neighbouring countries (Czech Republic, Austria, Poland, Romania) as soon as they are looking for a somewhat specific or higher-end aircraft, since the local market alone rarely suffices.

Price comparison by aircraft type

Price levels in Slovakia and Hungary are broadly similar, and both sit below the Western European average for comparable entry-level aircraft. As always, engine history (SMOH hours, i.e. hours elapsed since the last overhaul) and avionics condition weigh far more heavily on the final price than the country of sale alone.

Registration: OM- and HA- prefixes

In Slovakia, civil registration falls under the Dopravný úrad (the Transport Authority of the Slovak Republic), which assigns the OM- prefix. In Hungary, it is the Hungarian civil aviation authority that keeps the national register, under the HA- prefix. In both countries, a purchase between private individuals of an aircraft already registered locally is simply a change of owner on the register, while an aircraft imported from another country must first be deregistered from its original register.

  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and ARC — to be checked before signing, in both countries.
  • Bill of sale — dated, signed by both parties, stating the serial number, required in both Slovakia and Hungary.
  • Third-party liability insurance certificate — required before first use under the new registration, in both Slovakia and Hungary.
Good to know — Both countries apply the same common EASA rules for transferring registration between EU member states: expect generally 2 to 8 weeks depending on how complete the submitted file is, whether in Slovakia or Hungary.

VAT and taxation: two similar intra-EU regimes

As both Slovakia and Hungary are EU members, VAT treatment follows the same community logic in both countries: a sale between EU-resident private individuals is generally outside the scope of VAT, while a sale made by a professional or a company may be subject to it. An aircraft imported from a non-EU country remains liable for import VAT in both cases, calculated on its customs value at the applicable national rate.

The main practical difference between the two countries therefore lies not in the VAT regime itself, but in the availability of professionals familiar with cross-border formalities: since both markets remain modest in volume, it is often worth using a broker or accountant specialised in international aircraft purchases, based in Slovakia, Hungary, or a more established neighbouring market such as Austria or the Czech Republic.

Maintenance, workshops and parts availability

In both countries, the network of approved workshops remains smaller than in Western Europe and concentrated around the main airfields: Bratislava and Piešťany on the Slovak side, Budapest and its surrounding region on the Hungarian side. For common types (Cessna, Piper, Zlín), most routine maintenance is handled locally without difficulty. For more specific parts or a major overhaul on a rarer type, it is not unusual to have to bring in a technician or send the aircraft to a better-equipped neighbouring country, such as the Czech Republic or Austria.

This should be anticipated before buying, especially if you plan to base the aircraft there long-term: ask about the workshop that has historically serviced the aircraft and the distance to the nearest approved maintenance centre, whichever country you choose.

Pre-purchase inspection and documents

Whether the aircraft is Slovak or Hungarian, a pre-purchase inspection by a mechanic approved and independent of the seller remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises. It covers the condition of the airframe, the consistency of the engine logbook, the maintenance history, and avionics compliance with current European requirements (transponder, 8.33 kHz radio), common to both countries through the application of EASA standards.

  • Airframe, engine and propeller logbooks up to date, with no chronological gaps, whether kept in Slovakia or Hungary.
  • Maintenance invoices from the last three years; plan for a translation if the logbooks are written in Slovak or Hungarian.
  • No undeclared structural damage, verifiable through the maintenance file and a thorough visual inspection.
Point of caution — Never finalise a purchase, in Slovakia or in Hungary, without having seen the original logbooks and having their consistency checked by a professional independent of the seller.

Which country to choose based on your profile

The choice between Slovakia and Hungary depends above all on your project and the aircraft you are after. Hungary, with its tradition of gliding and aerobatics, can offer a bit more choice in gliders, motor gliders and training aircraft. Slovakia, more centred on a handful of key airfields, may hold good opportunities on simple single-engines from former aero clubs.

In both cases, the local market remains small enough that it is almost always worth widening your search to neighbouring countries (Czech Republic, Austria, Poland, Romania) to compare more listings at an equivalent budget. The deciding factor remains the quality of maintenance and the transparency of the aircraft's history, far more than the country of registration alone.

Tip — If you are considering an import between the two countries or from another EU country, have the administrative formalities validated beforehand by a specialised professional: this avoids unpleasant surprises after signing.
