Why the AT01 appeals to microlight/VLA buyers

Developed by the German manufacturer Aquila, the AT01 is a two-seat, composite-structure single-engine aircraft, originally designed as a certified aircraft in the Very Light Aircraft (VLA) category before also finding a place in the microlight category on some markets, depending on the country. Its spacious cabin, generous visibility and Rotax powerplant have made it a sought-after choice for flying schools and owner-pilots drawn to a modern, economical and enjoyable aircraft to fly.

On the European used market, the AT01 is found in various configurations depending on the production year: evolutions in avionics, landing gear and the Rotax powerplant. This diversity of model years makes it important to set a clear budget and priorities before starting a search through listings.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used AT01 depends above all on the year of manufacture, total airframe and engine hours accumulated, the status of the Rotax engine (hours remaining before the major overhaul, expressed as SMOH) and the level of avionics fitted. Older examples with reduced engine potential trade at a more accessible entry level than well-equipped recent versions.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: an independent mechanic's pre-purchase inspection familiar with the type, registration and certificate-of-airworthiness transfer fees, insurance, and possibly an avionics refresh if the aircraft needs one.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For an AT01, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Condition of the composite structure: look for any impacts, delamination or previous repairs, which can be hard to detect without a specialist inspection.
  • Complete Rotax engine history: hours elapsed since the last major overhaul, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Condition of the tricycle landing gear and brake components, heavily used during flying-school training sessions.
  • Condition and updates of the integrated avionics, as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements (transponder, traffic-monitoring equipment).
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

The Rotax engine: SMOH and follow-up

The AT01 is fitted with a Rotax engine, a powerplant widely used on modern light aircraft and microlights, valued for its reliability and the availability of its approved maintenance network across Europe. Engine follow-up relies on strict compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program and on precise tracking of engine hours, expressed as SMOH (hours elapsed since the last major overhaul), a key figure to always ask the seller for.

Before buying, it's worth checking the exact SMOH and the history of any technical work carried out on the powerplant, so you can factor this expense into your financing plan if the overhaul deadline is approaching.

Certificate of airworthiness and classification

Depending on the country of registration and the configuration of the specific aircraft, an AT01 may hold a certificate of airworthiness as a certified VLA aircraft or an equivalent microlight status, with classification rules varying from one European state to another. Before buying, it's essential to check the exact status of the specific aircraft, its operating conditions and the associated documentation requirements with the local authorities or an approved body, rather than relying on generic rules.

A complete technical file consistent with the aircraft's declared status greatly eases cross-border registration changes when selling, which are common on the European market for this type of aircraft.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of an AT01 remains moderate compared to a classic certified aircraft of similar capacity, thanks in particular to the efficiency of the Rotax engine. The main expense items remain the periodic inspection required under the aircraft's status, insurance, parking and, in the longer term, engine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type and the Rotax engine helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records in the logbook are a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the latest maintenance invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The AT01 benefits from an active used market in Europe, particularly among flying schools and owner-pilots, supported by good availability of Rotax parts. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and an up-to-date logbook, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same model year are almost always explained by remaining SMOH and the quality of the avionics fitted.
