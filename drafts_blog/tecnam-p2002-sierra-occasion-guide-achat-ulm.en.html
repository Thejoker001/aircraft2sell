Why the P2002 Sierra appeals to owner-pilots

The Tecnam P2002 Sierra stands apart from the rest of the Italian manufacturer's range through its low-wing shape, visually closer to a classic touring aircraft than to the high-wing microlights more commonly seen in the category. This configuration gives it a clear view upward, roll handling that pilots used to certified light aviation often find more direct, and a more "aeroplane" feel that particularly appeals to owner-pilots coming from the club or certified-licence world.

The P2002 Sierra has carved out a place on the European microlight market as a low-wing alternative for pilots who want different handling characteristics without giving up the light weight and lower running costs typical of the microlight category. As with other Tecnam models, the aircraft's relatively wide presence across Europe makes finding parts and comparing listings easier when buying used.

Microlight status and classification by country in Europe

As with any aircraft in this category, the P2002 Sierra's regulatory status varies by country of registration, with real consequences for the required licence, the paperwork and future resale. In France, the type is commonly flown under the national microlight (ULM) regime, with its own registration and oversight track separate from EASA-certified aviation.

In other countries the picture can differ meaningfully: in Germany, the line between the national microlight category and certified light aviation often depends on the exact version and configuration of the airframe; in Italy, the manufacturer's home country, some examples may sit in a national category specific to very light aircraft. In the UK and across several Central and Northern European countries, national classification regimes also differ significantly from one another.

Before committing to a specific example, always check which regulatory category it is currently registered under, and what changing the country of registration would involve: the pilot licence required, maintenance obligations, and possible usage restrictions. Ask for current registration documents rather than relying on a seller's verbal estimate, and contact the relevant microlight federation or authority in the intended destination country if in doubt.

What budget to plan for

The price of a used P2002 Sierra mainly depends on the airframe's age, the engine hours accumulated, how close the engine is to a major overhaul, and the level of onboard equipment. Older examples with reduced remaining engine potential trade at a more accessible entry level, while recent versions with modern avionics represent a larger budget.

Versions and engines to know

The P2002 Sierra comes in several engine variants, generally built around Rotax engines depending on generation, with differences in cabin equipment and avionics depending on the build year. As with the rest of the Tecnam range, some configurations sit closer to EASA-certified light aviation while others remain firmly in the national microlight category of the country of registration.

Pin down exactly which version and configuration a specific example is before making an offer: this determines its possible use, its regulatory category and the availability of spare parts. Ask for the complete engine logbook, the hours since the last major intervention, and traceability of replaced parts to judge the real condition of the powerplant.

Points to check before buying

A pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic familiar with the type remains essential before any transaction. For a P2002 Sierra, a few points deserve particular attention linked to its low-wing configuration.

  • Structure and landing gear condition: corrosion, impact marks on the wing upper surfaces and around the gear legs, which are more exposed on a low-wing design.
  • Full engine history: hours flown, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance programme, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Current registration and microlight classification documents, consistent with the aircraft's current country of registration.
  • Condition and operation of the installed avionics, and compliance with the equipment required in the intended country of operation.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no gaps in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely only on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report, together with a test flight, remains the best protection against a bad surprise after purchase.

Maintenance and running costs

Running a P2002 Sierra stays generally moderate, in line with other Rotax-powered microlights in its category. The main expense items remain the periodic servicing required under the microlight regime of the country of operation, insurance, hangarage or tie-down fees, and, further down the line, an engine overhaul once the hour potential is reached.

Having the aircraft serviced by an approved mechanic familiar with the type limits unpleasant surprises and helps preserve resale value. A rigorous, well-documented logbook is a strong argument at resale time, whatever the intended destination country.

Tip — Always ask for the latest maintenance invoices and the complete logbook before making an offer: they give a faithful picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The P2002 Sierra benefits from an active used market across Europe, helped by Tecnam's strong brand recognition and good parts availability. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and clear microlight classification documents for the intended country, generally resells faster and on better terms than one whose paper trail has gaps.

Comparing several listings for similar examples, factoring in the country of registration and its regulatory category, remains the best way to judge a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
