Used Schleicher ASW 20: glider buying guide

Why the ASW 20 remains a reference performance glider

The Schleicher ASW 20 is a 15-metre single-seat glider, designed by the German manufacturer Schleicher in the late 1970s for the Standard Class and later the Flapped Class. Its composite airframe, efficient wing profile and camber-changing flap system made it one of the most widely built competition gliders of its generation, and it remains much sought after today for club flying and regional competition.

On the used market you'll find several successive variants of the aircraft (ASW 20, 20B, 20C, 20L), with notable developments in wing and fuselage aerodynamics. This diversity of versions, combined with a large installed fleet across Europe, explains a fairly wide price range depending on airframe condition, flight hours and any modifications made over the decades.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used ASW 20 depends above all on its age, airframe hours, the condition of its composite skin, and the variant in question (20, 20B, 20C or 20L). The earliest examples, with a more dated skin and instrumentation, trade at a clearly accessible entry level, while later, well-maintained versions with a recently refurbished canopy and paint represent a larger investment within the category of used performance gliders.

Beyond the purchase price, budget for the additional costs: a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic or an approved workshop familiar with the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance, plus hangarage, a parking spot or trailer upkeep depending on how you plan to operate the glider.

What to check before buying

A pre-purchase inspection by an independent, type-qualified mechanic remains essential before finalising any transaction. For an ASW 20, a few points deserve particular attention given its age and composite structure.

  • Composite skin condition: look for any impacts, delamination or previous repairs, particularly around the wing root fittings.
  • Total airframe hours and the history of periodic airworthiness checks (ARC or equivalent), with a complete logbook and no gaps.
  • Operation and play of the camber-changing flaps and their control linkage, essential to the aircraft's performance.
  • Condition of flight controls, cables, bearings and airbrakes, as well as the canopy's overall fit and seal.
  • Past storage conditions (hangar or trailer): a glider regularly stored under cover keeps its skin and paint in noticeably better shape.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's description of the aircraft's condition: a written independent inspection report, ideally from a workshop familiar with this type's composite airframe, is your best protection against an unpleasant surprise after the purchase.

The flap system: a performance asset to maintain

One of the ASW 20's distinctive features is its camber-changing flap system, which optimises the wing profile according to flight speed and directly contributes to its glide ratio. This mechanism, coupled with airbrakes on some versions, needs regular checks of its play and synchronisation to keep its performance and safe operation intact.

Before buying, it's worth asking the seller or the mechanic handling the inspection for a detailed check of the flap linkage and the condition of the anchor points on the wing, a classic point of attention on this type of older flapped glider.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of an ASW 20 remains comparable to that of other single-seat performance gliders of its generation, with the specifics tied to a composite airframe that is now several decades old. The main expense items remain the periodic airworthiness check, insurance, storage (hangar or trailer) and, occasionally, refinishing the paint or gelcoat if UV exposure has marked the skin.

Having the aircraft maintained by a workshop approved for and familiar with composite structures of this generation limits unpleasant surprises and preserves resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance is a strong selling point at any future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the complete logbook and the latest periodic inspection reports before making an offer: they give a faithful picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The ASW 20 benefits from an active used market in Europe, supported by a large installed fleet and good parts availability for Schleicher gliders of this generation. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and skin in good condition, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one with gaps in its technical file.

Comparing several listings for similar models remains the best way to judge a fair price, whether you're buying now or preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same variant almost always come down to airframe hours, skin condition and documented maintenance quality.

It is also worth asking about any modifications carried out by approved workshops over the decades (canopy replacement, flight instrument upgrades, local reinforcement on the wing), which should be documented through the corresponding technical bulletins. A maintenance file with a clear history of such work is a strong sign of diligence that reassures buyers and smooths negotiation.

Finally, joining a club or an owner-pilot group experienced with the ASW 20 before buying often provides valuable insight into the aircraft's real-world handling, trustworthy workshops for composite airframe maintenance, and the common pitfalls to avoid in a private sale.
