Why the Twin Astir remains a benchmark training glider

Developed by the German manufacturer Grob (Burkhart Grob Flugzeugbau) in the late 1970s, the G103 Twin Astir was for a long time one of the most widespread two-seat training gliders in European clubs. Its composite structure, robust airframe and predictable handling made it a preferred training tool for teaching flying, dual instruction and first solo flights for student pilots.

Many examples built in the 1980s are still flying today, in clubs as well as in private ownership or group ownership. The used market therefore remains active for this model, with a regular supply across Europe, especially in Germany, France, the UK and German-speaking countries where club-based gliding is particularly well developed.

Twin Astir versions and their differences

The Twin Astir went through several evolutions during its production run. The original G103 was followed by the G103A Twin II, which brought aerodynamic and finish improvements, and then by an aerobatic-certified variant, the Twin II Acro, with a reinforced structure allowing certain two-seat aerobatic glider manoeuvres.

Before buying, it is essential to identify precisely the version and exact configuration of the aircraft in question: some operating limitations, particularly regarding aerobatics and allowable load factors, differ noticeably between the standard version and the Acro version. The flight manual (POH) and the type data sheet tied to the registration remove any ambiguity about the aircraft's actual configuration.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Twin Astir depends above all on its age, usage history (intensive club use versus more moderate private use), the condition of its composite structure, its equipment (instruments, parachute, radio, and possibly a towing or winch-launch fit suited to the type), and how close it is to major maintenance milestones. The oldest, most heavily used club examples trade at a noticeably more accessible entry level than better-preserved aircraft kept in private use with a complete, well-maintained technical file.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include costs specific to gliding: a road transport trailer if not included, insurance, club membership and parking fees, and possibly a pre-purchase inspection by an independent glider mechanic qualified on the type.

Good to know — A Twin Astir sold with its transport trailer, an up-to-date parachute and a complete maintenance logbook generally sells more easily than a standalone example whose history has grey areas.

What to check before buying

As with any used glider, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic qualified on the type remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the transaction. For a Twin Astir, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Condition of the gelcoat and composite structure: look for cracks, crazing, previous repair areas or signs of water ingress.
  • Complete history of the aircraft: total hours and launches flown, usage conditions (intensive club operation versus private use), and the repeated towing and landing cycles that stress the airframe.
  • Operation of the dual-seat flight controls, play in the control surfaces, and condition of cables or push-rods.
  • Expiry date and condition of the emergency parachute, as well as the safety harnesses in both seats.
  • Complete logbook, engine logbook (for any motorised variant) and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
Point of caution — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about the overall condition of a composite glider: a written independent inspection report, including a visual and acoustic check of the structure, remains essential before committing.

Condition of the composite structure and controls

Composite gliders like the Twin Astir age differently from metal aircraft: the main points to watch concern how well the gelcoat has held up against UV exposure and thermal cycling, any internal delamination that may be hard to detect visually, and the quality of repairs carried out over decades of club use. Prolonged outdoor storage without a protective cover generally accelerates gelcoat ageing and can encourage moisture ingress.

The flight controls, bearing condition, the retractable or fixed landing gear depending on the version, and the proper synchronisation of both cockpits should also be checked carefully, since a Twin Astir used for training is repeatedly handled by students whose inputs are not always precise.

Maintenance, major inspections and the logbook

Maintaining a composite glider relies on a schedule of periodic inspections defined by the manufacturer and tracked in the aircraft's logbook, together with more thorough major inspections at wider intervals, which include a detailed check of the structure and controls. A complete logbook, regularly kept by an approved workshop familiar with the type, is a strong argument for reassuring a future buyer.

Having routine maintenance and major inspections carried out by a workshop specialised in composite gliders helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve the aircraft's resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records are closely scrutinised by potential buyers at a future sale.

Tip — Always ask for the report of the last major inspection and the date of the next one before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of the structure's actual condition and the cost to plan for in the short term.

Club, school or private ownership

The Twin Astir suits both club-based gliding, where it serves as a training tool for dual instruction, and private ownership or group ownership between several licensed pilots. Club use, being more intensive, generates more flight hours and landing cycles, which can bring forward certain maintenance milestones compared with more moderate private use.

For a purchase intended for a club or association, it is worth anticipating shared maintenance, parking and insurance costs among members, as well as day-to-day management arrangements for the aircraft. For a private or group purchase, the same questions apply on a smaller scale, with the advantage of generally better-controlled and better-documented use.

Resale and the used market

The Twin Astir benefits from an active used market in Europe, supported by a large installed fleet in clubs and good parts availability for a model produced in significant numbers. A well-maintained example, with a transparent history, a sound composite structure and a complete maintenance logbook, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models, taking into account flight hours, usage history and proximity to a major inspection, remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
