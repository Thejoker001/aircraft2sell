Used Piper Lance: buying guide

The Piper Lance, the retractable-gear PA-32

The Piper PA-32R-300 Lance is the retractable-gear version of the PA-32 Cherokee Six family, produced in the late 1970s. It carries over the roomy six-seat cabin of the Cherokee Six but adds retracting gear that improves cruise performance, at the cost of more complex mechanics to maintain. The last examples, sold as the Lance II, feature a T-tail later carried over to the Piper Saratoga, which succeeded the Lance in the early 1980s.

On the European used market, the Lance occupies a particular niche: less common than a PA-28 Cherokee or a Cessna 182, but sought after by buyers who need to carry several passengers and luggage over long distances, often at a more accessible purchase budget than a newer retractable-gear single of equivalent capacity.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Lance depends above all on the age of the aircraft, accumulated engine hours, the engine's status relative to its overhaul potential (SMOH, the hours elapsed since the last major overhaul), and the level of avionics fitted. Aircraft with reduced engine potential and older avionics trade at a noticeably more accessible entry level than recently overhauled, well-equipped examples.

Beyond the purchase price, plan for ancillary costs: an independent mechanic's pre-purchase inspection, qualified on the type, re-registration fees, insurance, and possibly an avionics refresh if the aircraft needs one.

The retractable gear: what to check

The retractable landing gear is the main mechanical difference between the Lance and the fixed-gear versions of the PA-32 family. It is also the item that demands the greatest vigilance at purchase, since a poorly maintained gear system can lead to costly repairs and, in the worst case, a gear-up landing.

  • Complete history of work done on the gear system: actuators, wiring, position indicators, and any backup extension system.
  • Presence and traceability of airworthiness directives applicable to the retractable gear system on this type of aircraft.
  • Look for any prior gear-up landing incident or gear-related structural damage, recorded in the logbook or with the registration authority.
  • Correct operation of the audible and visual gear-position warning systems.
  • Experience of the seller or previous pilot on retractable-gear aircraft, which gives an indication of how carefully the aircraft was likely operated.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection by a mechanic familiar with Piper retractable gear systems is strongly recommended: the cost of fully overhauling the gear system can represent a significant share of the total budget if the problem is only discovered after purchase.

Lycoming IO-540 engine: history and vigilance

The Lance is powered by a 300hp Lycoming IO-540 engine, a widespread and well-known block for maintenance shops across Europe, which makes routine maintenance and parts availability easier. As with any engine in this Lycoming family, the history must be examined carefully: compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts, and confirmation that no open airworthiness directive affects the crankshaft or other critical components for the serial numbers concerned.

Always request the complete engine logbook, including oil analysis reports where available, and compare the displayed engine hours with the airframe hours to spot any inconsistencies. An engine that has undergone a documented full overhaul (major overhaul) at an approved shop is a strong argument both at purchase and resale.

A roomy cabin, the Lance's key asset

One of the Lance's main selling points remains its cabin, directly inherited from the Cherokee Six: six seats in standard configuration, with a rear door that eases access and loading, and generous baggage volume for the category. This capacity makes it an appealing aircraft for large families or business trips carrying several passengers and bags.

At purchase, check the condition of the upholstery and interior fittings, as well as compliance of the approved number of seats with the Certificate of Airworthiness (CofA) and the aircraft's weighing report, especially if interior modifications have been carried out since it left the factory.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a Lance remains broadly comparable to other retractable-gear singles in its category, with an added maintenance cost tied to the gear system compared to a fixed-gear PA-32. The main expense items remain the mandatory annual inspection, insurance, parking, periodic maintenance of the retractable gear, and, in the longer term, engine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with retractable-gear Pipers helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records in the logbook are a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the last three maintenance invoices and the latest annual inspection report before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Market and resale

The Lance benefits from a smaller used market than models produced in larger numbers, such as the PA-28 Cherokee or the Cessna 182, but there is steady demand from buyers looking for a six-seat cabin with good cruise performance. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history on the retractable gear and engine, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models, including newer Piper Saratogas that share the same general architecture, remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
