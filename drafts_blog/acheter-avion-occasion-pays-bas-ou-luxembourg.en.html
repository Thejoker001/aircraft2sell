Buying a Used Aircraft in the Netherlands or Luxembourg: Which Country to Choose

Market, prices, tax, registration and maintenance: a comparison between the Netherlands and Luxembourg to help you decide where to look for your used aircraft.

Two markets of very unequal size

Comparing the Netherlands and Luxembourg means putting side by side two light aviation markets of radically different sizes. The Netherlands has a real fleet of light aircraft, a network of airfields dedicated to general aviation, and airspace that is constrained but structured around several active sites. Luxembourg, by contrast, is a tiny territory whose civil aviation infrastructure relies almost entirely on a single certified airport, Luxembourg Airport (ELLX), shared between commercial aviation, business aviation and light aviation.

This imbalance does not rule out a genuine comparison: many buyers and owners based in Luxembourg search for, and base, their aircraft in neighbouring countries (Belgium, France, Germany), which in practice brings the two markets closer together for anyone willing to travel a little to find or maintain their aircraft.

Market size and dynamism of the used-aircraft trade

In the Netherlands, the used-aircraft market remains modest compared with the large European markets, but it is real and active, concentrated around airfields such as Lelystad Airport, Teuge, Hilversum or Seppe (Breda International). Common light single-engine piston types (Cessna 172, Piper PA-28, Diamond DA40) and microlights are regularly listed, with enough supply to compare several similar aircraft before deciding.

In Luxembourg, the domestic used-aircraft market is extremely limited, for a simple reason: the small size of the country and the limited number of private owners based locally. It is common for a listing described as "aircraft based in Luxembourg" to actually refer to an LX-registered aircraft parked at a nearby airfield in Belgium, France (Moselle) or Germany, due to the lack of space or suitable infrastructure for light leisure aviation at Luxembourg Airport. A buyer specifically targeting Luxembourg therefore has to widen the search to the surrounding cross-border region in practice to have any meaningful choice.

Price comparison

Price levels for the same model and the same maintenance condition remain broadly in line with the rest of Western Europe, which forms a relatively homogeneous price area for used light aviation. The gap observed on a given listing has more to do with the local scarcity of a model, the quality of the technical file and demand pressure at a given time than with any structural difference between the two countries.

In both cases, and even more so for Luxembourg where local supply is scarce, comparing listings published across several neighbouring countries before committing remains the best way to assess a fair price and avoid relying on a handful of unrepresentative listings.

For a buyer based in Luxembourg, widening the search to Belgium, France and Germany from the outset is therefore not a secondary option but, in most cases, a practical necessity to find a comparable choice of aircraft.

Tax and purchase-related costs

Applicable taxation depends above all on the buyer's status (private individual or professional), their country of tax residence and the country of registration chosen after the transaction, more than on the single country where the aircraft happens to be at the time of purchase. A sale between EU-resident private individuals involving an aircraft already in free circulation within the EU generally does not trigger new VAT, whether in the Netherlands (BTW) or Luxembourg. Luxembourg has historically applied one of the lowest standard VAT rates in the European Union, which makes it a registration country watched closely by some business-aviation operators; this advantage, however, mostly concerns professional operating structures rather than a one-off light-aircraft purchase by a private individual.

The situation differs for a sale carried out by a professional, or for an aircraft imported from outside the EU, which can trigger import VAT in either country. In a cross-border transaction between the Netherlands and Luxembourg, it is worth checking precisely the intra-EU VAT formalities that may apply between professionals, as well as the ownership transfer fees specific to each country. Using a professional experienced in cross-border transactions (broker, specialist lawyer) helps avoid procedural mistakes that can prove costly, particularly for a file involving Luxembourg, where firms specialising in aviation tax are few but experienced.

Registration and administrative steps

An aircraft based in the Netherlands is registered with the Inspectie Leefomgeving en Transport (ILT) under the PH- prefix, while an aircraft based in Luxembourg falls under the Luxembourg Direction de l'Aviation Civile (DAC) under the LX- prefix. An aircraft bought in either country can be re-registered in the other, depending on the buyer's choice: there is no obligation to keep the registration of the country of purchase. This change involves prior deregistration from the register of origin, then a new registration with the authority of the destination country, with the full technical file to be submitted and the certificate of airworthiness checked for compliance.

  • Check the consistency between the logbook, the technical file and the actual condition of the aircraft before any registration change.
  • Allow for administrative timelines, which can vary between the Dutch ILT and the Luxembourg DAC depending on how complete the file is.
  • Check any equipment requirements specific to the destination country.
  • Budget for ferry flight costs if an inspection or compliance work is needed before the transfer.
Good to know — Common EASA rules govern the transfer between member states and make it simpler than an import from outside the European Union, regardless of the direction of transfer between the Netherlands and Luxembourg.

Maintenance network and parts availability

The Netherlands offers a network of competent maintenance workshops, especially around the airfields concentrating leisure and training activity. For rarer models or specific engines, it can still be worth checking in advance the availability of parts and the proximity of a specialist workshop, including in neighbouring Germany or Belgium.

In Luxembourg, the supply of approved maintenance workshops dedicated to light aviation is very limited, consistent with the small size of the local fleet. In practice, almost all Luxembourg-based owners have their aircraft maintained at an approved workshop in Belgium, France or Germany, distances being very short from anywhere in the Grand Duchy. This cross-border pattern is so common that it is not really a constraint in practice, provided the chosen workshop is properly approved for the type of aircraft concerned.

The language barrier remains limited in both countries: technical English is commonly used in Dutch workshops, and Luxembourg, a naturally multilingual country (French, German, Luxembourgish), makes it easy to communicate with workshops in any of the three neighbouring countries.

Which country to prioritise depending on your profile

For a buyer looking for a real choice of aircraft to compare within the country where they intend to base their aircraft, the Netherlands offers a significantly larger market base than Luxembourg, whose local supply is very marginal. For a buyer based in Luxembourg, or who wants to register an aircraft there for professional or tax reasons, it is worth anticipating from the outset that the aircraft will most likely be found, bought and maintained in a neighbouring country, with Luxembourg acting mainly as the country of registration and, possibly, of partial basing at Luxembourg Airport.

Good to know — In all cases, a pre-purchase inspection by a qualified independent mechanic remains essential, whether the aircraft is located in the Netherlands, Luxembourg or a neighbouring country.

Ultimately, the choice between these two countries depends less on wanting to "find an aircraft locally" than on your actual objective: local use and a wide choice for the Netherlands, or registration and specific advantages for Luxembourg, accepting that the search and maintenance will, in most cases, happen beyond its borders.
