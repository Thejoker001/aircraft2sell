What is the Issoire APM20 Lionceau

The Issoire APM20 Lionceau is a recently designed French light aircraft, developed by Issoire Aviation, a company based in the Auvergne region specialising in composite structures for light aviation. A two-seater, it positions itself as a modern alternative to classic light aircraft, with a composite airframe that gives it good aerodynamic efficiency and controlled fuel consumption.

On the used market, the Lionceau attracts a varied range of buyers: private pilots looking for an economical aircraft to operate, amateur builders wanting to finish or take over a partly completed example, and enthusiasts of French light aviation keen to support an industrial sector that is still relatively young compared with the established manufacturers.

A kit-built aircraft: what you need to know

The Lionceau is offered as an amateur-built kit, which means every example on the market has been assembled by a different builder, with a level of finish and rigour that can vary noticeably from one aircraft to the next. This specific point completely changes how you should assess a used example compared with a mass-produced aircraft.

Before buying a Lionceau, it's strongly advisable to ask for the complete build log, including photos taken during assembly of the structure, which are no longer visible once the aircraft is finished and painted.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used APM20 Lionceau depends mainly on the quality of the amateur build, the age of the aircraft, the number of engine hours remaining before the next major overhaul, and the level of avionics fitted. An example built by an experienced, well-documented builder generally trades higher than an aircraft whose finish or traceability leaves something to be desired, even at a comparable number of hours.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic ideally already familiar with composite structures and amateur-built aircraft, registration, and insurance, which can vary depending on the exact regulatory status of the aircraft.

What to check before buying

A thorough pre-purchase inspection is essential before finalising a transaction on an amateur-built aircraft. For a Lionceau, a few points deserve particular attention given its composite structure and the way it was built.

  • Overall condition of the composite structure: look for any impacts, delamination or repairs, which can be hard to detect without a specialist inspection.
  • Consistency between the build log and the actual aircraft: verify that any changes are properly documented and compliant.
  • Complete engine history: engine hours elapsed since the last major overhaul, compliance with the maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Condition and operation of the installed avionics, often chosen freely by the amateur builder rather than dictated by an industrial specification.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history since it entered service.
Good to know — On an amateur-built aircraft, the quality of the paperwork matters at least as much as the apparent condition of the aircraft: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

Maintenance and spare parts

Maintaining a Lionceau generally remains accessible, but availability of certain specific parts can be more limited than for a mass-produced aircraft, due to this model's smaller production run. It's advisable to contact the manufacturer or an owners' network before buying to assess the real availability of parts and technical support over the long term.

Having the aircraft maintained at a shop used to composite structures and amateur-built aircraft helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records, along with a current CofA, remain strong arguments at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for contact details of other owners of the model before buying: their real-world experience often tells you more about actual reliability than a spec sheet.

Flying the Lionceau: what's different

The Lionceau is generally described as pleasant and precise to fly, helped by its light composite structure and carefully designed aerodynamic profile. Its efficiency translates into good range and controlled fuel consumption, two points often mentioned by owners satisfied with their choice.

As with any amateur-built aircraft, it's advisable to take a first flight with a pilot already experienced on this specific type, in order to get familiar with any quirks particular to that example, which can differ slightly from one Lionceau to another depending on the original builder's choices.

Resale and the used market

The used market for the APM20 Lionceau remains small compared with mass-produced aircraft, which means sales can sometimes take longer but also means less direct competition between sellers. A well-built example, with a complete build log and consistent maintenance, stands out clearly in this niche market and resells on better terms.

Comparing available listings and talking to the existing owners' network remains the best way to assess a fair price for this model, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. The quality of the amateur build and the completeness of the paperwork almost always explain the price differences seen between two examples.

A patient buyer, willing to view several examples and compare their respective technical files, generally has a better chance of finding a Lionceau that truly matches their budget and expectations than a buyer in a hurry who relies solely on listing photos. It can also help to bring along someone familiar with the model on early viewings, to quickly learn to spot quality differences between two amateur builds.

Finally, keep in mind that reselling an amateur-built aircraft rests largely on trust: a seller able to present a complete, consistent and well-kept file naturally reassures the buyer, and that transparency almost always translates into a faster sale at a price closer to the aircraft's real value.
