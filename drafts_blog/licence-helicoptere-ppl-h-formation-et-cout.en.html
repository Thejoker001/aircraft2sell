Helicopter PPL(H) licence: training and cost

Prerequisites, training programme, real budget and duration to get a private pilot helicopter licence in Europe.

What is the PPL(H)

The PPL(H) (Private Pilot Licence - Helicopter) is the basic licence allowing you to fly a light helicopter in a non-remunerated capacity, in Europe and in most countries recognising EASA licences. It is the rotary-wing equivalent of the fixed-wing PPL(A), but relies on a training programme specific to helicopter flying, most notably mastering the hover and autorotation.

Prerequisites before you start

Before starting PPL(H) training, a student must hold a class 2 medical certificate issued by an approved aeromedical examiner, and must be at least 17 years old to sit the final examination (training and first solo can generally start at a younger age, depending on the school). No prior qualification is required, but a good grasp of aviation English is a real advantage, since many documents and radio communications rely on international phraseology.

  • Class 2 medical certificate — issued by an approved aeromedical examiner, to be renewed periodically.
  • Minimum age for the final exam — training and first solo can start younger, depending on the school.
  • No prior flying experience required — training starts from a complete beginner level.

The training programme

PPL(H) training combines a practical flying component with a theoretical one, validated by a multiple-choice exam covering several subjects (air law, meteorology, navigation, helicopter flight mechanics, performance and mass, communications). On the practical side, the student progresses in stages: first dual-control flights, learning to hover (often the trickiest phase for a beginner), first solo, then navigation flights and preparation for the final flight test with an approved examiner.

  • Introductory flights — first dual-control flights with an instructor to discover helicopter flying.
  • Hovering — a skill unique to helicopters, usually the most demanding part of dual-control training.
  • Autorotation — a fundamental safety manoeuvre in case of engine failure, practised throughout training.
  • First solo — first solo flight, once the instructor is satisfied with the student's autonomy.
  • Navigation — solo and dual-control flights to other airfields, with full flight-planning preparation.
  • Final flight test — practical test with an approved examiner, validating the award of the licence.

The real budget to plan for

A PPL(H) in Europe costs noticeably more than a PPL(A), mainly because the hourly rental cost of a helicopter is higher than that of a comparable light aircraft. The total budget depends heavily on the country, the school and the type of helicopter used for training (a light single-turbine helicopter is noticeably cheaper per hour than a heavier model).

Point of caution — The minimum regulatory hours are rarely enough in practice for a student learning to hover: budget some margin for extra hours, especially on this initial phase, which usually takes longer than expected.

How long it takes to obtain

With intensive training, a PPL(H) can be obtained in just a few months. With standard training, at a rate of one or two flights per week, expect between 12 and 24 months in general. The regularity of sessions matters even more than for fixed-wing training, since hovering relies on muscle memory that fades quickly if training is interrupted for too long.

PPL(H) vs PPL(A): the differences

The PPL(H) and PPL(A) share a broadly similar structure (partly common theoretical subjects, a final flight test with an examiner), but differ sharply in practice and in budget. Mastering the hover, unique to helicopters, generally requires more dual-control hours than for an aeroplane before first solo, which largely explains the overall cost gap between the two licences.

Good to know — The two licences are independent: holding a PPL(A) does not exempt you from completing the full practical PPL(H) training, even though some common theoretical subjects may occasionally qualify for partial credit depending on the school and the applicable rules.

After the PPL(H): ratings and private use

Once obtained, the PPL(H) allows you to fly a light helicopter for private use, alone or with passengers, without remuneration. Additional ratings (type ratings, night flying, mountain flying depending on the school) can later widen your scope of use. Many licence holders eventually consider buying a used light helicopter, alone or in co-ownership, to fly more freely than with rental.

Tip — Before buying a helicopter, always have the logbook, engine hours and remaining time before the next major overhaul (SMOH) checked by a licensed mechanic independent of the seller: helicopter maintenance is generally more expensive and has stricter scheduled intervals than that of a comparable light aircraft.
