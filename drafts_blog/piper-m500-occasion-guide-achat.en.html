Used Piper M500: a turboprop buying guide

Why the M500 appeals to turboprop buyers

The Piper M500 is the most recent turboprop version of the renowned PA-46 family, a single-engine, pressurised-cabin aircraft designed to cover long distances at high altitude with a good level of comfort and reliability. It appeals to owner-pilots moving up from a piston single-engine aircraft who are looking for the performance, cruise speed and ability to fly above weather that a turbine engine provides.

On the used market, the M500 is sought after for its balance of acquisition cost, performance and ease of operation compared with larger, more expensive turboprops. It is nonetheless an aircraft that requires solid budget planning and specific training before taking the controls.

Its place in the PA-46 family

The PA-46 family has gone through several evolutions since its launch, with piston-engine versions and turboprop versions succeeding one another over time. The M500 represents the most recent evolution of the turbine engine for this lineage, with avionics and system improvements over earlier versions.

Before buying, it is important to carefully check the exact production year and the avionics standard installed, since several updates have occurred within the PA-46 turboprop range itself, sometimes creating significant differences in price and equipment.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Piper M500 depends above all on the age of the aircraft, engine hours since the last turbine overhaul, the avionics standard installed and the overall condition of the airframe and pressurised cabin. Turboprops naturally trade at a higher level of investment than an equivalent piston single-engine aircraft, consistent with their performance and operating cost.

Beyond the purchase price, the budget should include a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance suited to a turboprop, and the specific training required to be qualified on this type of aircraft.

What to check before buying

A pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For an M500, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Complete turbine history: engine hours since the last overhaul (SMOH), compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of work performed.
  • Condition of the cabin pressurisation system, often the most costly technical item to repair on a PA-46.
  • Condition and updates of the integrated avionics, as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
  • Validity of the certificate of airworthiness (CofA) and consistency of all modifications made to the aircraft over time.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report by a type specialist remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

Turboprop engine specifics

Moving from a piston engine to a turbine significantly changes the maintenance and operating logic. The turbine engine requires a specific maintenance program, usually based on strictly tracked engine hours, with overhaul work scheduled in advance rather than corrective repairs.

This discipline comes at a cost, but in return it offers generally high reliability and greater predictability of maintenance expenses over the long term, provided the manufacturer's program is strictly followed.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a Piper M500 remains comparable to other single-engine turboprops in its category, with jet fuel and turbine-specific maintenance items. The main expense items remain the mandatory annual inspection, insurance, parking, fuel and, in the longer term, the turbine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records are a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the latest turbine maintenance invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Pressurised cabin: an asset to watch

The M500's pressurised cabin allows flying at high altitude with good comfort for occupants, above most turbulence and bad weather. It is a major asset of this aircraft family, but also a technical system to watch: the seals and pressurisation systems wear with time and may need repair.

Before buying, it is worth asking the seller for the history of work done on the pressurisation system, so you can anticipate any upcoming expense if a repair is approaching.

Resale and the used market

The used market for the Piper M500 remains active in Europe, driven by the appeal of the PA-46 family for owner-pilots who want to move up to a turbine without switching to larger, more expensive-to-operate turboprops. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and up-to-date avionics, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same model year are almost always explained by remaining engine potential and the quality of the avionics fitted.
