Used Cessna Citation Mustang: buying guide

Why the Citation Mustang appeals to owner-pilots

The Cessna Citation Mustang is an entry-level twin-engine business jet designed by Cessna to give owner-pilots access to jet aviation without the operating complexity of larger jets. Certified for single-pilot operation, the Mustang opened the door to private jet ownership for experienced pilots often stepping up from a single-engine turboprop or a high-performance piston twin.

On the used market, the Mustang stands out for its relative simplicity of operation for a jet, modern integrated avionics and an entry cost into jet aviation markedly lower than larger light jets. This accessibility has made it one of the most sought-after twin jets among pilots looking to take the next step in their progression while keeping operating costs under control.

The Mustang also stands as a credible alternative to occasional charter or fractional ownership for owner-pilots who fly regularly over medium distances: once initial training is complete, the aircraft's immediate availability and operating flexibility often weigh more heavily in the decision than the hourly cost alone compared with a charter solution.

How the Mustang has evolved since launch

Since launch, the Mustang has gone through several updates, mainly affecting integrated avionics, navigation capability and some cabin features. The most recent production examples generally benefit from a more capable avionics suite and improved navigation capability compared with the very first aircraft delivered.

Before buying, it's worth pinning down the exact production year and avionics configuration of the aircraft you're considering: these elements directly affect operational capability, ease of resale and, to some extent, the regulatory upgrades that may need to be planned depending on the airspace in which the aircraft will be operated.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Mustang depends above all on the age of the aircraft, total airframe and engine hours, engine status relative to their maintenance program, and the level of avionics upgrades fitted. Early production examples, with reduced engine potential, trade at a noticeably more accessible level than recent, well-maintained aircraft enrolled in an up-to-date manufacturer program.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: a pre-purchase inspection carried out by a maintenance centre approved on the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance suited to a business jet, and possibly an avionics upgrade if the aircraft is not enrolled in an active manufacturer program.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection by a maintenance centre qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For a Mustang, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Engine status and whether the aircraft is enrolled in a manufacturer hourly-cost maintenance program, which directly affects the predictability of future costs.
  • Complete logbook history: compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts, no grey areas.
  • Condition and update level of the integrated avionics, as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements across the airspace it will overfly.
  • Condition of the cabin, landing gear and hydraulic systems, which are stressed differently depending on the aircraft's typical mission profile.
  • Complete certificate of airworthiness (CofA) and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report from a maintenance centre approved on the type remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

Single-pilot operation: type rating and training

The Mustang is certified for single-pilot operation, one of its main selling points for an owner-pilot looking to avoid the cost and complexity of a two-person crew. Single-pilot operation, however, requires a specific type rating delivered by an approved training centre, as well as regular recurrent training to maintain currency.

A pilot stepping up from a turboprop or a piston twin should plan for substantial training time and budget before taking charge of the aircraft, as well as periodic recurrent training to keep the type rating current. This initial and recurring training cost should be factored into the overall project budget, alongside the purchase price and maintenance.

Maintenance, manufacturer programs and operating costs

The operating cost of a Mustang remains comparable to that of other entry-level twin jets in its category, with a significant expense item tied to the hourly-cost maintenance programs offered by the manufacturer for engines and avionics. In exchange for an hourly or annual fee, these programs smooth out unexpected maintenance costs and are generally highly valued by buyers at resale.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved maintenance centre familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records, together with continuous enrollment in a manufacturer program, are strong arguments at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the current status of the manufacturer programs in force (engines, avionics) before making an offer: a lapsed program can trigger significant re-enrollment fees.

Fuel, hangarage and annual fixed costs

Beyond costs directly tied to flight hours, owning a Mustang involves substantial annual fixed costs: hangarage suited to a jet, insurance specific to business aviation, recurrent pilot training, and fuel, which remains more expensive than for a piston aircraft or a turboprop of comparable power.

These fixed costs need to be planned for right from the purchase-project stage, regardless of the number of flight hours envisaged each year, as they represent a significant share of the total cost of ownership of a business jet, even an entry-level one.

Resale and the used market

The Mustang benefits from an active used market, supported by a large installed fleet and good availability of parts and manufacturer support. A well-maintained aircraft, enrolled in active manufacturer programs and with a transparent history, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models, taking into account the status of manufacturer programs and the avionics level, remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.

For a first business jet purchase, it is also advisable to work with a broker or a consultant specialised in business aviation: their expertise in valuing manufacturer programs, maintenance history and the specifics of each individual aircraft often helps avoid costly mistakes and secures a smoother transaction.
