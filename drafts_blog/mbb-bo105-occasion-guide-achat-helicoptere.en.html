Used MBB Bo105: a helicopter buying guide

Why the Bo105 remains sought-after on the used market

Developed by MBB (Messerschmitt-Bölkow-Blohm) from the late 1960s, the Bo105 was the world's first certified light twin-engine helicopter fitted with a rigid main rotor, a design choice that still gives it remarkable agility and sideways/backwards flight capability for its category today. Long used by armed forces, air ambulance services and police units across several European countries, it has progressively joined the civilian used market as fleets were renewed.

On today's market, the Bo105 appeals to operators and private owners looking for a robust twin, proven over decades of intensive use, with excellent parts availability thanks to the large military and civilian fleet that has fed the spares market. Its twin-engine configuration also makes it a popular choice for missions over populated or maritime areas, where engine redundancy is a valued safety criterion.

The main Bo105 versions

The Bo105 range evolved through several variants over its career, distinguished mainly by engine type (different versions of the Allison 250), payload capacity and cabin configuration. The more recent versions benefit from increased power and a higher maximum take-off weight than the early series, which directly affects performance and available payload for operations.

Many examples on the used market come from former government, military or air ambulance fleets, converted for civilian use after a complete overhaul. For a buyer, it is essential to clearly identify the aircraft's origin and prior use, which strongly shapes its flight hour and cycle history.

Purchase budget to plan for

The price of a used Bo105 depends above all on its prior operating history (civilian, military or air ambulance use), the airframe and engine hours accumulated, the remaining life of life-limited components (blades, rotor head, transmission parts) and the quality of the available technical documentation. A well-documented aircraft, with a transparent history and maintenance tracked at an approved workshop, will trade significantly above an example with an uncertain past.

Beyond the purchase price, the budget should include a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type, registration and transfer costs, and the cost of any upcoming work if life-limited components are approaching their deadline.

Points to check before buying

Beyond the standard checks that apply to any used helicopter, the Bo105 presents a few specific points of attention linked to its design and its often intensive operating history.

  • Complete operating history: an aircraft from a military or air ambulance background has generally accumulated more cycles and structural stress than one used privately.
  • Condition and remaining life of the main rotor blades and the rigid rotor head, life-limited components whose replacement represents a significant expense.
  • Full history of both turbine engines, with complete traceability of hours and interventions on each engine separately.
  • Consistency between the logbook, the technical file and the actual condition observed during a physical inspection.
  • History of any incidents (hard landing, transmission damage) recorded in the logbook or with the registration authority.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection carried out by a workshop qualified on the Bo105 type is strongly recommended before any firm offer, particularly to check the condition of the transmission and rigid rotor head, two costly items if their life limits have been exceeded.

Transmission, rigid rotor and maintenance

The rigid main rotor that characterises the Bo105 requires a specific maintenance programme and closely spaced periodic inspections on the rotor head and transmission components. These life-limited parts must be replaced after a defined number of hours or a calendar period set by the manufacturer, regardless of their apparent condition, which represents a cost to anticipate over the ownership period.

The major engine overhaul is also a major expense item to document carefully before purchase: its cost and timing should be clearly established in the technical file, since an aircraft approaching this milestone will naturally be negotiated with a discount reflecting the cost of the upcoming overhaul on both engines.

Running costs and typical uses

The Bo105 remains a twin-engine helicopter, which implies higher operating costs than a comparable single-engine light helicopter, notably in fuel, insurance and periodic maintenance. Today it is mainly used for aerial work missions, twin-engine type training, or by private owners who value the engine redundancy this configuration offers.

Before any negotiation, it is useful to compare several listings for comparable versions to gauge the asking price against the real market, rather than relying on a single isolated reference.

Used market and resale

The Bo105 used market remains active in Europe, supported by a large installed base from civilian fleets and former government fleets progressively being sold off. An aircraft with a transparent civilian history, documented maintenance and recently replaced life-limited components generally resells more easily and on better terms than an example with a poorly traced operating history.

Tip — Always keep maintenance invoices and inspection reports: they are a strong argument at resale time, particularly for life-limited components whose documented history reassures the next buyer.
