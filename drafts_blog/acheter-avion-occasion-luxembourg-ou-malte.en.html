Buying a used aircraft in Luxembourg or Malta: which country to choose

Market, prices, tax, registration and maintenance: a comparison between Luxembourg and Malta to help you decide where to search for your used aircraft.

Two small states, two very different roles

Luxembourg and Malta are both small EU member states within the EASA area, but their positioning in the aviation market differs significantly. Luxembourg, located at the heart of continental Europe, has a used market that is limited in volume but active, driven by a frequently international clientele and a border location that makes it easy to travel back and forth to Belgium, France and Germany. Malta, on the other hand, has built a distinctive reputation in business aviation over the years thanks to an attractive aircraft registry, which skews its used market toward business jets and turboprops rather than leisure general aviation.

For a buyer of a light aircraft (touring single-engine, microlight or glider), Luxembourg is generally the more relevant market to watch, its network of flying clubs and proximity to larger neighbouring markets offering more opportunities in that segment. For a buyer targeting a business jet or a turboprop, however, Malta deserves particular attention, as the Maltese registry has become a recognised reference for this type of aircraft in Europe.

Both markets share the same European regulatory framework (EASA), which simplifies cross-border transactions, but the geographical distance between the two countries is considerably greater than between two direct neighbours: a remote pre-purchase inspection therefore requires more organisation, whether through personal travel or by hiring a local expert.

Size and activity of the used market

The Luxembourg market remains limited in volume given the size of the country, but it benefits directly from its central location and immediate proximity to Belgium, France and Germany: many aircraft listed in Luxembourg belong to international owners or operating structures. Listings there mainly concern light aviation and, to a lesser extent, business aviation.

The Maltese market, by contrast, is structured around a supply that leans much more heavily toward business jets and turboprops, driven by the appeal of the Maltese aircraft registry for management companies and private owners of this type of aircraft. The supply of leisure light aircraft is comparatively more limited, with a smaller locally based light aircraft fleet than in many other European countries.

Comparing the prices charged

Comparing price levels between Luxembourg and Malta only really makes sense model by model and segment by segment, since the two markets are positioned overall in fairly different aircraft categories. For a light aircraft, prices in Luxembourg generally remain comparable to those of neighbouring continental European countries, sometimes with a slight premium on the most sought-after models for lack of abundant local supply.

For a business jet registered in Malta, the listed price often already factors in an ownership and operating structure arranged through a local company, which can affect a direct comparison with a listing for a similar aircraft based elsewhere in Europe.

Tax and purchase-related costs

A private sale involving an aircraft already in free circulation within the European Union generally does not trigger a new VAT charge, whether the sale takes place in Luxembourg or Malta, since the tax was already paid when the aircraft was first put into circulation within the EU. Malta has, however, developed a tax framework specifically designed for aircraft used in a commercial or business-aviation context, which partly explains the country's appeal for this segment among management companies and international owners.

For an acquisition involving a holding structure in Malta or commercial use of the aircraft, it is essential to consult a tax advisor specialised in that country to precisely confirm the regime applicable to the buyer's situation. In both Luxembourg and Malta, using a professional familiar with cross-border transactions (broker, specialised lawyer) helps avoid procedural mistakes that can prove costly.

Registration: Luxembourg's DAC versus Malta's 9H register

In Luxembourg, registration falls under the Direction de l'Aviation Civile (DAC), which issues the LX- prefix. In Malta, the aircraft register is managed by Transport Malta's civil aviation directorate, which issues the 9H- prefix and has built a particular reputation among business jet operators and aircraft management companies, notably for its recognised administrative flexibility in that segment.

  • Check the consistency between the logbook, the technical file and the aircraft's actual condition before any change of registration, regardless of the register involved.
  • For an aircraft imported from another country, plan for its prior deregistration before the new registration can be issued.
  • Plan for administrative lead times, which can vary significantly between Luxembourg's DAC and the Maltese register, particularly depending on the intended operating structure of the aircraft.
  • Gather a valid Certificate of Airworthiness (CofA), the bill of sale and proof of liability insurance before the first flight under the new registration.
Good to know — Common EASA rules govern transfers between member states and make them simpler than an import from outside the European Union, regardless of the destination register. Exact timelines, however, depend on how complete the file is and, for Malta, on the intended operating structure: check precisely with the relevant authority before committing to the transaction.

Maintenance network and parts availability

Luxembourg, a very small country, has a limited number of approved workshops on its own territory, but benefits directly from the immediate proximity of the Belgian, French and German maintenance networks, with no competent workshop ever very far away given the short distances in this part of continental Europe.

Malta has a maintenance base more geared toward business and commercial aviation, with centres experienced in jet-category aircraft, in line with the profile of its registry. For a light aircraft based in Malta, however, it is worth checking in advance the availability of a workshop competent on the type, since the local fleet is smaller in that segment.

In both countries, English is widely used in professional aviation dealings, which makes things easier for a buyer coming from another European country.

Which country to favour depending on your profile

For a light aircraft buyer looking for a touring aircraft or a microlight, the Luxembourg market, with its immediate proximity to the major light aviation hubs of continental Europe, generally remains the more relevant option between the two countries. For a buyer or operator targeting a business jet or a turboprop, Malta deserves particular attention, as the Maltese register and its ecosystem of management companies have developed recognised expertise in that specific segment.

Good to know — In all cases, a pre-purchase inspection by a qualified mechanic or an approved centre on the type remains essential, whether the aircraft is located in Luxembourg, Malta or elsewhere in Europe.

Ultimately, the choice between the two countries depends above all on the category of aircraft being sought and, for business use, on the intended operating structure. Comparing listings and getting precise information on the regulatory and tax framework applicable to your project remains the best way to secure your purchase, regardless of the country chosen.
