Piper Arrow vs Cessna 182: which retractable-gear single-engine to choose

Retractable gear on the Piper Arrow versus the rugged fixed-gear Cessna 182 Skylane: a detailed comparison to help you pick the travel single-engine that fits your budget and mission.

Two philosophies for the same travel mission

The Piper Arrow (PA-28R) and the Cessna 182 Skylane are two highly popular piston singles on the used market, both built for personal and family travel over medium to long distances. Yet their design philosophies differ: the Arrow bets on a cleaner airframe and a retractable landing gear to gain cruise speed, while the Cessna 182 favours simplicity, ruggedness and generous payload with a fixed gear that is nearly bulletproof on rough strips.

Choosing between the two is therefore less about a universal value judgement than about matching the aircraft to your flight mission, your maintenance budget and your tolerance for the added technical complexity of retractable gear.

The Piper Arrow: performance from retractable gear

Derived from the Cherokee family, the Piper Arrow stands out for its retractable landing gear, which reduces drag in cruise and typically delivers a higher speed than a fixed-gear PA-28 of comparable power. Several generations have been produced, with engine and avionics updates that create a varied used-market offering depending on model year.

On the used market, the Arrow appeals to pilots who want to progress toward more complex aircraft, particularly ahead of a future multi-engine rating, since managing retractable gear and flaps is good preparation. Its main point of attention remains the reliability of the gear system, which adds a dedicated maintenance item not present on a fixed-gear aircraft.

The Cessna 182: simplicity and payload

The Cessna 182 Skylane is known for its ruggedness, generous payload and predictable flying qualities, inherited from the wider fixed-gear Cessna family. Its more powerful engine compared with the Arrow allows it to carry more useful load — passengers, baggage, fuel — making it an appealing choice for families or missions that call for capacity rather than raw speed.

The absence of retractable gear considerably simplifies maintenance and landing procedures: no hydraulic or electric gear system to monitor, and no risk of a gear-up landing from a missed checklist item. This is a significant argument for pilots who favour operational peace of mind over cruise performance.

Compared performance: speed, payload, fuel burn

In cruise, the Piper Arrow generally enjoys a speed advantage thanks to its cleaner profile and retracted gear, for fuel consumption broadly comparable to the Cessna 182. In return, the Cessna 182 keeps the advantage in useful load and payload, thanks to a roomier airframe and higher engine power.

Maintenance and operating costs

Operating costs for the Cessna 182 tend to be more predictable, with maintenance concentrated on the powerplant and the items common to any fixed-gear single. The Piper Arrow adds, on top of that base, a dedicated maintenance item for the retractable gear system: actuators, wiring or a hydraulic circuit depending on the generation, plus a manual backup system that needs regular testing.

This difference often shows up as a slightly higher insurance premium for the Arrow, since insurers factor in the specific risk tied to retractable gear, as well as in annual inspections that must systematically confirm the gear system is working correctly.

Good to know — On a used Piper Arrow, always ask for the gear system's maintenance history and check the date of the last backup-system test before committing.

Buying either one used: what to check

For both models, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic familiar with the type is essential. On the Cessna 182, attention mainly focuses on airframe corrosion, engine condition and compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance programme. On the Piper Arrow, add to that list a thorough check of the retractable gear system, its sensors and its backup mechanism.

  • Complete engine history: hours since the last overhaul and compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance programme.
  • On the Arrow: an in-flight test of the full gear retraction and extension cycle, plus a check of the backup system.
  • Overall airframe condition: checking for corrosion, especially in hard-to-reach areas.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no gaps in the aircraft's history.

Comparing several listings of similar model years and equipment remains the best way to assess a fair price, whether for an Arrow or a Skylane.

Which aircraft fits your profile

For a pilot who wants to progress toward more complex aircraft and who values cruise speed, the Piper Arrow is an excellent fit, provided you accept the extra maintenance and vigilance tied to retractable gear. For a pilot who prioritises operational simplicity, generous payload for family trips and maximum peace of mind, the Cessna 182 remains a safe bet.

In either case, the budget available for annual maintenance and your intended frequency of use should weigh at least as heavily as the initial purchase price in your final decision.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Piper Arrow's retractable gear more expensive to maintain?

Yes, retractable gear adds hydraulic or electric components, a backup system and actuators that need regular maintenance, which is an extra upkeep item not present on the fixed-gear Cessna 182.

Is the Cessna 182 faster than the Piper Arrow?

The Cessna 182 offers more engine power and a generous payload, but the Piper Arrow's cleaner airframe, combined with its retractable gear, usually gives it a higher cruise speed at comparable power.

Which aircraft is simpler for a less experienced pilot?

The Cessna 182, with its fixed gear and simpler landing procedure, is often seen as more approachable for a less experienced pilot, while the Arrow demands strict checklist discipline around managing the retractable gear.
