Why the Stemme S10 appeals to demanding glider pilots

The Stemme S10 is a high-end two-seat touring motor glider built by the German manufacturer Stemme, renowned for its outstanding aerodynamic performance within its category. With its very long wingspan and a combustion engine that retracts into the fuselage, it combines the qualities of a high-performance glider with the versatility of an aircraft able to take off and land under its own power, without needing a tow plane or a winch.

This versatility makes it a highly regarded aircraft among pilots who want to fly independently, complete long cross-country soaring flights, or simply enjoy a comfortable two-seater for thermal flying or instrument cruising. On the used market, the S10 remains a niche aircraft, sought after by knowledgeable buyers who are already familiar with the world of powered gliding.

The main S10 variants

The S10 has gone through several evolutions since its launch, with notable differences in powerplant, wingspan and avionics depending on the year of manufacture. The earliest versions rely on a conventional piston engine, while later variants (sometimes marketed under different engine designations, or offered with an extended wingspan) deliver better glide performance and, in some cases, a different powerplant. Before starting a serious search, it is essential to identify precisely which variant is being advertised, since price, performance and maintenance costs can vary significantly from one version to the next.

The aircraft's paperwork (airframe logbook, engine logbook, flight manual) should always confirm the exact configuration advertised by the seller, particularly the wingspan and the powerplant version.

What budget to plan for

The Stemme S10 sits at the top end of the used motor glider market, well above a standard single-seat touring motor glider. The price depends above all on the year of manufacture, total airframe and engine hours, the condition of the composite structure, the avionics fit and the specific variant concerned.

CriterionImpact on price
Year of manufacture and variantThe newest, best-performing versions command noticeably higher prices
Engine hoursAn engine nearing its overhaul limit weighs heavily on resale price
Installed avionicsAn up-to-date electronic variometer, FLARM and glider GPS add meaningful value
Maintenance historyA complete logbook and consistent maintenance by an approved workshop reassure buyers

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic familiar with the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance specific to a motor glider, and the cost of a hangar sized for the aircraft's very long wingspan.

Tip — Always compare listings at equivalent variant and engine potential: an S10 priced unusually low often hides an upcoming engine overhaul or an avionics refresh that is due soon.

What to check before buying

As with any used motor glider, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic qualified on type is essential before closing any transaction. For an S10, a few points deserve particular attention given its technical complexity.

  • Condition of the composite structure and of the folding/detachable wings: look for any impact, delamination or previous repairs, which are hard to detect without a specialised inspection.
  • Operation of the engine retraction and extension mechanism, which should move smoothly, with no binding or excessive play.
  • Full engine history: hours since the last overhaul, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance programme, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Condition and currency of the soaring avionics (variometer, FLARM, logger), as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements.
  • Complete flight logbook, engine logbook and technical file, with no gaps in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report, including a ground test of the engine mechanism and ideally a flight test, remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises after the purchase.

The retractable engine: a technical asset that needs special attention

The retractable engine is one of the Stemme S10's defining features: it allows the aircraft to self-launch, cruise under power, and then retract the powerplant into the fuselage to fly as a pure glider once altitude and favourable thermal conditions have been reached. This sophisticated mechanism requires rigorous, regular maintenance to remain reliable over time.

Before buying, it is worth asking the seller for a detailed history of work carried out on the retraction system, as well as the date of the last major engine overhaul and the hours remaining before the next one, so that this potential expense can be factored into your financing plan if the deadline is approaching.

Maintenance, hangarage and running costs

Running costs for an S10 remain higher than for a pure glider of similar performance, due to the presence of the engine and the very long wingspan, which requires either a suitable hangar or careful de-rigging for transport. The main cost items remain the mandatory annual inspection, insurance, hangarage and, in the longer term, the engine overhaul once its hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved workshop familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and protects resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records, particularly for the powerplant, are a strong selling point for a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the last three maintenance invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained, particularly on the engine system.

Licence and rating required

Flying a Stemme S10 requires a valid glider pilot licence (SPL), together with a specific rating for powered flight on gliders (TMG, touring motor glider), given the presence of a combustion engine. The training and validation requirements for this rating vary depending on the pilot's existing experience on pure gliders or on powered aircraft.

Before buying, make sure you understand exactly which prerequisites apply to your personal situation, and plan for the time and cost of obtaining this rating before taking delivery of the aircraft, if needed.

Resale and the used market

The Stemme S10 enjoys an active but limited used market in Europe, supported by a loyal community of powered-gliding pilots attached to the brand. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent engine history and up-to-date avionics, generally resells more easily and on better terms than an aircraft whose technical file has gaps.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same year almost always come down to the remaining engine potential and the quality of the installed soaring avionics.
