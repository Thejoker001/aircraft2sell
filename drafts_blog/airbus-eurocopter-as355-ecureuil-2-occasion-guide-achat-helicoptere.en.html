The AS355: the twin-engine Ecureuil variant

The AS355 Ecureuil 2, also known commercially as the Twin Squirrel in some markets, is the twin-engine version of the famous Ecureuil family originally developed by Aérospatiale and later Eurocopter, now Airbus Helicopters. It shares the airframe and cabin of the single-engine Ecureuil, but is fitted with two turbines mounted side by side, giving it the engine redundancy valued for certain sensitive missions, notably extended flights over hostile terrain, water or mountainous areas.

On the used market, the AS355 is mainly aimed at professional operators (aerial work companies, emergency medical services, government agencies) rather than private owners, because its operating cost is higher than that of single-engine versions. It remains, however, a sought-after aircraft for its versatility and its ability to operate in demanding conditions thanks to its dual powerplant.

Typical uses: VIP, EMS, aerial work, training

The prior operational profile of a used AS355 varies widely, which has a direct impact on its general condition and resale value. The main uses found on the European market are:

  • Emergency medical services (EMS/HEMS): frequent missions, short repeated flight cycles, high availability requirements.
  • VIP and business transport: comfortable cabin configuration, often with a high-end interior fit-out and extensive avionics.
  • Aerial work and surveillance missions: external load operations, patrol, pipeline or coastline monitoring, missions on behalf of government agencies or local authorities.
  • Multi-engine training, to a lesser extent, for pilots qualifying on twins after a background on single-engine helicopters.

The same model can therefore have had a very different operational life from one airframe to another: an aircraft used for private VIP transport does not have the same wear profile as one that has flown intensively on emergency medical missions. Operational history is therefore one of the first things to ask for before any viewing.

Inspection points to watch

Beyond the standard checks that apply to any used helicopter (complete logbook, compliance with airworthiness directives, general airframe condition), a few points deserve particular attention on an AS355 because of its twin-engine configuration.

  • Precise usage history (EMS, VIP, aerial work, training) and consistency with declared flight hours and the logbook.
  • Condition and remaining potential of each of the two turbines separately: each follows its own maintenance and overhaul cycle, with deadlines that can be offset between the two engines.
  • Condition and remaining potential of the main rotor blades, rotor head and transmission components, life-limited parts whose replacement represents a significant expense.
  • Signs of corrosion or accelerated wear, particularly on aircraft that have operated in coastal, humid or otherwise demanding environments.
  • History of any incidents (hard landing, transmission damage, engine FOD) recorded in the logbook or with the registration authority.
  • Compliance of the cabin/equipment configuration (EMS, VIP, utility) with the buyer's intended use.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection by a shop approved on the type is strongly recommended before any firm offer, particularly to check the condition of both turbines, the transmission and the rotor head separately — three costly items if potential has been exceeded.

Airworthiness and regulatory follow-up for a twin

Like any helicopter in this category, the AS355 is subject to airworthiness directives and manufacturer service bulletins that must be scrupulously followed and documented in the technical file. The twin-engine configuration means separate follow-up for each turbine, which adds administrative load but also provides finer traceability of each engine's condition.

Always check the validity of the Certificate of Airworthiness (CofA), that the registration matches the file presented by the seller, and that the aircraft complies with the requirements applicable to its intended use (public transport, EMS, aerial work), which can impose additional obligations depending on the country of operation.

Maintenance and major overhauls of both engines

Having two separate turbines is both a safety asset and a cost item to plan for carefully: each engine follows its own periodic maintenance and overhaul schedule, with life-limited components (blades, rotor head, certain transmission parts) that must be replaced after a number of hours or a calendar period set by the manufacturer, regardless of their apparent condition.

A major engine overhaul is already a significant maintenance item on a single-engine aircraft; on the AS355, this item must be anticipated for both engines, with deadlines that don't always coincide. Their respective cost and deadline should be clearly documented in the aircraft's technical file before any purchase, since an airframe close to one of these deadlines logically trades at a discount reflecting the cost of the upcoming overhaul. Also check the availability of an approved maintenance shop near the planned operating base.

Price range and operating budget

The AS355 sits at a higher price point than equivalent single-engine light helicopters, due to its dual powerplant and predominantly professional use profile. On the European used market, observed prices vary widely depending on age, total hours, the status of both turbines and cabin equipment, ranging from an entry level for the oldest airframes to a substantially higher budget for recent, well-equipped aircraft with low potential.

Before any negotiation, compare several listings of comparable configurations (year, hours, status of both turbines, cabin equipment, prior use) to gauge the asking price against the actual market, rather than relying on a single isolated reference.

Market and resale

The AS355 benefits from an active international resale market, driven by demand from professional operators looking for twin-engine redundancy at a more moderate operating cost than newer or heavier aircraft. Resale is generally made easier by a complete and transparent maintenance file for both turbines, a clearly documented usage history and life-limited components that have been recently replaced or still have a comfortable margin before their next deadline.

Tip — Always keep maintenance invoices and inspection reports for each of the two turbines separately: they are a strong argument at resale time, particularly to demonstrate consistent follow-up of both engines.
