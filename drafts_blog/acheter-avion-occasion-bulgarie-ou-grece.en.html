Buying a used aircraft in Bulgaria or Greece: which country to choose

Market, prices, tax, registration and maintenance: a comparison between Bulgaria and Greece to help you decide where to look for your used aircraft.

A Balkan market and a Mediterranean market, two different stories

Bulgaria and Greece both occupy a modest place in the European light aviation landscape compared with Germany or France, but for very different reasons. Bulgaria, an EU member since 2007, has a relatively young aircraft fleet and a lower cost of living, which feeds through into maintenance and parking rates. Greece, a country with a strong civil and military aviation tradition, has a more developed network of airfields, largely driven by the need to connect its many islands, yet its used-aircraft market remains narrow given the country's size.

For a buyer whose priority is a good balance between purchase price and ongoing operating cost, both countries are worth comparing together, each offering specific advantages that do not fully overlap.

Size and activity of the used market

Bulgaria's used-aircraft market remains modest in size, concentrated around a handful of active flying clubs and a limited number of professional sellers. Supply is mostly made up of light single-engines and microlights, with a notable share of Eastern European-built aircraft alongside more familiar Western models.

The Greek market, despite a long-standing civil aviation activity, also offers a limited volume of used aircraft. Local demand is partly driven by flight schools and the need for inter-island connections, which favours rugged, cost-efficient aircraft suited to sometimes short runways.

Comparing prices

Price levels for the same model and comparable maintenance condition tend to be slightly more accessible in Bulgaria, reflecting a lower cost of living and technical labour. In Greece, prices sit closer to Western European averages, without any particular discount tied to the country itself.

In both countries, listing-to-listing price gaps for the same aircraft type can remain significant: comparing several similar offers before committing remains the best way to avoid paying a price disconnected from the local market.

Tax and purchase-related costs

As with any transaction within the European Union, the applicable taxation depends above all on the buyer's status (private individual or professional), their country of tax residence and the country in which the aircraft will be registered after the sale. A sale between private individuals is generally not subject to VAT, while a sale by a professional or a company may be subject to it under the national rules applicable in the relevant country.

For a cross-border transaction between Bulgaria and Greece, it is worth checking any intra-EU VAT formalities applicable between professionals, as well as the administrative fees specific to each destination country. Using a professional or adviser experienced in cross-border aircraft transactions reduces the risk of procedural errors, especially when the original documents are not in English.

Registration: Bulgaria's authority versus the Greek HCAA

In Bulgaria, aircraft registration is handled by the Bulgarian civil aviation authority, under the LZ- prefix. In Greece, it is the Hellenic Civil Aviation Authority (HCAA) that manages the registration register, under the SX- prefix. Both authorities apply common EASA rules for registration transfers between member states, which simplifies the process compared with an import from outside the European Union.

  • Valid certificate of airworthiness (CofA) and ARC — a precondition for any flight under the new registration, whichever destination country is chosen.
  • Deregistration from the register of origin — required before the new registration if the aircraft changes country.
  • Complete technical file — airframe, engine and propeller logbooks to be submitted to the new authority.
Good to know — The time needed for a registration transfer between Bulgaria and Greece depends mainly on how complete the submitted file is; planning for several weeks remains a sensible precaution, as with any intra-European transfer.

Maintenance network and parts availability

Bulgaria has a limited number of approved workshops, but these generally charge lower hourly labour rates than Western Europe, which can offset a sometimes longer lead time for certain imported parts. Greece has more workshops spread across the mainland and the main islands, a logical setup given the country's geography, but the availability of specific parts is still often tied to importing from Western Europe.

In both countries, it is advisable to check beforehand that the chosen workshop is properly approved for the aircraft type concerned and has recent experience with the model, rather than relying solely on geographic proximity.

Which country to favour depending on your profile

For a buyer whose priority is minimising purchase price and hourly maintenance cost, Bulgaria often has the edge, provided a narrower choice of models and sometimes longer parts lead times are acceptable. For a buyer planning operations linked to inter-island connections or flight schools, or who simply wants to rely on a more extensive airfield network, Greece may be more relevant despite slightly higher prices.

Overall ownership cost should also factor in ferrying: an aircraft spotted far from your home base will add extra cost and delay before it can first be used, whether it is located in Bulgaria or Greece. The distance between the two countries is moderate but the route can require a stopover, a point worth quantifying before settling on one listing over another.

Good to know — In all cases, a pre-purchase inspection by a qualified independent mechanic remains essential, whether the aircraft is located in Bulgaria, Greece or elsewhere in Europe.

Ultimately, widening your search to both markets often provides access to a broader supply and better negotiating opportunities, especially when the profile you are after matches more closely one country's specialty than the other's. Taking the time to calmly compare listings available in Bulgaria and Greece, rather than limiting yourself to the closest or best-known market, remains the best strategy for finding the aircraft best suited to your use and your budget.
