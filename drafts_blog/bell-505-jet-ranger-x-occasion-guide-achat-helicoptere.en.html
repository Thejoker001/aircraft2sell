The Bell 505: a JetRanger reimagined for today

The Bell 505 Jet Ranger X is a light single-engine turbine helicopter launched by Bell Helicopter and brought into commercial service in the late 2010s, positioned as the spiritual successor to the legendary Bell 206 JetRanger, whose name and versatile character it carries forward. Unlike its illustrious predecessor, designed in the 1960s, the 505 was conceived from the outset around a thoroughly modern architecture: a fully glass cockpit with integrated avionics as standard, a wide five-seat cabin with excellent panoramic visibility, and an airframe optimised to simplify maintenance and keep long-term operating costs under control.

The 505 sits in an intermediate segment between entry-level piston helicopters (Robinson R44) and more established light turbines such as the classic Bell 206 or the Airbus H125. On the European used market, however, it remains a fairly young aircraft: the first delivered airframes have been flying for less than a decade, which currently limits the number of listings available compared with older, more widespread models. This relative scarcity directly affects asking prices and the time it takes to find an example that precisely matches a buyer's requirements in terms of configuration, flight hours and usage history.

Typical uses: training, tourism, utility and VIP

A used Bell 505's usage history varies considerably from one airframe to another, and this has a direct impact on condition and resale value. The most common profiles found on the market are:

  • Advanced training and piston-to-turbine transition — the 505 is a popular choice with flight schools and private pilots thanks to its ease of operation and modern avionics, which make it a good stepping stone toward instrument flying.
  • Tourism and sightseeing flights, thanks to its roomy cabin, excellent visibility and higher comfort level than comparable piston helicopters.
  • Light VIP and business transport for short to medium trips requiring discretion and comfort without the operating overhead of a twin-engine aircraft.
  • Light aerial work: surveillance, aerial photography and occasional missions for operators or local authorities.

An airframe used mainly for flight training generally accumulates more flight hours with frequent take-off and landing cycles, while one used privately or for VIP work often has fewer hours but a higher expectation of interior presentation. The complete usage history therefore remains one of the first things to ask for before any viewing.

Garmin G1000H avionics as standard

One of the Bell 505's major distinguishing features is that it was designed with an integrated Garmin G1000H glass cockpit as standard from the very first delivered airframe, unlike many older helicopters whose analogue avionics had to be retrofitted over the years to stay competitive. This native integration simplifies avionics maintenance, ensures better system consistency and is a strong selling point at resale, since the buyer does not need to budget for an expensive cockpit upgrade down the line.

During a viewing, it remains worth checking the installed software version, the availability of up-to-date navigation databases, and the history of any screen failures or replacements, since these electronic components remain expensive to replace once out of warranty.

Inspection red flags

Beyond the standard checks that apply to any used helicopter (complete logbook, compliance with airworthiness directives, general airframe condition), a few points deserve particular attention on a Bell 505.

  • Precise usage history (training, tourism, VIP, aerial work) and its consistency with the flight hours declared in the logbook.
  • Condition and remaining life of the main rotor blades, the rotor head and life-limited transmission components, whose replacement represents a significant expense.
  • Time remaining before the next major overhaul of the Safran Arrius 2R engine, and consistency with the engine logbook and maintenance invoices.
  • Software version and operating condition of the Garmin G1000H cockpit, along with the history of any electronic failures.
  • History of any incidents (hard landing, engine FOD, transmission damage) recorded in the logbook or with the registration authority.
  • Condition of the cabin interior and optional equipment, particularly for VIP-configured aircraft.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection carried out by a Bell-approved shop is strongly recommended before making a firm offer, especially to check the condition of the transmission, the rotor head and the complete engine file, three costly items if the potential is nearly exhausted.

Maintenance and the Safran Arrius 2R engine

The Bell 505 is powered by a Safran Arrius 2R turbine, an engine known for its reliability and controlled operating costs compared with earlier generations of light turboshaft engines. As with any turbine helicopter, a sustained periodic maintenance schedule applies, with life-limited components (blades, rotor head, certain transmission parts) that must be replaced after a set number of hours or a calendar duration defined by the manufacturer, regardless of their apparent condition.

The major engine overhaul is the heaviest maintenance item to plan for: its cost and timing should be clearly documented in the aircraft's technical file before any purchase. An airframe close to this milestone will logically be negotiated with a discount reflecting the cost of the upcoming overhaul. It is also worth checking the availability of a Bell-approved maintenance shop near the intended base of operations, a particularly relevant criterion since the 505-specific maintenance network is still developing in some parts of Europe.

Price range and operating budget

The Bell 505 sits in a price bracket above light piston helicopters, but generally below heavier light turbines such as the Airbus H125. Since the used market remains young, prices typically range from around one million to roughly one point eight million euros depending on the year of entry into service, cumulative flight hours and configuration (training, utility or VIP). These figures are indicative only — always refine them with market professionals and by comparing several listings with similar specifications.

The resale market: still a young model

The Bell 505 benefits from a modern image and an international Bell support network, both of which work in favour of resale. However, the used market for this model remains young compared with established light helicopters: the number of airframes available for resale is more limited, which can both ease the sale of a well-maintained example (less direct competition) and make it harder to find a precise benchmark for setting a fair price.

Tip — Keep every maintenance invoice and inspection report: they are a strong argument at resale, particularly for the engine, the life-limited components and the Garmin G1000H avionics.
