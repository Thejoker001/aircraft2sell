Extra 300 vs Pitts Special: Which Aerobatic Aircraft to Choose

Two legends of aerobatic flying, two different philosophies. A comparison of the Extra 300 and the Pitts Special to help you choose the aircraft suited to your level, your budget and your competition ambitions.

Two icons of aerobatics, two different designs

The Extra 300 and the Pitts Special rank among the most celebrated aerobatic aircraft in the world, yet they embody two very different approaches to the discipline. The Pitts Special, a biplane born in the United States in the 1940s, dominated competition aerobatics for decades thanks to its agility and compact size. The Extra 300, a German monoplane that appeared later, was designed to push the limits of modern aerobatics with composite structures, high load factors and an exceptional roll rate.

Choosing between the two is therefore not simply a matter of price or availability on the used market: it depends above all on the flying style you are after, the competition level you're aiming for, and the operating budget you're ready to commit to over time.

Both aircraft regularly cross paths at competitions and air shows, making them essential references for any pilot seriously interested in aerobatics, whether for recreational flying or preparation for regional, national or international competitions.

The Extra 300: performance and versatility

The Extra 300 monoplane, offered in several versions (including the fixed-gear 300L and the more competition-oriented 300S), has established itself as a benchmark in top-level aerobatics since the 1990s. Its largely composite airframe, structural strength and powerful engine allow it to withstand very high load factors, sought after by pilots aiming for the most demanding competition categories.

On the used market, the Extra 300 attracts pilots already experienced in aerobatics who want to progress to the most complex figures, as well as certain aerobatic schools and clubs that value its versatility for both advanced training and competition practice.

The Pitts Special: aerobatics' historic biplane

The Pitts Special is a compact biplane, valued for its exceptional low-speed agility and small size, which make it a particularly manoeuvrable aircraft. Offered in several versions over the decades (single- and two-seat, various engines), it remains a very popular choice both for entering serious aerobatics and for competition in intermediate categories.

Its purchase price is generally more accessible than that of a comparable Extra 300, and the used market offers a varied selection in terms of age and condition, which often makes it a preferred entry point for pilots discovering ownership of a dedicated aerobatic aircraft.

Flying style and feel

The two aircraft deliver very different flying sensations. The Pitts Special biplane, with its two-wing configuration, offers immediate control response and great agility, particularly valued for tight aerobatic figures at low speed. Cockpit visibility, however, is more limited than on a monoplane, which requires an adjustment period.

The Extra 300, in its monoplane configuration, offers significantly better cockpit visibility and an extremely fast roll rate, sought after for high-energy modern aerobatic figures. Its overall stability and high structural margins make it an aircraft that is more forgiving of certain piloting imprecisions, while still remaining demanding to fully master its potential.

  • Pitts Special: low-speed agility, limited visibility, "raw" flying feel much appreciated by purists.
  • Extra 300: very high roll rate, better visibility, structure built for extreme load factors.

Budget comparison: purchase and upkeep

Acquisition budget often remains the decisive criterion. The Pitts Special, thanks to a wider used-market supply and older examples available, generally allows for a more accessible entry price. The Extra 300, with its newer composite structure and superior performance, generally sits at a higher acquisition budget for comparable equipment.

Which aircraft for which competition level

The choice between the two aircraft largely depends on the targeted competition level and the pilot's experience. A pilot discovering serious aerobatics or aiming for intermediate categories will find in the Pitts Special a proven, manoeuvrable and economical-to-run aircraft. An already experienced pilot aiming for the most demanding competition categories will more naturally turn to the Extra 300, whose structural margins and roll rate allow high-energy modern aerobatic figures to be flown.

  • Pitts Special: ideal for a serious introduction to aerobatics and intermediate competition categories.
  • Extra 300: relevant for experienced pilots aiming for advanced competition categories.
  • Availability of an instructor or club familiar with the type in question — often a decisive criterion for progressing safely.

Buying either one used: points to watch

An aerobatic aircraft, whether a Pitts Special or an Extra 300, is subject to structural stresses far greater than those of a conventional touring aircraft. The G-logbook, which records the load factors experienced in flight, is an essential document to request systematically before any purchase, alongside the engine logbook and flight logbook.

Good to know — Always have the pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic familiar with aerobatic aircraft, paying particular attention to the airframe (tube-and-fabric for the Pitts, composite structure for the Extra) and to the consistency of the G-logbook with the declared flight history.

In all cases, compare several listings of similar models and model years: it remains the best way to assess a fair price and spot a well-maintained aircraft, whether a historic biplane or a modern competition monoplane.

Finally, check the availability of spare parts and the existence of a network of specialised mechanics near your home base: an aerobatic aircraft grounded for months over an unavailable part represents a hidden cost often underestimated by buyers new to this category of aircraft.
