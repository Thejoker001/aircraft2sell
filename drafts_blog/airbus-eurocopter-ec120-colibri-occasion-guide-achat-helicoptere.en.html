The EC120 Colibri: a light single-turbine built for training and private use

The Eurocopter EC120 Colibri (now under the Airbus Helicopters brand) is a five-seat light single-turbine helicopter, designed in the 1990s to bring the advantages of a turbine engine — reliability, smoothness, and a low noise signature thanks to its Fenestron tail rotor — at an acquisition and operating cost more accessible than the heavier single-turbine models in the Airbus range.

On the used market, the Colibri is a natural step for private pilots wanting to move from a piston helicopter to a single-turbine, as well as for flight schools that value its gentle handling and training-friendly robustness. It differs from piston helicopters such as the Robinson R44 in its turbine engine, which is more expensive to buy and maintain, but generally offers better reliability and a quieter cabin.

Typical uses: training, private and light utility

The use profile of a used EC120 varies significantly depending on its history, which has a direct impact on its condition and value. The main uses found on the European market are:

  • Advanced flight training, for students transitioning to turbine after a piston helicopter background.
  • Private and family use, valued for cabin comfort and the smoothness of its Fenestron compared with a conventional tail rotor.
  • Light utility or surveillance missions (site overflights, light police or civil security missions), depending on the aircraft's configuration.
  • Air taxi and light passenger transport over short distances, in some professional operations.

The same model can therefore have had a very different operational life from one aircraft to another: one used in intensive training does not have the same wear profile as one used mainly for occasional private flying. The operating history is therefore one of the first things to ask about before any viewing.

Inspection points to watch

Beyond the standard checks applicable to any used helicopter (complete logbook, compliance with airworthiness directives, general condition of the airframe), a few points deserve particular attention on an EC120.

  • Precise operating history (training, private, utility) and consistency with the declared flight hours and the logbook.
  • Condition and remaining potential of the main blades, rotor head and Fenestron, components with a limited lifespan whose replacement represents a significant expense.
  • Time remaining before the next Arrius turbine overhaul and consistency with the engine logbook and maintenance invoices.
  • History of any incidents (hard landing, transmission damage, engine FOD) recorded in the logbook or with the registry authority.
  • Condition of the avionics and cabin equipment, particularly for aircraft coming from a flight school fleet, which tend to see heavier use.
  • Signs of corrosion or accelerated wear, particularly on aircraft that have frequently operated near the coast or in demanding environmental conditions.
Good to know — A pre-purchase inspection carried out by an approved Airbus Helicopters workshop is strongly recommended before any firm offer, especially to check the transmission, the Fenestron and the complete turbine file, three costly items if potential is exceeded.

Maintenance and engine overhaul

Like any turbine helicopter, the EC120 requires a sustained periodic maintenance schedule, with components of limited potential (blades, rotor head, Fenestron, certain transmission parts) that must be replaced after a number of hours or a calendar period set by the manufacturer, regardless of their apparent condition.

The Arrius turbine overhaul is the heaviest maintenance item to anticipate: its cost and due date should be clearly documented in the aircraft's technical file before any purchase, as an aircraft close to this deadline logically trades at a discount reflecting the cost of the upcoming overhaul. Also check the availability of an approved maintenance workshop near the planned operating base.

Price range and operating budget

The EC120 sits in an intermediate price segment: more expensive to buy and operate than a light piston helicopter such as the Robinson R44, but generally more accessible than a heavier single-turbine such as the Airbus H125. On the European used market, observed prices vary significantly with age, hours and remaining potential before overhaul, and most often represent several hundred thousand euros for a well-maintained aircraft.

Before any negotiation, compare several listings of comparable configurations (year, hours, cabin equipment, previous use) to gauge the asking price against the real market, rather than relying on a single isolated reference.

Type rating and regulations

Flying an EC120 requires a specific type rating, distinct from that required for a piston helicopter: a pilot coming from a Robinson R44 or another piston single will need to complete a turbine transition course before being able to operate the aircraft independently. This should be anticipated in the overall budget of a buying project, alongside insurance and maintenance. As with any aircraft, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program and applicable airworthiness directives is what keeps the aircraft's Certificate of Airworthiness (CofA) valid.

Market and resale

The EC120 benefits from an active resale market, driven by steady demand from flight schools and private pilots wanting access to turbine flying on a more moderate budget than the heavier models in the Airbus Helicopters range. Resale is generally made easier by a complete and transparent maintenance file, a clearly documented use history, and limited-potential components that have been recently replaced or have a comfortable margin before their deadline.

Tip — Always keep maintenance invoices and inspection reports: they are a strong argument at resale time, particularly for the turbine and the limited-life components.
