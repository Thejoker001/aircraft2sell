Vans RV vs Cirrus SR20: experimental or certified aircraft

Build-it-yourself kit or factory-built certified single-engine: comparing a Vans RV with the Cirrus SR20 to help you choose based on your budget, your project and your priorities.

Two different philosophies of light aviation

The Vans RV and the Cirrus SR20 follow two very different approaches to light aviation. The Vans RV is a range of experimental (amateur-built) aircraft sold as kits, assembled by their owner or by a specialist shop, while the Cirrus SR20 is a factory-built certified single-engine aircraft, ready to fly straight from the production line.

Comparing the two therefore means weighing up two radically different approaches to aircraft ownership: on one side, a customisable, high-performance building project for a controlled budget; on the other, a certified, standardised aircraft governed by a stricter regulatory framework, backed by established manufacturer support.

This choice goes well beyond price alone: it shapes the future owner's relationship with the aircraft for many years, between personal involvement in building and maintaining it on one hand, and the simplicity of using a factory-built aircraft on the other. Understanding the implications of each status, experimental or certified, is therefore an essential first step before comparing the numbers.

The Vans RV: the benchmark experimental aircraft

The Vans RV range has established itself as the world benchmark for build-it-yourself experimental aircraft, with several models (tandem or side-by-side two-seaters, airframes geared toward aerobatics or cross-country travel) sharing a common reputation for handling, strong performance for their category, and contained operating costs. Experimental status allows great freedom in the choice of engine, avionics and equipment, without depending on an STC as a certified aircraft does.

This freedom comes at a price: building the aircraft demands a significant time investment (from several hundred to well over a thousand hours depending on the model and the builder's experience), and experimental status brings specific operating constraints (commercial use limitations, an initial flight-test programme, and maintenance carried out by the owner or a qualified mechanic depending on local regulations).

The Cirrus SR20: an accessible modern certified aircraft

The Cirrus SR20 is the entry-level model in the Cirrus range, a certified single-engine aircraft with a composite airframe, modern integrated avionics and, a distinctive brand feature, a whole-aircraft emergency parachute system (CAPS). It offers the experience of a factory-built aircraft: delivered ready to fly, covered by manufacturer support and parts, and subject to the usual certification framework (maintenance by an approved shop, airworthiness directives, a tracked maintenance logbook).

This regulatory comfort and level of finish translate into an acquisition and maintenance budget generally higher than that of a comparable Vans RV, but also into resale and insurability that are often simpler, since the certified aircraft market is wider and more liquid than the experimental market.

The SR20 has also evolved across generations, with different engine and avionics variants depending on the production year. It is therefore possible to find used examples with dated analogue avionics or, conversely, a fully digital cockpit depending on the model year, which creates a significant budget gap for comparable equipment and flight hours.

Budget comparison: building, buying and operating

The budget for a Vans RV depends heavily on how it's acquired: a new kit to build represents a considerable time investment but often a lower material cost than an equivalent certified aircraft, while an already-built, used Vans RV can reach a price close to that of an older SR20 depending on equipment and finish. The SR20, on the other hand, has a more predictable but higher entry budget, with maintenance costs managed through the approved Cirrus network.

Which aircraft for which pilot profile

The choice between the two depends above all on the future owner's personal project: wanting to build and tinker with your own aircraft, or looking for a ready-to-use aircraft with minimal paperwork.

  • Vans RV: for a pilot who wants to get involved in building, prioritises performance and handling, and accepts the constraints of experimental status.
  • Cirrus SR20: for a pilot looking for a factory-built aircraft ready to fly, a conventional regulatory framework and established manufacturer support.
  • Time available for building or, conversely, for managed maintenance paperwork.
  • Family use or regular travel: the SR20, with its emergency parachute and cabin, is often seen as more reassuring for non-pilot passengers.

Conversely, an experienced pilot looking above all for flying feel and a controlled operating cost after building will find in the Vans RV a hard-to-match proposition in its category.

Buying either one used: points to watch

For an already-built, used Vans RV, the main focus is the quality of the amateur build: complete documentation of the build process, a correctly completed initial flight-test programme, and consistency of the maintenance logbook since the aircraft entered service. An incomplete build file is a major warning sign, as it makes it much harder to assess the aircraft's real quality.

Good to know — For a Vans RV, always use a mechanic or inspector familiar with experimental aircraft to assess build quality, which varies significantly from one example to another.

For a used SR20, the pre-purchase inspection follows the usual pattern for a certified aircraft: maintenance logbook, engine hours (SMOH), the status of airworthiness directives and, a feature specific to the brand, checking the status and expiry date of the CAPS parachute, whose replacement is a notable expense to plan for.

In both cases, insurance deserves particular attention before buying. As an experimental aircraft, a Vans RV can be harder or more expensive to insure for a pilot with little experience on the type, while an SR20 generally benefits from a more mature insurance market and better-established pricing. Always get an insurance quote before signing, as the gap between the two categories can noticeably affect the real annual cost of ownership.
