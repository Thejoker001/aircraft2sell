Exclusive vs Non-Exclusive Sales Mandate: Advantages and Limits

Entrusting the sale of your used aircraft to a broker under an exclusive or a non-exclusive mandate changes everything: visibility, how involved you stay, and the final net price. Here is how to choose.

What a sales mandate is and why it matters

A sales mandate is the contract by which the owner of a used aircraft entrusts a broker or an aviation sales professional with finding a buyer, in exchange for a commission paid once the transaction is completed. This document sets out the duration of the assignment, how the broker is remunerated, the exact scope of the work (listing distribution, buyer qualification, organising visits and test flights, negotiation) and, above all, the nature of the relationship between the seller and the broker: exclusive or not.

This choice has very concrete consequences on the marketing strategy, on how involved the seller is expected to stay, and ultimately on the net price obtained. Many sellers sign this document without fully grasping its implications, even though it determines how their aircraft will be presented on the market for several months.

The exclusive mandate: how it works and its advantages

Under an exclusive mandate, the seller entrusts the marketing of their aircraft to a single broker for a defined period. No other professional can be tasked with the sale during that period, and the seller generally agrees not to sell directly outside that channel without informing the broker, or, depending on the contract's terms, may still owe a commission even in that case.

  • Strong broker commitment — holding exclusivity on the assignment, the broker typically invests more in presenting the listing well (professional photos, detailed description, targeted distribution).
  • Consistent listed price — a single point of contact controls pricing and messaging, avoiding contradictory listings across multiple marketplaces.
  • Personalised follow-up — the broker knows the aircraft's full history and can make a precise case to each prospective buyer.
Tip — An exclusive mandate is mainly worthwhile if the broker has a network of qualified buyers and a genuine distribution strategy; always ask for a written action plan before signing.

The exclusive mandate: limits and points of caution

The trade-off for this exclusivity is total dependence on a single professional: if the broker lacks network or real commitment, the aircraft can remain invisible to a large part of the market for the entire duration of the mandate. A seller in a hurry to close a deal also loses the freedom to approach other brokers in parallel or to seize a spontaneous offer received through another channel.

  • Too long a duration without an exit clause — a 12-month exclusive mandate with no early termination option locks the seller in even if results are disappointing.
  • Commission owed even on a personal sale — some clauses require paying the commission if the seller finds a buyer themselves during the mandate period.
  • No precise commitment of means — without a written commitment on distribution actions, exclusivity can translate into passivity from the broker.
Point of caution — Always negotiate a reasonable duration (3 to 6 months) with an early termination clause in case of clear inaction by the broker, and clarify in writing what happens to a buyer found directly by yourself.

The non-exclusive mandate: how it works and its advantages

Under a non-exclusive mandate (also called a simple mandate), the seller can entrust the sale of their aircraft to several brokers in parallel, and generally retains the freedom to sell directly without owing a commission to anyone. Only the broker who actually introduces the final buyer will receive a commission.

  • Multiplied visibility — the aircraft can appear on several networks and marketplaces simultaneously, increasing the chances of finding a buyer quickly.
  • Brokers compete — each one is motivated to act fast, since the first to bring a serious buyer earns the commission.
  • Freedom to negotiate directly — the seller can keep approaching their personal network or responding to spontaneous enquiries without any contractual restriction.
Tip — A non-exclusive mandate suits a seller who already has a good network or is in a hurry to sell quickly, as a complement to professional distribution on Aircraft2Sell.

The non-exclusive mandate: limits and points of caution

Without exclusivity, no broker has any guarantee of being paid for their efforts, so some invest less in presenting the listing well, settling for standard distribution without a real prospecting strategy. The seller may also end up with inconsistent listings (different prices, uneven photo quality, diverging descriptions) if each broker manages their own communication without coordination.

  • Diluted sales effort — no broker feels fully responsible for the final outcome.
  • Inconsistent listings — the same aircraft visible at different prices or terms damages the credibility of the sale in front of serious buyers.
  • Coordination burden — it often falls to the seller to centralise viewing requests and reconcile the schedules of several intermediaries.
Point of caution — Require every mandated broker to use the same listed price and consistent visuals, so a prospective buyer does not become suspicious when comparing several listings of the same aircraft.

Essential clauses to check before signing

Whichever type of mandate is chosen, certain clauses should always be read carefully before signing, ideally with the support of a legal professional if the value of the transaction justifies it.

  • Duration and renewal — precise start and end dates, renewal terms (automatic or not) and termination notice period.
  • Commission amount and trigger — percentage or fixed amount, and the precise event that triggers payment (signing of the preliminary agreement, effective transfer of registration, receipt of the price).
  • Exact scope of the assignment — which platforms the listing is distributed on, whether viewings, test flight organisation and negotiation are included.
  • What happens to a buyer found outside the mandate — a clause specifying whether a commission is still owed if the owner sells directly during the contract period.
  • Floor price and negotiation margin — a minimum acceptable amount agreed in advance, to avoid any misunderstanding when an offer falls below expectations.
Tip — Always request a signed copy of the mandate before the broker distributes the listing anywhere, and keep written records of any later changes to the terms.

Which mandate to choose depending on your seller profile

An exclusive mandate particularly suits a seller who wants to fully delegate the sale to a trusted professional with a genuine network of qualified buyers and a track record on comparable aircraft, accepting in return a controlled but less extensive distribution. It also reassures the most serious brokers, who invest more time and resources in an assignment where they are certain to receive the commission if it succeeds.

A non-exclusive mandate better suits a seller who already has an active personal network, wants to sell quickly by maximising visibility across several channels, or prefers to keep control over marketing without depending on a single intermediary. In return, it requires more personal coordination and closer attention to the consistency of the listings being distributed.
