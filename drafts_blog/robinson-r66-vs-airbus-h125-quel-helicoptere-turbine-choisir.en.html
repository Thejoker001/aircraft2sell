Robinson R66 vs Airbus H125: Which Turbine Helicopter to Choose

Two turbines, two philosophies

The Robinson R66 and the Airbus H125 (formerly the AS350 Ecureuil/Squirrel) are two turbine helicopters with a strong presence on the European used market, but they are not quite aimed at the same buyers or the same missions. The R66 is Robinson Helicopter's light turbine, designed as a natural step up from the R44 for private owners and small operators who want the benefits of turbine power without jumping to a much heavier aircraft to buy and run.

The Airbus H125, on the other hand, belongs to a higher category: larger, more powerful, able to carry a bigger useful load and more passengers, and built for demanding missions — aerial work, mountain operations, rescue, professional transport — that the R66 simply cannot cover under the same conditions. Comparing the two aircraft therefore starts with clarifying your actual mission before comparing specification sheets.

Typical uses: private owners, training, professional operators

The R66 is commonly found with private pilot-owners, in advanced training schools, or with small operators (light VIP transport, occasional agricultural or utility work). Its size and running cost make it accessible to a wider range of buyers than the H125.

The H125, by contrast, is above all a professional tool: mountain rescue, firefighting, aerial work (lifting, line construction), transporting larger numbers of passengers, or government and civil-security missions. A private individual can certainly own and operate an H125, but the usage profile and the infrastructure required — hangar, specialised maintenance, crew — are generally on a different scale than for an R66.

Performance and capability: what sets the two aircraft apart

Beyond the general philosophy, several structural differences separate the two models: the H125's maximum take-off weight is significantly higher than the R66's, translating into a much larger carrying capacity for passengers, cargo or specific equipment. The H125 also has a highly modular cabin that accepts a wide range of configurations and external accessories (hoist, cargo hook, external basket), which explains its popularity in professional aerial work.

The R66, by design, remains a more compact aircraft, built for a maximum of five seats and for missions with lower payload requirements. Exact weight, power and performance figures vary by version and the specific configuration of each airframe, so always check the flight manual and weighing schedule for the actual aircraft rather than a generic model average.

Used purchase budget

A used R66 sits well below the H125 in purchase price, making it a more accessible entry point into turbine ownership. The H125, because of its size, versatility and the professional demand that drives its market, commands noticeably higher used prices, with well-equipped aerial-work versions or recently overhauled examples trading at even higher levels.

As with any turbine purchase, it is essential to compare several listings of comparable versions (year, airframe and engine hours, equipment) before settling on a price, since the gap between two seemingly similar examples can be significant depending on history and the remaining life of time-limited parts.

Running and maintenance budget

Both aircraft run on Jet A1 and share the general maintenance logic of a light turbine: time-limited components (blades, rotor head, transmission parts), a major engine overhaul to anticipate based on remaining hours, and regular scheduled inspections. The H125, being heavier and more heavily used in intensive professional missions (mountain work, aerial work), generally incurs higher running and maintenance costs than the R66, along with a maintenance structure closer to that of a commercial operator than an occasional private user.

In practice, the R66 stays closer to the maintenance standards of a private light helicopter, with a fairly dense network of Robinson-approved shops across Europe, making maintenance more accessible for a private owner.

Points to check during inspection

The basic checks remain common to both aircraft: logbook compliance, compliance with airworthiness directives, consistency between displayed hours and invoices, condition of the transmission and rotor head, and a complete engine file.

  • On an R66: usage history (private, VIP, agricultural, advanced training) and the remaining life of time-limited parts before the next major overhaul.
  • On an H125: mission history is often more intensive (mountain work, aerial work, rescue) and should be precisely documented, since it strongly affects actual wear on the aircraft.
  • On both: signs of corrosion, particularly for coastal or humid-climate use, and consistency between the engine file and the logbook.
  • Presence of specific equipment (hoist, cargo hook, cabin fit-out) on an H125, and verification of its own compliance and maintenance record, separate from the airframe's.
Good to know — Whichever model you choose, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by a shop approved for that brand remains the best protection against an unpleasant surprise on the transmission, rotor head or engine file.

Market and resale

The R66 benefits from an active and fairly liquid resale market in Europe, driven by Robinson's reputation for reliability and steady demand from pilot-owners moving up from piston aircraft. The H125 benefits from structural professional demand (rescue, aerial work, transport), which supports its value but concentrates the pool of potential buyers more around operators than private individuals.

In both cases, a complete and transparent maintenance file, a clearly documented usage history and recently replaced time-limited components are the strongest arguments at resale time.

Which one to choose for your profile

The choice between the two aircraft depends above all on the intended mission rather than brand preference. The R66 suits a pilot-owner or small operator looking to step into turbine ownership with a controlled purchase and running budget, for private use, advanced training or occasional light operations. The H125 becomes the right choice as soon as the mission requires substantial payload capacity, operational versatility — mountain work, aerial work, rescue — or intensive professional use, at the cost of a significantly higher acquisition and running budget.

In either case, working with a brand-approved shop for a detailed pre-purchase inspection and comparing several listings of comparable versions remains the best way to secure your decision, rather than relying solely on a model's general reputation.
