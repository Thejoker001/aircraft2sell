Used Cessna Citation Excel: buying guide

Budget to plan for, inspection points to watch, maintenance and resale outlook to help you buy a used Cessna Citation Excel, a midsize business jet, with full knowledge of the facts.

Why the Citation Excel appeals to business jet buyers

The Cessna Citation Excel belongs to Cessna's (now Textron Aviation's) midsize business jet family. It stands out with a wider, taller cabin than entry-level Citations, while keeping operating costs and crew requirements closer to those of a light jet, making it a sought-after choice for buyers who want more cabin space without moving up to the long-range jet category.

On the used market, the Citation Excel benefits from a large installed fleet and strong manufacturer support, which makes technical follow-up easier and keeps parts readily available — two essential factors when considering the purchase of a used business jet.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Citation Excel depends above all on the age of the aircraft, total airframe hours, engine status (enrolled or not in an hourly cost maintenance program), the avionics fitted and the cabin finish level. The oldest examples, with no active engine program, trade at a noticeably more accessible entry level than recent, well-equipped aircraft covered by a maintenance contract.

CriterionImpact on price

Avionics and regulatory complianceA cockpit up to date with current navigation and surveillance requirements increases the aircraft's value

Beyond the purchase price, a business jet budget like the Citation Excel's includes specific items: a thorough pre-purchase inspection entrusted to an approved maintenance center with specific experience on the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance adapted to the jet category, and an annual operating cost that remains noticeably higher than that of a light single-engine piston aircraft.

What to check before buying

Buying a used business jet like the Citation Excel justifies a particularly thorough pre-purchase inspection, usually carried out by an approved maintenance center with specific experience on the type. A few points deserve particular attention.

  • Complete engine history: hours elapsed, status of any hourly cost maintenance program, traceability of major interventions.
  • Status of major airframe inspections and structural checks due within the schedule, along with their foreseeable short- and medium-term cost.
  • Avionics compliance with current navigation and traffic-monitoring requirements on the routes you intend to fly.
  • Overall condition of the cabin, upholstery and entertainment systems, along with the history of any interior refurbishments.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no grey areas, and a valid certificate of airworthiness (CofA).
Good to know — For a business jet, the pre-purchase inspection often spans several days and requires a dedicated technical team: this timeline should be planned into the transaction schedule from the start.

Engine programs and maintenance contracts

One of the key factors affecting the value and financial risk of a used Citation Excel is whether the engines are enrolled in an hourly cost maintenance program offered by the engine manufacturer. An aircraft whose engines are enrolled in such a program offers better predictability of future maintenance costs and is generally a strong selling point, while an aircraft with no active program exposes the buyer to significant expense risk at the time of a major uncovered intervention.

It is essential to check precisely the conditions for transferring the program to the new owner, any enrollment costs if the program has lapsed, and the history of payments made by the seller.

Crew, training and regulatory requirements

The Citation Excel requires a crew qualified on the type, with specific training that generally includes a type rating and regular recurrent training. This should be factored into the budget from the outset, whether the buyer intends to fly the aircraft personally or use an outside professional crew.

Regulatory requirements applicable to operating a business jet vary depending on the intended use (private non-commercial use or commercial operation), which has a direct impact on training, maintenance and documentation obligations that must be met.

Maintenance and operating costs

The annual operating cost of a Citation Excel remains noticeably higher than that of a light piston aircraft, owing to jet fuel consumption, scheduled maintenance costs and fixed expenses specific to the business jet category (hangar, insurance, crew). Having the aircraft maintained by an approved center experienced with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve resale value.

A rigorous, fully documented maintenance history, including detailed invoices for past interventions, remains one of the most decisive arguments at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for a full breakdown of the interventions carried out over the last three years before making an offer: it gives an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained and the real status of the engine programs.

Resale and the used market

The Citation Excel benefits from an active used market, supported by a large installed fleet and strong manufacturer support worldwide. A well-maintained aircraft, with engines enrolled in an hourly maintenance program, a transparent history and avionics compliant with current regulatory requirements, generally resells more easily and on better terms.

Comparing several listings of business jets in the same category remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
