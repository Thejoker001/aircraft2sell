Used Hawker 800XP: Buying Guide

Why the Hawker 800XP remains a benchmark

The Hawker 800XP has held a central place in the mid-size business jet segment for several decades. A direct descendant of the Hawker/BAe 125 line, this British-designed aircraft was produced in large numbers and still equips numerous charter fleets, corporate operators and private owners across Europe and the United States today. Its wide cabin for its category, well-proven airframe and large installed fleet make it a particularly liquid used aircraft, with a steady supply on the market and generally good parts availability.

On the used market, several successive generations of the 800XP were produced over a long period, with differences in avionics fit, cabin configuration and maintenance history that explain significant price gaps between two aircraft of similar apparent age. Before focusing on a specific aircraft, it is essential to clearly define your intended use (short regional missions versus longer transcontinental flights) in order to pick the configuration best suited to your needs.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Hawker 800XP varies significantly depending on year of manufacture, airframe hours, engine status (time remaining before the next major overhaul) and the level of avionics fitted. An older aircraft with reduced engine potential and dated avionics trades at a noticeably more accessible entry level than a recent example retrofitted with modern avionics and a refreshed cabin interior.

Beyond the acquisition price, your budget should include ancillary costs: a thorough pre-purchase inspection by a shop qualified on the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance suited to a business jet, and, where needed, an avionics upgrade to remain compliant with current navigation and surveillance requirements.

Cabin, range and typical use

The Hawker 800XP offers a comfortable cabin for its category, typically configured for eight to nine passengers depending on the layout chosen, with a cabin height that is well regarded compared with other jets of similar size. Its range positions it well for regional and short intercontinental missions, which suits typical business travel within Europe or between Europe and the Middle East.

Before buying, it is worth comparing several cabin configurations available on the market: some aircraft have been fitted out for purely executive missions, others for more intensive charter use, which affects the overall condition of the cabin and how often refurbishment is needed.

What to check before buying

As with any used business jet, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by a maintenance shop approved and qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For a Hawker 800XP, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Complete engine history: hours elapsed since the last major overhaul, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Condition of the airframe structure and flight controls, with particular attention to areas historically flagged by applicable manufacturer bulletins for this type.
  • Compliance of the avionics with current navigation and surveillance requirements on the routes you plan to fly.
  • Overall condition of the cabin, interior and ancillary systems (air conditioning, pressurisation, entertainment equipment).
  • Complete airframe and engine logbooks and technical file, with no grey areas or chronological gaps in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — For a business jet, the pre-purchase inspection is usually longer and more expensive than for a light single-engine aircraft: it often involves partial access to the airframe and specific engine testing. This cost should be factored into the acquisition budget from the outset.

Engines and major overhaul

The operating cost of a Hawker 800XP is very largely driven by its engine status and by how close the aircraft is to its next major overhaul. An aircraft whose engines are approaching that deadline typically trades at a significant discount compared with an example with substantial remaining potential, since the buyer will need to plan for a major expense in the short to medium term.

Before making an offer, it is essential to request a detailed, up-to-date engine status report, including the time remaining before the next major overhaul and the history of any repairs already carried out. Certain hourly-cost maintenance programs (so-called hourly-cost cards) can also sometimes be transferred or subscribed to, which can smooth this expense over time and is worth investigating before buying.

Maintenance and operating costs

Operating a Hawker 800XP involves fixed and variable costs well above those of a light aircraft: mandatory periodic inspections, qualified personnel (type-rated pilots and possibly a dedicated crew), hangar storage, insurance specific to business jets, and fuel consumption typical of this type of aircraft. These items should be anticipated from the planning stage, just like the acquisition price itself.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. A rigorous, thoroughly documented maintenance file remains the strongest argument at a future resale, far more than the aircraft's external appearance.

Tip — Always request the complete maintenance invoice history for recent years before making an offer: it gives an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained and helps anticipate upcoming costly deadlines.

Financing and managing your Hawker 800XP

Acquiring a used business jet such as the Hawker 800XP is rarely funded entirely with cash: leasing, specialist aviation finance loans, or co-ownership structures are common options depending on the buyer's profile and expected intensity of use. The choice of financing structure also depends on the intended operating status (strictly private use or partial charter availability).

Once the aircraft is acquired, many owners engage an aircraft management company that handles day-to-day operations, maintenance, regulatory compliance and, where applicable, charter marketing of the aircraft when it is not being used by its owner.

Resale and the used market

The Hawker 800XP benefits from an active used market, supported by a large installed fleet across Europe and North America and generally good parts availability, despite production of the type having ended several years ago. A well-maintained aircraft with a favourable engine status, a transparent history and avionics compliant with current requirements generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar aircraft, taking engine status and cabin configuration into account, remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
