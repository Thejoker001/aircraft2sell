Why the Sinus appeals to light aviation enthusiasts

Designed by the Slovenian manufacturer Pipistrel, the Sinus is a light motorglider known for its remarkable aerodynamic efficiency, composite structure and versatility: it can be used for conventional powered flight as well as extended gliding thanks to its retractable engine. This combination makes it a popular choice among pilots looking for low fuel consumption, low noise and long flight endurance at a contained operating cost.

On the used market, the Sinus comes in several successive versions, with developments in wingspan, engine and onboard avionics. This diversity of configurations explains significant price differences, which makes it essential to clearly define your budget and intended use before starting a search.

It is also advisable to look into the community of Pipistrel pilots and owners, fairly active in Europe, in order to benefit from concrete feedback on maintenance, recurring issues observed on the type, and specialist workshops to favour before finalising a transaction.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Pipistrel Sinus depends above all on the age of the aircraft, engine hours, the condition of the composite structure and the level of avionics fitted. The oldest models, with higher engine hours, trade at a noticeably more accessible entry level than well-equipped recent versions, which can represent a larger investment.

CriterionImpact on price
Age of the aircraftMore recent generations trade noticeably higher
Engine hours (SMOH)An engine close to its major overhaul limit weighs heavily on resale price

Maintenance historyA complete logbook and consistent maintenance at an approved shop reassure the buyer

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: an independent mechanic's pre-purchase inspection qualified on the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance suited to the intended use, and possibly an avionics upgrade if the aircraft needs one.

Costs related to importing from another European country should also be anticipated, particularly when a change of registration register involves administrative formalities and filing fees that vary depending on the relevant civil aviation authority. Comparing these ancillary costs across several aircraft can tip the final decision.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For a Sinus, a few points deserve particular attention given its composite structure and engine-retraction mechanism.

  • Condition of the composite structure of the wings and fuselage: look for any impacts, delamination or previous repairs, which can be hard to detect without a specialist inspection.
  • Operation of the engine retraction and extension mechanism, a specific mechanical component that must be checked carefully.
  • Complete engine history: engine hours elapsed, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Condition of the folding or retractable propeller and its locking system in gliding configuration.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase, especially on an all-composite structure.

A motorglider: long wings, retractable engine and dual use

The Sinus's distinguishing feature lies in its motorglider formula: long wings offering high aerodynamic efficiency, combined with an engine that can be retracted in flight to extend gliding once altitude has been reached. This design allows for dual use, combining economical powered trips and leisure gliding, but it also requires specific maintenance of the retraction mechanism and increased attention to the integrity of the wings, which are longer and thinner than on a conventional light aircraft.

Before buying, it is worth having the full engine extension and retraction cycle test-flown by a pilot experienced on the type, to confirm the mechanism operates without binding or excessive play.

A retraction mechanism that binds or shows noticeable play can indicate premature wear of the relevant guides or actuators, the repair of which can represent a significant cost. This point deserves as much attention as the engine's own condition when negotiating the price.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a Sinus remains among the lowest in its category, thanks to its low fuel consumption and lightweight structure. The main expense items remain the mandatory annual inspection, insurance, parking, and in the longer term engine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type and its composite structure helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records, particularly of the engine retraction mechanism, are a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the last three maintenance invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Regulatory status and operating framework

The Pipistrel Sinus is generally operated under a microlight or light-aircraft status depending on the country and the aircraft's configuration, which affects applicable pilot licence and operating requirements. The precise regulatory framework (class, mass limits, licence required) varies by country of registration and is updated regularly: it is therefore essential to check the applicable rules with the relevant civil aviation authority or a qualified instructor before any purchase or operation.

This point deserves particular attention in a cross-border transaction in Europe, where regulatory statuses can differ significantly from one country to another.

An aircraft registered in one country may thus require additional steps to fly in another, particularly regarding a recognised pilot licence or an airworthiness certificate suited to the new regulatory framework. Checking in advance with the relevant authority avoids unpleasant surprises after the purchase.

Resale and the used market

The Sinus benefits from an active used market in Europe, supported by a notable installed fleet among fans of economical light flying and motorgliding. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history, a functional engine retraction mechanism and a sound composite structure, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same model year are almost always explained by remaining engine hours and the condition of the composite structure.

Finally, a complete sales file, including maintenance invoices, inspection reports and clear traceability of the registration history, greatly facilitates the transaction and reassures a potential buyer about the reliability of the aircraft on offer.
