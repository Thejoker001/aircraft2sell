A budget under 5 million euros: which jet segment?

An acquisition budget under 5 million euros radically changes what's available on the used market compared with a budget under 3 million. You leave the light and entry-level jet segment and enter the mid-size and super-mid-size category, and even some early long-range jets. These aircraft offer a stand-up cabin, generous seat spacing, longer range and, often, the ability to cross the Atlantic nonstop on the best-equipped versions.

Three models illustrate particularly well what this budget can buy on the used market today: the Cessna Citation Sovereign, the Bombardier Challenger 300 and early generations of the Dassault Falcon 2000. Three different philosophies, for three different mission profiles.

Cessna Citation Sovereign: a wide cabin, single-pilot operation possible

The Citation Sovereign was designed by Cessna as a mid-size jet offering an unusually wide cabin for its category, with standing headroom over most of its length. It can typically seat 8 to 9 passengers in travel configuration, with a flexible cabin layout.

An advantage often cited by private operators: certain Sovereign versions are certified for single-pilot operation, which noticeably reduces operating costs compared with an aircraft that always requires two crew members. On the used market, the oldest examples trade at an entry level well below 5 million euros, while well-equipped recent versions can approach that figure.

Bombardier Challenger 300: the super-mid-size benchmark

The Challenger 300 (and its later Challenger 350 derivative) set the standard for the super-mid-size segment: a wide, bright cabin, high cruise speed for its category, and enough range to connect Europe with a large part of the American continent on the best-equipped versions. Its reputation for reliability and large in-service fleet make it a well-known type for maintenance shops, which makes finding parts and technical expertise easier.

On the used market, the earliest Challenger 300 examples (entering service in the mid-2000s) fall within the 5 million euro envelope depending on condition, airframe hours and engine status. It's a frequent choice for a company or family group wanting a versatile aircraft capable of transcontinental missions without the complexity and cost of a heavier long-range jet.

Dassault Falcon 2000 (early generations): a generous cabin, trijet heritage

The Falcon 2000, derived from Dassault's trijet Falcon lineage but built as a twin-engine aircraft, offers a particularly spacious cabin for an aircraft of this range, directly inherited from Dassault's expertise on Falcon 900 cabins. The earliest Falcon 2000 generations, now available on the used market at price levels compatible with a budget under 5 million euros, retain this cabin-comfort philosophy while offering an interesting range for transatlantic missions with a stop, or direct intra-continental trips.

The aircraft has a reputation for ruggedness built on Dassault's experience in high-end business aviation. As with any jet of this age, engine status and maintenance history become decisive in assessing the real cost of ownership in the years following purchase.

Good to know — At this price point, two aircraft of the same model and model year can show a large value gap depending on engine status (remaining potential before major overhaul) and installed avionics. Never compare only the listed price without adjusting both aircraft to the same technical status.

In all three cases, the listed purchase price represents only part of the total budget: you must always factor in the cost of a pre-purchase inspection, registration fees, insurance and, above all, anticipate upcoming major overhauls and engine overhaul depending on the aircraft's remaining potential.

Operating costs and maintenance to plan for

These three jets belong to categories where annual operating cost far exceeds that of a light aircraft or a turboprop. Beyond fuel, the main expense items to budget are manufacturer scheduled maintenance, hangarage, insurance, recurrent crew training and, in the longer term, the major overhaul or engine overhaul depending on the hourly potential remaining at purchase.

  • An hourly engine maintenance program can smooth the cost of an overhaul but applies differently depending on the model and engine manufacturer.
  • Crew cost (one or two type-qualified pilots) weighs directly on the annual operating budget.
  • The age of the installed avionics may require an upgrade to remain compliant with current navigation and traffic-monitoring requirements.
  • The availability of parts and approved shops varies noticeably from one model to another depending on the size of the in-service fleet.

How to choose based on your mission

The choice between these three aircraft should primarily be based on your real mission rather than on budget alone. A Citation Sovereign suits a company looking for a versatile jet for European and intra-continental missions with a controlled operating cost. A Challenger 300 is for those who need longer range, high cruise performance and an upscale cabin for regular transcontinental missions. An early-generation Falcon 2000 will appeal to buyers for whom cabin comfort and Dassault's long-range heritage matter more than raw performance.

In all cases, the number of flight hours planned per year remains the most decisive criterion: a jet in this category only makes economic sense for sufficiently regular use, otherwise fixed costs (hangarage, insurance, maintaining crew proficiency) weigh disproportionately heavily against alternatives such as charter or fractional ownership.

Points to watch before buying

Whether it's a used Citation Sovereign, Challenger 300 or Falcon 2000, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by a maintenance centre approved on the type remains essential before any transaction of this size. Engine status, the complete logbook history, avionics compliance and the absence of undocumented structural damage are the four points that determine the aircraft's real value, far more than its outward appearance.

Tip — Always request a detailed technical summary (engine status, airframe hours, major overhaul history) before any physical viewing: it quickly rules out aircraft whose technical profile doesn't match your real budget, including upcoming major overhauls.

Comparing several listings of similar models, with comparable engine status, remains the best way to assess a fair price in this segment, where gaps between two seemingly similar aircraft can represent several hundred thousand euros.
