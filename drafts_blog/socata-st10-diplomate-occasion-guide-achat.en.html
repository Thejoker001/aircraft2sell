The Socata ST10 Diplomate: an overlooked French single-engine

The Socata ST10 Diplomate is a French four-seat touring aircraft, developed by Socata (at the time a subsidiary of Sud Aviation, based in Tarbes) from the Gardan GY-80 Horizon. The aircraft was first presented under the names "Super Horizon 200" and then "Provence", before finally being marketed as the Diplomate. The first prototype flew on 7 November 1967, and series production ran from 1970 to 1974.

Unlike models produced by the thousand, such as the Cessna 172 or the Piper PA-28, the ST10 Diplomate remained a limited-series aircraft: just over fifty examples were built before production ended. That rarity makes it a distinctive aircraft on the European market today, sought after more by enthusiasts of vintage French aviation than by a buyer simply looking for a conventional touring aircraft.

Technical specifications and flight characteristics

The ST10 Diplomate is a low-wing single-engine aircraft with electrically-actuated retractable gear, powered by a fuel-injected Lycoming IO-360-C1B producing around 200 hp. It carries over some of the lines of the GY-80 Horizon it derives from, with enlarged tail surfaces and an all-moving elevator, while offering noticeably more interior space and an externally accessible baggage compartment compared with its predecessor.

  • Capacity: four seats, a single access door on the right-hand side.
  • Engine: fuel-injected Lycoming IO-360-C1B, around 200 hp, Hartzell two-blade constant-speed propeller.
  • Cruise speed at 75% power around 265 km/h (143 kt), maximum speed around 280 km/h (150 kt).
  • Range of roughly 1,300–1,400 km with one pilot and three passengers.
  • Empty weight of around 724 kg, maximum take-off weight of around 1,222 kg.

These figures, taken from period manufacturer data, remain indicative: the actual condition of a given example (onboard equipment, any modifications) can shift these performance numbers. Any figure should be confirmed against the technical file and flight manual of the specific aircraft.

An extremely rare aircraft on the used market

With just over fifty examples built more than fifty years ago, the ST10 Diplomate almost never appears on mainstream listing platforms. A large share of the original fleet has since disappeared, been deregistered, or is held by collectors and associations dedicated to preserving aviation heritage, like the prototype displayed at the Ailes Anciennes museum in Toulouse.

Finding an example for sale is therefore closer to a collector's pursuit than a conventional touring aircraft purchase: networks of vintage flying clubs, associations dedicated to historic French aviation, word of mouth among GY-80 and ST10 owners, or classified ads specialising in historic aircraft are the most relevant channels for locating one.

Good to know — Given this rarity, it is unrealistic to expect to compare several similar listings to assess a fair price, as you would for a Cessna 172 or a Piper PA-28. Each transaction is negotiated case by case, based on the actual condition of the specific example and its documentation.

What budget to plan

Giving a precise, reliable price figure for a ST10 Diplomate would be misleading: transaction volume is far too low to establish a robust market value, unlike mass-produced models. Price will depend above all on the actual condition of the airframe and engine, the completeness of the technical file, the consistency of the airworthiness history, and the rarity as perceived by both buyer and seller.

Beyond the purchase price, plan for maintenance costs potentially higher than average for this segment, due to the difficulty of sourcing airframe-specific parts and the need for a workshop familiar with vintage French aircraft. A full restoration of a neglected example can represent a substantial investment, to be costed case by case with a specialised mechanic before any commitment.

Points to check before buying

For an aircraft over half a century old, built in very small numbers, the pre-purchase inspection carries particular weight. It must be entrusted to an independent mechanic, ideally familiar with the GY-80 and the ST10, or failing that with French all-metal aircraft of this generation.

  • Condition of the metal structure: corrosion, signs of past repairs, consistency of rivets and skin panels.
  • Operation and upkeep of the electrically-actuated retractable gear, often a more sensitive mechanical point on an older aircraft.
  • Complete history of the Lycoming IO-360 engine: hours since major overhaul (SMOH), compliance with the maintenance program, no internal corrosion linked to prolonged inactivity.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with an unbroken documentary trail since the aircraft was built.
  • Valid certificate of airworthiness (CofA), and verification of compliance with applicable airworthiness directives for the type.
  • Avionics: condition and compatibility with current requirements, as an upgrade can be a significant expense on an aircraft with original-era equipment.

Parts, maintenance and support network

Parts availability is one of the main concerns for a prospective owner. The ST10 shares part of its design with the GY-80 Horizon, which can to some extent ease access to parts or skills common to both types. That said, the airframe and some equipment specific to the ST10 remain unique to it, and sourcing depends largely on existing stock, workshops specialising in vintage aviation, and networks of enthusiast owners.

The Lycoming IO-360 engine, on the other hand, benefits from a much wider maintenance network, having equipped many other light aircraft types: engine parts and workshops qualified for a major overhaul remain considerably more accessible than for the airframe itself.

Who is this aircraft for

The ST10 Diplomate is primarily aimed at a buyer passionate about vintage French aviation, ready to invest time and patience into finding an example and then maintaining it. It is not a sensible choice for someone looking for a simple touring aircraft to operate day-to-day with technical support and parts availability comparable to a Cessna 172 or a Piper PA-28 Cherokee.

For an owner motivated by the aircraft's heritage value and rarity, however, owning an airworthy ST10 Diplomate is a distinctive experience: a 1970s French touring aircraft with respectable performance for its era, of which only a very small number of flying examples remain today.

Long-term upkeep and resale

The same rarity that complicates the purchase works, paradoxically, in the owner's favour at resale time: a well-maintained ST10 Diplomate, with a complete technical file and up-to-date airworthiness, will interest a small but genuinely motivated community of vintage French aviation enthusiasts, who tend to value that rarity rather than discount it.

In all cases, meticulously documenting every intervention, keeping all invoices and the logbook, and staying connected with associations and clubs dedicated to this type of aircraft remains the best strategy, both to secure day-to-day ownership and to prepare for a future sale on good terms.
