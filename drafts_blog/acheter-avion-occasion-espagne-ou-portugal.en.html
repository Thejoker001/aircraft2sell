Buying a Used Aircraft in Spain or Portugal: Which Country to Choose?

Market, prices, AESA or ANAC registration, taxation, climate and running costs: a complete comparison to help you choose between Spain and Portugal for your next used light aircraft purchase.

Two active markets, two different dynamics

Spain and Portugal share the Iberian Peninsula and a climate broadly favourable to light aviation, but their used markets show quite different profiles. Spain benefits from a notably wider and deeper market, driven by a dense network of airfields, significant flight-school activity in the south of the country, and a substantial volume of listings, ranging from microlights to high-performance single-engine aircraft.

Portugal, on the other hand, offers a smaller market, with a number of airfields and clubs well below Spain's. Used listings are therefore less abundant, which can lengthen the search for a specific model, but the supply remains adequate for the most common categories (microlights, touring light aircraft). A Portuguese buyer often widens their search to neighbouring Spain to access a broader choice.

Prices compared by aircraft type

Price ranges remain broadly comparable between the two countries for an aircraft of equivalent type and condition, since the Iberian used market is largely interconnected with the rest of Europe. A few nuances exist nonetheless: in Spain, the density of listings sometimes allows for better-negotiated deals on the most common models, while in Portugal a smaller market can mean longer selling times and, as a result, sometimes more room for the buyer to negotiate.

Registration: AESA vs ANAC

In Spain, aircraft registration falls under the Agencia Estatal de Seguridad Aérea (AESA), which issues registrations with the EC- prefix. In Portugal, the competent authority is the Autoridade Nacional da Aviação Civil (ANAC), with the CS- prefix. In both cases, a purchase between private individuals already registered in the country simply updates the owner of record, while an aircraft imported from another country requires prior de-registration from its original register.

  • Valid Certificate of Airworthiness (CofA) and ARC — check this before signing, in both countries.
  • Bill of sale — dated, signed by both parties, stating the serial number.
  • Liability insurance certificate — required before first use under the new registration, in both Spain and Portugal.
Good to know — Common EASA rules govern transfers between European Union member states, which simplifies the process between Spain and Portugal. Allow generally 2 to 8 weeks depending on how complete the paperwork is, in either direction.

VAT and taxation compared

In both countries, a sale between private individuals is, in principle, not subject to VAT. A sale made by a professional or a company may be subject to VAT, and an aircraft imported from outside the European Union remains liable for import VAT, calculated on its customs value and applied at the national rate of the importing country.

Sale between EU-resident private individualsGenerally VAT-freeGenerally VAT-free

Beyond VAT, it is worth checking the local taxation specific to owning an aircraft (annual taxes, airport fees), which can vary noticeably from one region to another within each country, particularly between a small rural airfield and a busier airport on either side of the border.

Climate and operating conditions

The Iberian climate is broadly favourable to light aviation in both countries, with a high number of flyable days year-round, particularly in southern Spain and Portugal's Algarve. Portugal benefits from a stronger Atlantic influence, with generally milder summer temperatures but somewhat more wind and coastal humidity than inland Spain. Spain, for its part, shows greater climate diversity by region, ranging from a more continental centre to a Mediterranean coastline with conditions close to those found in the Algarve.

This climate factor also has an indirect effect on an aircraft's condition: exposure to salt and coastal humidity, more common at airfields near the Portuguese and Spanish coastlines, can promote corrosion on a poorly maintained aircraft or one stored outdoors, while prolonged sun and heat exposure inland in Spain can accelerate ageing of certain interior fittings and paintwork. In either case, hangarage remains the best protection, regardless of country.

Cost of ownership: hangarage, fuel, maintenance

Ownership costs remain broadly similar between the two countries, with differences depending mainly on the chosen airfield rather than the country as a whole. Hangarage on the Costa del Sol or in Mallorca, for instance, can cost noticeably more than a comparable spot at a less touristy Portuguese airfield, while some Algarve airfields, in high demand during the season, charge rates close to those of major Spanish facilities.

  • Fuel: broadly comparable prices, with occasional differences depending on the airfield and its proximity to a major commercial airport.
  • Hangarage or shelter: significant variation by region and proximity to a touristy or urban hub, in both countries.
  • Maintenance: a denser network of approved workshops in Spain for common models, with sometimes more limited availability in Portugal for less common aircraft.
  • Insurance: generally similar rates, varying mainly by pilot profile, declared use and insured value.

Which country to choose based on your profile

There is no universal answer: the choice between Spain and Portugal mainly depends on your intended use. A buyer who prioritises depth of supply, a well-developed network of workshops and flight schools, and a wide choice of models should look primarily at the Spanish market. A buyer looking for a smaller market, a milder Atlantic climate and a quieter lifestyle may find pleasant operating conditions in Portugal, while accepting a potentially longer search for a specific model.

In either case, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic remains the essential step, regardless of the aircraft's country of registration or location, as does a thorough check of the maintenance logbook and operating history before signing anything.

Tip — Compare Spanish and Portuguese listings for aircraft of equivalent type and condition: this often gives the fairest picture of the real market, beyond preconceived ideas about either country.
