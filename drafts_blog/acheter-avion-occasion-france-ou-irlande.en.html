Buying a used aircraft in France or Ireland: which country to choose

Market, prices, tax, registration and maintenance: a comparison between France and Ireland to help you decide where to search for your used aircraft.

Two markets of very different size

France is one of the largest light aviation markets in continental Europe, with a dense network of flying clubs and airfields across the whole country. Ireland, by contrast, is a much smaller market in terms of the number of aircraft based locally, but it holds a particular place in European aviation: it has historically been a widely used registration and leasing jurisdiction for business and commercial aviation, thanks to a regulatory and tax framework that is attractive to corporate operators.

For a private buyer looking for a single-engine aircraft or a leisure light sport aircraft, these two markets are not really in the same league: France offers a substantially wider choice of light aviation listings, while Ireland stands out mainly on higher-end segments (turboprops, business jets) linked to corporate ownership structures.

Size and profile of the used market

In France, the supply of used light aircraft is abundant and diverse: Cessna 172, Piper PA-28, Robin DR400, Diamond DA40 and a wide range of light sport aircraft can be found in numbers on specialised marketplaces and through flying clubs. The market's turnover is supported by the density of the club network and the high number of private pilot licences issued each year.

In Ireland, the leisure light aviation market is smaller, with fewer aircraft based locally. On the other hand, the country plays a disproportionately large role in business aviation and commercial aircraft leasing worldwide, with many leasing companies having established their headquarters in Dublin or Shannon for tax and regulatory reasons. A buyer looking for a business jet or a turboprop may therefore find interesting selling structures in Ireland that are rarely present on the French market for this type of aircraft.

Comparing the prices charged

For leisure light aviation, price levels remain broadly comparable to those found elsewhere in Europe for the same model and an equivalent maintenance condition, though the smaller supply in Ireland can sometimes mean a narrower choice to compare against before negotiating. For business aviation, the context is different: the presence of an established leasing and ownership hub in Ireland can create interesting buying opportunities from corporate sellers, with transaction conditions specific to this type of professional seller.

In both cases, comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price before committing, regardless of which country the aircraft you want is located in.

English as a working language can be an advantage for a French buyer comfortable with it, in particular for dealing directly with leasing structures or professional sellers based in Ireland, without needing to translate technical documents.

Tax and offshore registration

The tax treatment applicable depends above all on the buyer's status (private individual or company), the country of tax residence and the country in which the aircraft will be registered after the transaction, more than on the single country where the aircraft happens to be at the time of purchase. Ireland is known for its attractive regime for aircraft leasing structures, which explains the concentration of aircraft holding companies in the country, particularly for commercial and business aircraft.

For a private buyer of a light aircraft, this Irish specificity has little direct impact. For a professional buyer or a company considering the purchase of an aircraft held by an Irish structure, it is advisable to have the arrangement precisely reviewed by a tax adviser specialised in aviation before any transaction, particularly regarding intra-EU VAT issues and the transfer of ownership of the holding structure itself.

Registration and administrative steps

An aircraft bought in Ireland can be registered in France, and vice versa, depending on the buyer's choice: keeping the registration of the purchase country is not mandatory. The competent Irish authority is the Irish Aviation Authority (IAA), which manages the registration register under the EI- prefix. As with any registration transfer between two EU member states, the complete technical file must be submitted, the Certificate of Airworthiness (CofA) checked, and the aircraft deregistered from the original register before the new registration.

  • Check the consistency between the logbook, the technical file and the aircraft's actual condition before any change of registration.
  • Plan for administrative lead times between the French DGAC and the Irish IAA, which can vary depending on how complete the file is.
  • For an aircraft previously held by a leasing company, carefully check the chain of ownership and the absence of any lien or mortgage on the aircraft.
  • Budget for ferry-flight costs if the aircraft needs to be brought back to France after the purchase.

Maintenance network and parts availability

France has a very dense network of approved maintenance workshops, covering almost all common light aviation models, with generally good parts availability thanks to the large size of the national fleet. Ireland also has a good quality maintenance network, particularly for business and commercial aviation given the presence of many airlines and leasing companies in the country, but the network dedicated to leisure light aviation is smaller.

For a light aircraft bought in Ireland and then brought back to France, routine maintenance can then be carried out without difficulty by an approved French workshop for the type concerned. For a business or commercial aircraft, it may instead be worth keeping a relationship with an Irish workshop used to that type of fleet, at least for the first checks after the purchase.

Which country to favour depending on your profile

For a private buyer looking for a light aircraft or a leisure light sport aircraft, France remains in practice the richest market in terms of listings and the simplest to approach, with a substantially denser supply and maintenance network. Ireland, on the other hand, becomes a market worth considering seriously for a professional buyer or a company targeting a turboprop or a business jet, thanks to the concentration of leasing structures and institutional sellers in the country.

Good to know — In all cases, a pre-purchase inspection by a qualified independent mechanic remains essential, whether the aircraft is located in France, Ireland or elsewhere in Europe.

Ultimately, the choice between the two countries depends above all on the type of aircraft sought: France for leisure light aviation with a wider choice, Ireland for more specific opportunities on the business aviation and leasing side.
