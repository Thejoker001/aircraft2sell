Best used helicopter under €150,000

With a budget capped at €150,000, the used helicopter market is dominated by piston-engine Robinson R22 and R44 models. A full comparison to help you pick the right one for your use.

Why a €150,000 budget points mainly to piston models

Below the €150,000 mark, the European used helicopter market narrows sharply towards piston-engine machines, for a simple reason: turbine helicopters, even used and with high engine hours, almost always exceed this budget once properly equipped. It is the Robinson R22 and R44 segment, built in very large numbers, that dominates this price bracket on the resale market.

These two models share the same design philosophy — mechanical simplicity, running costs that are reasonable for the category, and widely available spare parts — but address fairly different needs once you look closely at their size, payload and original intended use.

The Robinson R22: the most accessible entry point

The Robinson R22 is a two-seater, originally designed for helicopter pilot training and practice flying. It is historically the most economical aircraft for earning a licence and building helicopter flight hours, thanks to low fuel consumption and some of the lowest hourly running costs on the market for a certified helicopter.

On the used market, a well-maintained R22, with comfortable remaining engine potential and a complete maintenance logbook, generally sells well below the €150,000 ceiling, leaving room to fund a pre-purchase inspection and any necessary repairs without exceeding the target budget.

Good to know — The R22 is a strict two-seater: it is not suited to family use or carrying equipment, but it excels at training, maintaining currency and simply enjoying flying at a lower cost.

The Robinson R44: the most sought-after compromise

The Robinson R44 is the enlarged version of the R22, with four seats, a more spacious cabin and significantly greater payload. It is the model most sought after by private buyers in this budget segment, since it allows for family use or light professional work (travel, aerial photography, survey) while remaining a single-engine piston helicopter with running costs that stay reasonable for the category.

Depending on the year of manufacture, the remaining engine hours before the major overhaul (SMOH) and the avionics fitted, a used R44 can sell within a wide range that often approaches the €150,000 ceiling for the newest or best-equipped examples, while older machines with less remaining engine potential sell well below it.

Budget comparison: purchase, maintenance, insurance

Choosing between the R22 and R44 under this budget cap mostly comes down to the balance you want between purchase price, payload and recurring running costs. The R22, smaller and lighter, remains structurally cheaper to fly, while the R44 involves proportionally higher maintenance, fuel and insurance costs, in exchange for greater versatility.

Beyond the list price, you should always factor in the real cost per flight hour, which depends heavily on the remaining engine hours before the next major overhaul and the intended frequency of use.

Which helicopter for which use

The choice between the R22 and R44 should above all be based on the aircraft's intended real use, far more than on the available budget alone. For a pilot starting out in helicopters, working towards a licence, or simply wanting to build flight hours at a lower cost, the R22 remains the most coherent choice under this budget cap.

  • R22: ideal for training, maintaining currency and a first helicopter purchase on a tight budget.
  • R44: relevant for family use, regular travel or light professional activity requiring passengers or equipment on board.
  • Budget available for annual maintenance and the major engine overhaul: a criterion often more decisive than the initial purchase price.
  • Intended frequency of use, which directly drives the real cost per flight hour over the ownership period.

Conversely, a buyer who already holds a licence and is looking for a versatile aircraft for regular use with others will find in the R44 a better-suited capacity/cost compromise, even if it means getting closer to the €150,000 ceiling for a well-equipped example.

Alternatives worth watching under this budget

Other light piston helicopters exist on the European market, but they remain significantly rarer on the used market than the Robinsons, which narrows your choice and can make sourcing spare parts more difficult. In practice, the R22/R44 duo dominates this price segment thanks to the sheer number of aircraft produced and the availability of technical support across Europe.

For a budget that approaches but does not exceed the €150,000 ceiling, it can be worth widening the search to newer Robinson R44 units with more engine hours, rather than turning to light turbine models such as the Robinson R66, whose acquisition and running budget generally exceeds this envelope by a wide margin.

Points to watch when buying used

Whether it's an R22 or an R44, the pre-purchase inspection should pay particular attention to engine history (total time and time since overhaul, SMOH), the condition of the main and tail rotor blades, and the consistency between the maintenance logbook and the flight logbook. A machine used at a flight school will generally have accumulated more hours, but also more regular and better-documented maintenance.

Word of caution — Always have the pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic approved on the Robinson type, never by a provider recommended by the seller, and ask for the complete maintenance logbook before committing.

Comparing several listings of similar models and model years remains the best way to assess a fair price under this budget cap and spot a well-maintained aircraft, whether it's an R22 or an R44.
