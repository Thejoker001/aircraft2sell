Buying a Used Aircraft in Luxembourg or Switzerland: Which Country to Choose

Market, prices, registration, VAT/taxation and pitfalls to avoid: a complete comparison to help you decide where to buy your used light aircraft between Luxembourg and Switzerland.

Two markets of very different sizes

Luxembourg and Switzerland are both Western European countries with strong purchasing power, but their light aviation markets are very uneven in size. Luxembourg, a small territory, has a very limited number of airfields and a small based fleet: the supply of used light aircraft registered directly in the country remains small, and many buyers or owners based in Luxembourg in practice operate across neighbouring markets (Belgium, France, Germany) to find an aircraft.

Switzerland, where terrain limits the number of airfields but the installed base is much larger, offers a wider and more diverse supply, driven by strong purchasing power and generally rigorous maintenance. There you will find more high-performance single-engines, turboprops and business jets than on the Luxembourg market.

In both cases, EASA airworthiness standards apply, which makes it straightforward to compare the technical condition of a Luxembourg aircraft with a Swiss one, regardless of the respective size of the two markets.

Price comparison by aircraft type

Price ranges remain broadly comparable between the two countries for an aircraft of equivalent category and condition, with price level depending above all on engine history (hours remaining before TBO), airframe condition and installed avionics rather than the country of sale alone. The small size of the Luxembourg market, however, means a more limited direct supply, which may push a Luxembourg-based buyer to widen the search to neighbouring countries to compare more listings.

Registration: DAC versus FOCA

In Luxembourg, the competent authority is the Direction de l'Aviation Civile (DAC), which assigns the LX- prefix. In Switzerland, the competent authority is the Federal Office of Civil Aviation (FOCA / OFAC), which assigns the HB- prefix. In both countries, a purchase between private individuals where the aircraft is already registered locally simply requires updating the registered owner, while an aircraft imported from another country must first be deregistered from its register of origin.

  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and ARC — check this before signing, in both countries.
  • Bill of sale — dated, signed by both parties, stating the serial number, required by both the DAC and FOCA.
  • Liability insurance certificate — required before first use under the new registration, in both Luxembourg and Switzerland.
Good to know — Luxembourg, as an EU member, directly applies the common EASA rules for transfers between EU member states, which are generally simpler and faster (2 to 8 weeks). Switzerland, although part of the EASA system through a bilateral agreement, is not part of the EU customs union: importing from an EU member state, or the reverse, remains possible but requires specifically checking the applicable customs formalities with FOCA.

VAT and taxation: Luxembourg in the EU, Switzerland outside

This is the most important point of difference between the two countries. Luxembourg, as an EU member, applies the common EU VAT regime: a sale between EU resident private individuals is generally VAT-exempt, while a sale by a professional or an import from outside the EU remains subject to import VAT.

Switzerland is not a member of the European Union or its VAT regime. An aircraft brought into Switzerland is in principle subject to Swiss VAT (calculated on the value of the aircraft), except in specific situations that need to be confirmed case by case. Conversely, a Swiss aircraft sold and exported to the EU may require specific customs formalities on entry into EU territory. In both directions, it is essential to have your tax situation confirmed before acquisition, particularly if the aircraft has already changed country of registration several times.

Pre-purchase inspection and documents

Whether the aircraft is from Luxembourg or Switzerland, a pre-purchase inspection by a certified mechanic independent of the seller remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises. It covers the condition of the airframe, the consistency of the engine logbook, maintenance history and the compliance of the avionics with current European requirements (transponder, 8.33 kHz radio) — requirements common to both countries thanks to the application of EASA standards.

  • Up-to-date airframe, engine and propeller logbooks, with no chronological gaps, whether kept in Luxembourg or Switzerland.
  • History of maintenance invoices from the last three years, from an approved workshop in the relevant country.
  • No undisclosed structural damage, verifiable via the maintenance file and a thorough visual inspection.
Point of caution — Never finalise a purchase, in Luxembourg or in Switzerland, without having seen the original logbooks and having their consistency checked by a professional independent of the seller.

Which country to choose depending on your profile

The choice between Luxembourg and Switzerland depends above all on your project and your place of residence. Luxembourg may suit a buyer already based in the country who wants to simplify local administrative formalities, but requires accepting a limited direct supply and widening the search to neighbouring countries to compare more aircraft. Switzerland offers a larger market and attractive high-end configurations, but requires carefully anticipating the customs and tax questions specific to a country outside the European Union, especially in the event of a cross-border import or export.

In all cases, the decisive factor remains the quality of maintenance and the transparency of the aircraft's history, far more than the country of registration alone. Comparing several listings in both countries, as well as in bordering markets, at an equivalent budget and category, remains the best way to make an informed choice.

Tip — If you are considering an import between the two countries, have the applicable customs and tax formalities confirmed in advance by a specialist professional: this avoids unpleasant surprises after signing.
