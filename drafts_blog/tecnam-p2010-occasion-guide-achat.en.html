Why the P2010 appeals to clubs and private owners

The Tecnam P2010 is a four-seat single-engine piston aircraft launched by the Italian manufacturer Tecnam in the early 2010s, designed as a modern alternative to the classic light-aircraft staples like the Cessna 172 or the Piper PA-28. It stands out with a cabin that is wide for its category, a cockpit often fitted with digital avionics as standard, and controlled fuel burn, which has made it an appealing choice for both flight schools and private owners looking for an economical family aircraft.

On the used market, the P2010 remains a relatively recent model compared with the great American classics, which translates into a smaller pool of available aircraft but also into generally younger airframes, often with comfortable remaining engine and airframe potential. This relative youth of the fleet is a strong argument for buyers who want to limit short-term refurbishment costs.

The P2010 versions on the market

The P2010 has gone through several developments since its introduction, with notable differences in engine fitment depending on the series found on the used market. Some versions are fitted with conventional gasoline engines, while more recent variants have been offered with diesel/Jet A1-compatible engines, designed to address fuel availability issues in certain regions.

Before starting any search, it is essential to identify precisely the version and engine fitment of the aircraft in question: this directly determines fuel cost, parts availability and engine maintenance intervals, which can vary significantly from one engine type to another.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used P2010 depends above all on the year of manufacture, total airframe and engine hours, the engine fitted and the level of avionics installed. Being a relatively recent model, even the oldest examples available on the market remain markedly younger than the average used light-aircraft fleet, which is reflected in the entry-level price point.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance, and possibly an avionics upgrade if the aircraft needs one.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For a P2010, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Complete logbook history: hours elapsed since the last major overhaul, strict compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program.
  • Condition of the structure and assemblies, particularly at the junction points between composite elements and internal reinforcements.
  • Operation and updates of the integrated digital avionics, as well as availability of manufacturer software updates.
  • Validity of the certificate of airworthiness (CofA) and full traceability of any modifications or repairs made to the aircraft.
  • Compatibility and availability of spare parts through the Tecnam distribution network, especially for an aircraft imported outside its original market.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

The mixed wood-composite structure: a feature to know

The P2010 differs from the metal classics in its category through a structure combining composite elements and construction incorporating wood in certain parts of the airframe, reflecting an approach rooted in Tecnam's industrial know-how. This architecture, designed to optimise the weight-to-strength ratio, calls for inspection points different from those of a conventional all-metal aircraft.

During the pre-purchase inspection, it is recommended to have these elements checked by a mechanic with specific experience of the type, able to spot any signs of ageing or moisture ingress that would not be detectable through a superficial visual check.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a P2010 remains competitive overall within its category, supported by controlled fuel burn and the relative youth of the fleet, which limits major maintenance work in the short term. The main expense items remain the mandatory annual inspection, insurance, parking and, in the longer term, the major engine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

The network of Tecnam-approved maintenance centres remains less dense than that of the great American classics in some parts of Europe: it is therefore worth checking, before buying, the proximity of a competent shop for the type relative to your intended base of operations.

Tip — Always ask for the last three maintenance invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Typical uses: flight school, club and private travel

The P2010 is widely used by flight schools and aero clubs for training, thanks to its robustness and modern avionics that make learning easier. It is also well suited to private family travel, offering a good balance of cabin comfort, payload and controlled operating costs over medium-distance legs.

An aircraft with intensive flight-school use will generally show a high number of hours but, if it has been rigorously maintained at an approved shop, this does not necessarily rule it out as a purchase: the logbook and maintenance history remain the best indicators of an aircraft's real condition, far more reliable than the hour meter alone.

Resale and the used market

The used P2010 market remains relatively young in Europe, with a fleet expanding gradually over the years, which for now limits the number of comparable listings available at any given time. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and up-to-date avionics, generally stands out more easily on this still thin market.

Comparing several listings of similar models, including other modern four-seaters in the same category, remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
