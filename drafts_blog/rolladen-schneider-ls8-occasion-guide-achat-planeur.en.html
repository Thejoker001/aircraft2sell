The LS8, a well-known performance glider

The Rolladen-Schneider LS8 is a single-seat performance glider designed by the German manufacturer Rolladen-Schneider Flugzeugbau for soaring pilots looking for a fast, agile aircraft that is competitive both in contests and on cross-country distance flights. Its fully composite structure and optimised wing profile have made it a respected reference in its category since launch.

On the used market, the LS8 can be found in different wingspan and equipment configurations, which explains a fairly wide price range depending on the model year, accumulated flight hours and the level of onboard equipment (navigation instruments, oxygen, and on some variants a retractable auxiliary engine).

The LS8 variants to know

The LS8 was produced in several variants over time, with notable differences in wingspan, winglets and auxiliary propulsion depending on the version. Before starting a search, it's essential to identify precisely which variant you're after, since this directly affects in-flight performance and the budget required.

  • Wingspan and wing configuration, which directly affect glide ratio and thermalling behaviour.
  • Presence or absence of a retractable auxiliary engine, which noticeably changes price, weight and maintenance requirements.
  • Onboard equipment: variometer, navigation instruments, oxygen, which may be more or less complete depending on the previous owner.
  • Usage history: a glider used for club training does not have the same flight-hour profile as one owned by a competition pilot.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used LS8 depends above all on the age of the aircraft, accumulated flight hours and number of landings, the overall condition of the composite structure, and the level of onboard equipment. An older example with a heavy club training usage history trades at a noticeably more accessible level than a recent, well-equipped, lightly used aircraft.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: an independent mechanic's or approved gliding workshop's pre-purchase inspection, registration and transfer fees, insurance and, depending on condition, a paint or gelcoat refresh.

What to check before buying

As with any used glider, a pre-purchase inspection by a mechanic or workshop qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For an LS8, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Overall condition of the composite structure: look for any impacts, delamination, gelcoat cracks or poorly documented previous repairs.
  • Complete history of the aircraft: flight hours, number of landings, compliance with the maintenance program and periodic inspections.
  • Operation of the flight controls, airbrakes and, where fitted, the auxiliary engine retraction system.
  • Condition of the landing gear and tyres, which are often heavily stressed during training flights.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with clear traceability of repairs and the certificate of airworthiness (CofA).
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

Composite structure: a key focus point

As with the vast majority of modern performance gliders, the LS8's fully composite structure requires particular attention to the condition of the gelcoat and the detection of possible internal damage that isn't visible to the naked eye. A poorly repaired or undisclosed impact can affect the aircraft's structural integrity without this being immediately obvious during a superficial inspection.

It's recommended to have a thorough check carried out by a workshop specialised in composite glider structures, able to detect suspicious areas through tapping tests or other suitable non-destructive methods, before finalising the purchase.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of an LS8 remains comparable to that of other performance gliders in its category, with the main expense items linked to mandatory periodic inspections, insurance, hangar or trailer storage and, where fitted, the specific maintenance of the auxiliary engine on powered versions.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with composite gliders helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records remain a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the complete logbook and the latest periodic inspection reports before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The LS8 benefits from an active used market in Europe, supported by a significant installed fleet within gliding clubs and by its reputation for performance in its category. A well-maintained aircraft, with a sound composite structure and a transparent history, generally resells more easily than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar performance gliders, taking flight hours, number of landings and the condition of the structure into account, remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale.
