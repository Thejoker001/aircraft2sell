What 30,000 euros really buys in light sport aircraft

A 30,000-euro budget puts a buyer in a comfortable bracket of the used light sport aircraft market: enough to reach relatively recent, well-equipped three-axis aircraft, or a high-end trike, while staying reasonable for a first purchase. Unlike certified aircraft, where this amount is often limited to older examples, light sport aircraft at this budget level can come with a fairly complete package: modern avionics, moderate hours on a Rotax engine, and a wing or airframe in good general condition.

The flip side is a used market that varies widely depending on which family of light sport aircraft you're targeting. Price gaps between a simple trike and a three-axis aircraft fitted with electronic instruments can be significant for the same budget, which makes it essential to define your priority use before comparing listings.

As with any used purchase, the listed price only tells part of the real cost story. Ferrying the aircraft, bringing an incomplete logbook up to a usable state, or upgrading ageing avionics can add several thousand euros on top of the negotiated price.

The weight-shift trike: simplicity and ruggedness

The weight-shift trike remains one of the most economical options, both to buy and to maintain. Its simple mechanics, absence of complex flight controls and generally modest parts cost make it a popular choice for local leisure flying, day trips or initial training.

With a 30,000-euro budget, it's possible to target a recent trike, well equipped with instrumentation and with plenty of remaining hours on its Rotax engine, which limits the risk of having to fund an overhaul in the near term. The trade-off remains a greater sensitivity to weather conditions, especially wind, which narrows the available flying windows compared with a three-axis aircraft.

The three-axis aircraft: comfort and versatility

The three-axis light sport aircraft, flown like a conventional airplane with controls on all three axes, offers more cabin comfort, better crosswind handling and often greater range than a trike. It's the closest formula to a certified light aircraft in terms of handling logic, while keeping operating costs well below it.

At 30,000 euros, the used market offers a wide choice of three-axis aircraft from different generations: from older models with analogue instruments to more recent aircraft with partial electronic avionics. Remaining engine hours and the consistency of the logbook should take priority over the year of construction alone in the final decision.

Good to know — On a Rotax-powered light sport aircraft, always check the remaining hours before the next major overhaul and compliance with the engine maker's maintenance schedule, just as you would on a certified aircraft.

Gyrocopters and paramotors: niche alternatives

At a 30,000-euro budget, used gyrocopters and some high-end two-seat paramotors are alternatives worth considering for a specific use. The gyrocopter appeals for its ability to operate at low speed and its perceived safety in the event of an engine failure, but it requires training and handling different from a classic light sport aircraft.

The paramotor, for its part, remains an entry-level option in terms of operating cost, but with a very different use and comfort level, geared more toward occasional leisure flying than regular travel. Both families remain a minority on the used market compared with trikes and three-axis aircraft, which can limit the number of listings available at any given time.

The criteria that matter more than the sticker price

At a comparable budget, several criteria should take priority over the listed price alone to identify the real best choice.

  • Remaining engine hours before the next major overhaul, weighed against the estimated cost of that work for the engine model concerned.
  • General condition of the wing (trike) or airframe (three-axis), and the date of the last wing replacement or inspection.
  • Consistency between the logbook, the displayed hours and the history stated by the seller.
  • Parts availability for the model, as some smaller brands can have a limited maintenance network.
  • Insurance and parking costs at your usual airfield, which vary by category and by region.
Watch out — A light sport aircraft listed well below market price for its model and engine hours deserves a thorough inspection before any commitment, particularly regarding the condition of the wing or airframe.

How to make the best choice for your use

The "best" light sport aircraft under 30,000 euros depends entirely on the intended use. For mostly local flying, solo or with one passenger, on a minimal maintenance budget, the trike is often the most rational choice. For more regular use, longer trips and a search for comfort closer to a certified light aircraft, the three-axis aircraft is generally the best compromise at this budget level.

Gyrocopters and paramotors remain valid choices for a buyer seeking a different flying experience, but they call for accepting a smaller used market and, for the gyrocopter, factoring in specific training from the start of the project.

In every case, comparing several listings of the same model, checking the logbook's full consistency and having the aircraft inspected by a competent person before buying remain the best guarantees for turning a 30,000-euro budget into a light sport aircraft you'll actually be satisfied with day to day.

Tip — Always set aside at least 10% of the total budget for ancillary costs (inspection, avionics compliance work, additional equipment) before fixing your maximum purchase price.
