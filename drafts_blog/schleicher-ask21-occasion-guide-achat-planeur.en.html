Why the ASK 21 remains a training reference

The Schleicher ASK 21 is one of the most common two-seat gliders in European soaring clubs. Designed by the German manufacturer Alexander Schleicher with an all-composite airframe, it was conceived from the outset as a trainer and aerobatic two-seater able to withstand intensive club use while remaining pleasant to fly for both student and instructor.

Its structural robustness, handling and forgiving nature make it a popular choice both for clubs renewing their trainer fleet and for private owners or co-ownership groups looking for a versatile two-seater that can cover training, advanced dual instruction and an introduction to glider aerobatics.

On the used market, the ASK 21 was built across several decades of production, with minor changes to equipment and instrumentation along the way. This long production run explains the good availability of listings across Europe, but also a wide price range depending on age, airframe hours and skin condition.

ASK 21 and ASK 21 Mi: what's the difference

The base ASK 21 is a pure glider with no engine, relying on a tow plane or a winch to launch. This is the most common configuration found in clubs, with a more accessible purchase and running cost.

The ASK 21 Mi version adds a retractable auxiliary engine, which allows a fully self-launched take-off and relaunch without depending on an available tow plane or winch. This autonomy particularly appeals to clubs and owners wanting to reduce their dependence on towing infrastructure, but it comes at a cost: purchase price is higher, and maintaining the powerplant and its retraction system adds to the airframe's own upkeep.

Before targeting either version, it is worth clearly defining the intended use — club training with a tow plane readily available, or more self-sufficient flying outside a well-equipped club — because the gap in budget and maintenance obligations between the two versions is significant.

What budget should you plan for

The price of a used ASK 21 mainly depends on age, total airframe hours, the condition of the composite skin and, for the Mi version, engine hours and condition. The oldest examples, with heavy club use, trade at a clearly accessible entry level, while recent, well-maintained, low-hour examples owned by a private individual or a small group command a considerably higher price.

Beyond the purchase price, budget for the additional costs: a pre-purchase inspection by an independent, type-qualified mechanic, registration and transfer fees, insurance, plus hangarage, a trailer or a parking spot depending on how you plan to operate the glider.

What to check before you buy

As with any used glider, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent, type-qualified mechanic remains essential before finalising a transaction. For an ASK 21, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Composite skin condition: look for impacts, delamination or past repairs, particularly on the areas most exposed to intensive club use (nose, leading edges, winch-launch attachment zone).
  • Total airframe hours and the history of periodic airworthiness checks (ARC or equivalent), with a complete logbook and no gaps.
  • For the ASK 21 Mi version, engine hours and maintenance, plus proper operation of the powerplant retraction system.
  • Condition of flight controls, cables, bearings and airbrakes, which see repeated use on a glider flown in a training role.
  • Past storage conditions (hangar or trailer): an example regularly kept under cover keeps its composite skin in noticeably better shape over time.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's description of the aircraft's condition: a written independent inspection report, ideally from a workshop approved for the type, remains your best protection against an unpleasant surprise after the purchase, especially for an aircraft that has seen intensive club use.

Maintenance and running costs

An ASK 21's running costs remain considerably lower than those of a comparably sized powered aircraft, which partly explains its appeal to clubs and private owners alike. The main cost items remain the periodic airworthiness check, insurance, storage (hangar or trailer) and, for the Mi version, maintenance of the engine and its retraction system.

Having the aircraft maintained by a workshop approved for the type and familiar with composite materials limits unpleasant surprises and preserves resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance is a strong selling point at any future resale, all the more so for an aircraft that has flown in a club setting with several pilots at the controls.

Tip — Always ask for the complete logbook and the latest periodic inspection reports before making an offer: they give a faithful picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained, especially for an example that came from a club.

Resale and the used market

The ASK 21 benefits from an active used market in Europe, backed by a very large installed base in soaring clubs and good parts availability from the manufacturer and its approved workshops. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and skin in good condition, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one with gaps in its technical file.

Comparing several listings for similar examples remains the best way to judge a fair price, whether you're buying now or preparing a future resale. Price differences between two examples of the same vintage almost always come down to airframe hours, skin condition and documented maintenance quality, as well as the version (pure or self-launching Mi) involved.
