Citation CJ vs Embraer Phenom 100: which entry-level jet to choose

Two entry-level jets, two philosophies

The Cessna Citation CJ (particularly the CJ1, CJ2 and CJ3 variants) and the Embraer Phenom 100 both sit in the entry-level business jet segment, designed for a single pilot or a reduced crew and regional missions. Yet these two aircraft reflect two different design philosophies: the Citation CJ extends a historic lineage built over decades, while the Phenom 100 was designed more recently with a cabin and avionics suite aimed at today's generation of owner-pilots.

Choosing between the two is therefore less about an objective ranking than about matching the intended mission, available budget, and preferences regarding cabin and avionics.

The Citation CJ: the historic benchmark

The Citation CJ family has established itself as a safe bet on the used market thanks to a very dense worldwide maintenance network, proven parts availability and a reputation for ruggedness. Successive versions — CJ1, CJ2, CJ3 — have progressively improved range, cruise speed and payload, offering a wide choice on the used market depending on budget and intended mission.

Its relatively simple handling, long a strong selling point, also makes it an attractive option for an owner-pilot who wants to fly with a reduced crew, subject to a type rating suited to the specific variant.

The Embraer Phenom 100: the modern challenger

Launched more recently than the first generations of the Citation CJ, the Phenom 100 was designed from the outset for the "very light jet" segment, with a wider cabin for a comparable fuselage width, natively integrated Garmin avionics and sound insulation particularly well engineered for this segment. Its more recent design also translates into initial maintenance costs that are often more predictable in the first years of operation, thanks to an Embraer support network structured from the aircraft's entry into service.

In exchange, the Phenom 100 has historically had a less extensive worldwide network of parts and service centers than the Citation, although it has grown considerably since the aircraft's launch.

Budget comparison: purchase and operation

On the used market, acquisition price depends heavily on the model year, the engine hours remaining before the next major overhaul, and the installed avionics equipment. Generally speaking, early Citation CJ generations (CJ1) trade at entry price levels comparable to or slightly below a Phenom 100, while a newer, better-equipped CJ3 sits above it.

Beyond the purchase price, the real cost per flight hour depends heavily on the remaining engine potential, any hourly maintenance program subscribed to, and parts availability for the specific model year in question.

Performance and typical mission

Both aircraft target similar missions: medium-range regional flights, one-off business trips, and access to shorter runways than those used by higher-category jets. The most recent and best-engined Citation CJ versions, notably the CJ3, offer greater range and payload than the Phenom 100, which can make the difference for frequent transcontinental missions or a higher passenger count.

The Phenom 100 remains relevant for shorter missions with a limited passenger count, where its more comfortable cabin and consistent avionics weigh more heavily in the decision than maximum available range.

Cabin and passenger comfort

Cabin width is often the most cited argument in favour of the Phenom 100: for comparable exterior dimensions, its cabin offers more usable space and a better sense of room for passengers seated facing each other. The Citation CJ, particularly on its early generations, offers a narrower cabin, although the CJ3 variants and avionics/interior retrofits have significantly improved perceived comfort over the years.

For mainly personal or family use on short trips, this difference in cabin comfort can weigh more heavily in the final choice than the raw performance gap between the two aircraft.

Buying either one used: points to watch

Whether it's a Citation CJ or a Phenom 100, buying used requires a rigorous check of the logbook, the engine hours remaining before the next major overhaul (SMOH), and compliance with every airworthiness directive applicable to the specific type and model year. A pre-purchase inspection carried out by an authorized service center of the relevant manufacturer remains the best protection, particularly for verifying the history of the engines and installed avionics, which are often modified over successive owners.

Good to know — Always request an up-to-date engine potential report from the engine manufacturer before committing, in addition to the aircraft's logbook.

In all cases, comparing several listings of similar model years and equipment remains the best way to assess a fair price and identify a well-maintained aircraft, whether it's a Citation CJ or a Phenom 100.
