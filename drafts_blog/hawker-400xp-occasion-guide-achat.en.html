Why the Hawker 400XP remains popular in the light jet market

The Hawker 400XP is a twin-engine light business jet, valued for its comfortable cabin, well-established reliability and operating costs that are relatively well controlled for its category. Descended from a long-established lineage in the business aviation market, it remains a sound choice for companies and individuals looking for a jet capable of short regional and transcontinental missions with a good level of cabin comfort.

On the used market, the 400XP comes in several model years with different avionics levels, with some aircraft having received avionics upgrades after entering service. This diversity makes it essential to clearly define the intended usage profile before comparing available listings.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Hawker 400XP depends above all on the age of the aircraft, total airframe hours, engine status (remaining potential before overhaul), avionics level and maintenance history. Older aircraft with reduced engine potential trade at a noticeably more accessible level than recent examples that have received avionics and cabin upgrades.

Beyond the purchase price, the budget should include the ancillary costs specific to a business jet: a thorough pre-purchase inspection at a shop approved on the type, registration and transfer fees, business aviation-specific insurance, and possibly an avionics or cabin upgrade.

What to check before buying

Buying a business jet like the Hawker 400XP requires a particularly thorough pre-purchase inspection, carried out by a maintenance shop approved and experienced on this type of aircraft. Several points deserve specific attention.

  • Complete engine history: engine hours elapsed, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program, status relative to the next major overhaul.
  • Structural condition of the airframe, particularly areas subject to repeated stress from cabin pressurisation cycles.
  • Avionics compliance with current regulatory requirements of the airspace the aircraft is intended to operate in.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with continuous traceability since the aircraft entered service.
  • Validity of the certificate of airworthiness (CofA) and no outstanding airworthiness directives on the specific aircraft.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report, carried out by a shop approved and experienced on light business jets, remains the best protection before an acquisition at this level of investment.

Mission, cabin and day-to-day operation

The Hawker 400XP is positioned for light business jet missions: short regional or transcontinental trips, with a cabin that can comfortably seat a limited number of passengers. Before buying, it's worth precisely defining the intended mission profile (typical distance, frequency of use, usual passenger count) to check whether the aircraft genuinely matches the real need.

Operating a business jet also involves specific operational constraints: managing crew qualified on the type, access to runways compatible with the aircraft's performance, and logistics for parking and regular maintenance.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a light business jet like the Hawker 400XP remains significantly higher than that of a light piston aircraft, due to the complexity of its systems, the cost of parts and specialised labour. The main expense items include scheduled maintenance under a manufacturer program, insurance, fuel, parking and, eventually, the engine's major overhaul.

Having the aircraft maintained at a shop approved by the manufacturer and experienced on the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records are a strong argument at a future resale, especially important in this market segment where professional buyers scrutinise the technical file in detail.

Tip — Always ask for the complete maintenance history under the manufacturer program and the latest invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained and of the engines' compliance with the current program.

Financing and ownership structure

As with any business jet, acquiring a Hawker 400XP can be done through outright purchase, leasing, or fractional/co-ownership arrangements when usage doesn't justify full ownership. The right choice depends on the intended usage profile, the expected annual flight hours and the buyer's financial strategy.

It's advisable to compare the different financing options ahead of purchase, taking into account the fixed costs (insurance, parking, scheduled maintenance) and variable costs (fuel, airport fees) specific to operating a light business jet.

Resale and the used market

The Hawker 400XP benefits from a significant installed fleet across the global business aviation market, which supports parts availability and long-term technical support. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and avionics compliant with current requirements, generally resells more easily than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same model year are almost always explained by remaining engine potential and the level of avionics and cabin upgrades.
