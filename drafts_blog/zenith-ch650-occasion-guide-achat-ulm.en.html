The Zenith CH650: a widely built two-seat kit

The Zenith CH650 is a low-wing two-seater designed by Zenith Aircraft Company for amateur construction from a riveted aluminium sheet-metal kit. A direct evolution of the CH601/CH650 family, it has earned a loyal following thanks to its simple structure, robustness and easy maintenance, which explain its popularity on the used market in Europe as well as North America.

The aircraft offers a relatively roomy cabin for its category, good visibility thanks to its generous canopy, and modest but adequate cruise performance for touring or ab-initio flights. On the used market, available examples almost always come from an individual amateur build, which calls for particular care when assessing build quality before buying.

Light aircraft kit or microlight: status depends on the country

The regulatory classification of the Zenith CH650 is not uniform across Europe: depending on its configuration (maximum weight, engine, stall speed) and on the country where the aircraft is based, it may fall under the amateur-built light aircraft status or under the microlight category (or its national equivalent). This point directly affects the licence requirements to fly it, the applicable technical inspection regime and the registration procedures.

Before buying, it is essential to check with the civil aviation authority of the country where the aircraft will be based which category it is, or will be, classed under, because the same airframe can be registered differently from one country to another depending on its exact configuration and local rules in force. Never assume the status of a given example based solely on its model: the actual configuration (weight, engine) takes precedence over the commercial name.

Good to know — Regulatory status also affects insurance, resale and the ability to fly in other European countries: always check the consistency between the certificate of airworthiness (or its microlight equivalent) and the actual configuration of the aircraft being offered for sale.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Zenith CH650 depends above all on the quality of the amateur build, the engine fitted (several engines are compatible with this kit), accumulated engine hours and the level of avionics installed. A carefully built example, well documented and fitted with a recent engine, trades noticeably higher than an aircraft whose build history is incomplete or unclear.

Amateur build: what to check in the build file

Since this is a kit-built aircraft, the amateur build file is a central piece of the transaction. It should document the entire assembly process, the inspections carried out during construction, and any deviations or modifications made relative to the manufacturer's original plans.

  • Build photos and reports documenting the key assembly stages, particularly structural elements.
  • Inspection reports from the competent authority prior to the aircraft's first flight.
  • Traceability of any modifications made to the original plans, and confirmation of their compliance.
  • Complete history since first registration: successive owners, flight hours, reported incidents.
Point of caution — An incomplete or sparse build file should trigger a price negotiation, or even a thorough structural inspection before buying: the quality of the original assembly determines the aircraft's safety over the long term.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic familiar with Zenith-type amateur builds is strongly recommended. A few points deserve particular attention for this model.

  • Condition of the rivets and aluminium structure: look for any signs of corrosion, cracking or poorly executed re-riveting.
  • Complete engine history: hours elapsed since the last major overhaul, compliance with the engine manufacturer's maintenance program, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Operation and consistency of the panel instruments, as well as compliance with the equipment required in the country of operation.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no chronological gaps or grey areas.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about the quality of the amateur build: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection before buying this type of aircraft.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a Zenith CH650 remains modest compared with a certified single-engine aircraft of comparable power, thanks to a simple structure and generally accessible parts. The main expense items remain the mandatory periodic inspection (depending on the regulatory status chosen), insurance, parking and, in the longer term, a major engine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained by a mechanic familiar with this type of amateur build helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. A rigorous, well-kept logbook remains a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the latest maintenance invoices and the engine hours record (SMOH) before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Common pitfalls

The most common mistakes when buying a used Zenith CH650 mainly stem from underestimating the importance of the build file: accepting an incomplete history, failing to check the consistency between the actual configuration and the declared regulatory status, or neglecting to have the structure checked by a professional before signing.

Tip — Compare several similar listings before committing: the price difference between two examples of the same model is almost always explained by build quality and remaining engine potential.

Resale and the used market

The Zenith CH650 benefits from an active used market, supported by a large installed fleet and good parts availability. A well-built aircraft, with a complete file and regular, well-documented maintenance, generally resells more easily and on better terms than an example whose build history has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between comparable aircraft are almost always explained by the quality of the amateur build and the remaining engine potential.
