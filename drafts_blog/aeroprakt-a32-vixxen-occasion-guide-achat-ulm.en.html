Why the A32 Vixxen appeals to microlight pilots

The Aeroprakt A32 Vixxen is a three-axis microlight of Ukrainian design, launched on the European market as a larger and more modern evolution of the earlier A22 range. Its metal airframe, a cabin that feels spacious for the category, and reasonable cruise performance have made it a popular choice for pilots looking for a versatile aircraft for leisure flying and short trips, without the administrative and budget constraints of a certified aircraft.

On the European used market, the Vixxen remains a relatively recent model compared with other, more established microlights, which limits the number of examples available but generally guarantees a young fleet that is well equipped with modern avionics. This relative scarcity directly influences the price levels seen on the market.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used A32 Vixxen depends mainly on the year of manufacture, the number of engine hours, the level of avionics fitted (digital displays, autopilot) and the overall condition of the airframe and paint. A recent example with low hours and rigorous maintenance documented in the logbook naturally trades at a higher level than an older or less well-maintained aircraft.

On top of the purchase price, budget for the usual ancillary costs: a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic familiar with the type, microlight registration fees, insurance, and possibly an avionics upgrade if the aircraft isn't fitted to your needs.

What to check before buying

As with any used microlight, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic who knows the metal airframe and the Rotax engine well is strongly recommended before finalising a transaction. For an A32 Vixxen, a few points deserve particular attention.

  • Condition of the metal airframe: look for any trace of corrosion, especially around the wing attachment points and the landing gear.
  • Complete Rotax engine history: hours elapsed since the last major overhaul or intermediate service, compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance program.
  • Condition and pitch setting of the propeller, along with any visible impact damage or cracks.
  • Full operation of the onboard avionics and instruments, checking the latest software updates where applicable.
  • Complete logbook and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history or its microlight airworthiness status (CofA).
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase.

Technical file and logbook

Before making any offer, always ask for the aircraft's complete logbook as well as every document that proves its administrative status: microlight registration certificate, proof of current insurance and the history of any modification made by the previous owner. An incomplete file or one containing date inconsistencies should raise a red flag and can justify a lower offer or, in more serious cases, walking away from the transaction.

Traceability of replaced parts, especially on the engine and the propeller, is another key point to check: it determines how much trust you can place in the aircraft's declared history.

Rotax engine: upkeep and remaining potential

The A32 Vixxen is most often fitted with a Rotax 900-series engine, a powerplant widely used across the microlight world and whose maintenance is well documented and accessible at many approved shops in Europe. Following the manufacturer's recommended service intervals, and respecting the intermediate overhaul schedule, are key factors for the aircraft's reliability and resale value.

Before buying, ask the seller for the exact number of engine hours remaining before the next major overhaul, as well as the history of any major work already carried out. An engine close to its hourly limit should be factored into your overall budget.

Microlight regulations in Europe

Microlight regulatory status varies from one European country to another: licensing and flight conditions, maximum weight limits and registration procedures fall under national regulation specific to each state, rather than a single harmonised European framework. It is therefore essential to check the exact rules that apply with the relevant authority or microlight federation in the country where you plan to register the aircraft before finalising a cross-border purchase.

This check matters even more if you are considering importing an aircraft registered in another European country: procedures and timelines can vary significantly between administrations.

Day-to-day operating costs

The operating cost of an A32 Vixxen remains relatively contained compared with a certified light aircraft, thanks to moderate fuel consumption and generally accessible maintenance costs on the Rotax engine. The main expense items remain the periodic technical inspection, insurance, parking at the airfield and, in the longer term, the major engine overhaul once the hourly potential is reached.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value, in a market where maintenance traceability is closely scrutinised by buyers.

Tip — Always ask for the latest maintenance invoices before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Resale and the used market

The A32 Vixxen benefits from a still-young but growing used market in Europe, driven by Aeroprakt's increasing reputation among microlight pilots. A well-maintained aircraft, with a transparent history and up-to-date avionics, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same model year are almost always explained by the level of avionics fitted and the quality of maintenance records.
