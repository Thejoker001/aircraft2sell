Why the Piper Aztec remains a popular twin

The Piper Aztec (PA-23) is a light piston twin produced by Piper starting in the 1960s, designed as a beefed-up evolution of the Apache. With its generous cabin, solid payload capacity and two six-cylinder engines, it became a popular choice for family or professional transport, light business flying and certain utility missions requiring equipment to be carried over medium distances.

On the used market, the Aztec appeals buyers for its capacity-to-purchase-price ratio: for a budget often lower than that of a more recent twin, the buyer gets an aircraft able to carry five to six occupants with luggage, while benefiting from the engine redundancy inherent to twin-engine aircraft. Its rugged structure and long service career also make it a well-documented aircraft, with extensive experience available from specialised workshops and owner associations.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used Piper Aztec varies widely depending on the model year, the condition of both engines (hours since the last overhaul), the installed avionics and the maintenance history. The oldest examples, with reduced engine potential on one or both engines, trade at a markedly more accessible entry level than more recent, well-equipped versions with up-to-date avionics.

Beyond the purchase price, factor into your budget the pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic familiar with the type, registration and transfer fees, insurance — generally higher than for an equivalent single-engine aircraft — and any potential upgrade of the avionics or cabin interior.

The twin-engine specifics: two engines to track

Owning a twin like the Aztec means tracking two engine logbooks, two overhaul schedules and two hour histories in parallel. This dual maintenance represents a higher operating cost than a comparable single-engine aircraft, but in return offers a valuable safety margin in the event of an in-flight engine failure, particularly on trips over mountains, sea or areas poorly suited to an emergency landing.

Before buying, always ask for both engine logbooks separately and check the consistency of flight hours between the two powerplants. A significant gap between the two engines (one close to major overhaul, the other recently overhauled) should be factored into price negotiation, as it signals a significant expense in the near to medium term.

Points to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For an Aztec, certain points deserve particular attention due to its age and twin-engine configuration.

  • Complete history of both engines: hours since the last overhaul, compliance with the maintenance programme, traceability of replaced parts.
  • Structural condition and corrosion check, especially on an older aircraft that may have been parked in a humid or coastal environment.
  • Operation of the retractable landing gear and its associated hydraulic or electrical systems.
  • Condition and updates of the avionics, as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements (transponder, traffic monitoring).
  • Complete flight log, logbook and technical file, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after purchase, especially on an older twin.

What use suits a Piper Aztec

The Aztec is particularly well suited to an owner-pilot looking for good payload capacity for regular family or professional travel, as well as an increased safety margin on demanding routes thanks to engine redundancy. Its spacious cabin makes it a comfortable aircraft for travelling with several passengers and luggage over medium to long distances.

In return, you must accept a higher operating cost than an equivalent single-engine aircraft, linked to maintaining two engines and two propellers, as well as higher fuel consumption. This is a trade-off that remains worthwhile for regular use justifying the investment, less so for an occasional pilot who would be better served by a more economical single-engine aircraft.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of an Aztec remains comparable to other light piston twins in its category: mandatory annual inspection, insurance, parking, and in the longer term, the overhaul of each of the two engines once their hourly potential is reached. Having the aircraft maintained at an approved workshop familiar with the type helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value.

Rigorous, well-documented maintenance tracking of both engines is a strong argument at future resale, all the more so since buyers of twins are generally very attentive to hour consistency and any history of in-flight engine failure.

Resale and the used market

The used market for classic piston twins like the Aztec remains active in Europe, supported by a significant installed base and good parts availability, thanks notably to a long service career and active owner associations. A well-maintained aircraft, with transparent history on both engines and up-to-date avionics, generally resells more easily than an example whose technical file contains grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two Aztecs of the same model year are almost always explained by the remaining potential on both engines and the quality of the avionics equipment.
