The DA62: a modern composite twin

The Diamond DA62 is an all-composite twin fitted with diesel engines, designed to combine the engine redundancy of a twin with controlled fuel consumption, an important argument for owners who cover long distances. Its aerodynamic airframe, integrated glass cockpit avionics and spacious cabin, seating up to seven depending on configuration, make it both a versatile aircraft for family travel and a model favoured by some professional training centres.

On the used market, the DA62 remains a relatively recent aircraft compared with other long-established twins, which translates into a still limited but generally consistent used offering in terms of equipment, since most examples already come with modern digital avionics as standard.

What budget to plan for at purchase

The price of a used DA62 depends above all on the age of the aircraft, airframe hours and the hours on each individual engine, engine status (remaining potential before the next overhaul), and the level of avionics and cabin equipment fitted. As a relatively recent and technologically advanced composite twin, the entry price remains noticeably higher than that of a single-engine aircraft or an older metal-airframe twin.

Beyond the purchase price, your budget should include ancillary costs: an independent mechanic's pre-purchase inspection, registration and transfer fees, insurance, and possibly an avionics or cabin interior refresh if needed.

What to check before buying

As with any used aircraft, a pre-purchase inspection by an independent mechanic qualified on the type is essential before finalising a transaction. For a DA62, a few points deserve particular attention, related to its composite structure and diesel powerplants.

  • Condition of the composite structure: look for any impacts, delamination or undocumented previous repairs, which can be hard to detect without a specialist inspection.
  • Complete history of each engine: hours elapsed since the last overhaul (SMOH), compliance with the manufacturer's maintenance programme, and consistency of hours between the two powerplants.
  • Condition and updates of the integrated avionics, as well as compliance with current regulatory requirements (transponder, traffic-monitoring systems).
  • Availability of technical support and diesel-specific parts in your country, a point worth checking with an approved shop.
  • Complete logbook and technical file for each of the two engines, with no grey areas in the aircraft's history.
Good to know — Never rely solely on the seller's statements about overall condition: a written independent inspection report remains the best protection against a bad surprise after the purchase, especially on a composite structure and a diesel powerplant still relatively recent on the market.

The diesel engines: an asset and a point to watch

The DA62's diesel engines are one of its main selling points: noticeably lower fuel consumption than classic gasoline engines, a significant advantage on long trips or for intensive school use. Since this powerplant is more recent on the light aviation market than traditional gasoline engines, it's advisable to check the availability of technical support and specific parts in your country before buying.

It's also worth asking for the complete history of each engine separately, since the two powerplants can show different flight hours and maintenance status depending on their respective use.

Maintenance and operating costs

The operating cost of a DA62 remains higher than that of a single-engine aircraft, since every overhaul, every propeller replacement or every engine intervention is now calculated for two complete powerplants. The diesel engines' lower consumption, however, partly offsets this extra cost under intensive use, particularly for professional training centres that operate the aircraft for a large number of hours per year.

Having the aircraft maintained at an approved shop familiar with the type and the diesel powerplant helps limit unpleasant surprises and preserve its resale value. Rigorous, well-documented maintenance records for both engines are a strong argument at a future resale.

Tip — Always ask for the latest maintenance invoices for both engines before making an offer: they give an accurate picture of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained.

Which use fits the DA62

The DA62 appeals to a varied range of buyer profiles: private owners looking for a versatile aircraft for family or business travel over long distances, and professional training centres that want to give their students an environment close to modern commercial aviation standards thanks to its integrated glass cockpit.

  • Family or business travel: spacious cabin, greater payload and engine redundancy for long trips.
  • Advanced professional training: glass cockpit avionics close to commercial aviation standards.
  • Expected annual flight hours: a decisive factor in amortising the operating cost of a twin.
  • Need for extra payload (passengers, baggage) for the intended trips.

In all cases, the DA62 justifies its higher investment through a combination of safety, comfort and performance that is uncommon on the light-twin market in its category.

Resale and the used market

The DA62 benefits from a still-young but active used market in Europe, supported by a growing installed fleet and rising interest from professional training centres in this type of aircraft. A well-maintained example, with transparent history for both engines and up-to-date avionics, generally resells more easily and on better terms than one whose technical file has grey areas.

Comparing several listings of similar models and model years remains the best way to assess a fair price, both for buying and for preparing a future resale. Price differences between two aircraft of the same model year are almost always explained by remaining engine potential on both powerplants and the quality of the avionics fitted.
