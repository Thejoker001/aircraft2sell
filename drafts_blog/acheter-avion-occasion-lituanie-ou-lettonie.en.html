Buying a used aircraft in Lithuania or Latvia: which country to choose

Market, prices, tax, registration and maintenance: a comparison between Lithuania and Latvia to help you decide where to look for your used aircraft.

Two small Baltic markets with different profiles

Lithuania and Latvia are two Baltic states, both members of the European Union and the Schengen area, sharing a common currency, the euro, and strong geographic and cultural proximity. Their light aviation markets remain modest in size compared with Western Europe, but each has specific features worth knowing before starting a search for a used aircraft.

Lithuania, more populous than its neighbour, benefits from notable activity around the manufacturing of light aircraft and microlights, with several local manufacturers whose output is exported across Europe. Latvia, for its part, builds on a well-established gliding and aerobatics tradition, with active clubs and a long-standing sporting practice.

Size and activity of the used market

The Lithuanian used-aircraft market benefits from the presence of local light aircraft and microlight manufacturers, which translates into an interesting supply of relatively recent aircraft, sometimes coming directly from national production. This local industrial dynamic gives the Lithuanian market a certain depth in the light and microlight categories.

The Latvian market remains smaller in volume, in line with the country's size, but keeps an active supply of gliders, aerobatic aircraft and classic touring single-engines, supported by a well-established community of flying clubs. In both countries, the number of listings available at any given time remains limited compared with the major markets of Western Europe, which makes it worth widening a search to both neighbouring countries to multiply opportunities.

Comparing prices

Price levels between Lithuania and Latvia remain broadly comparable for the same model and maintenance condition, with no systematic premium or discount in either direction. Variations seen are mostly down to local supply and demand, as well as the relative scarcity of certain aircraft types, in particular aircraft from Lithuanian industrial production, which can trade on favourable terms directly on their home market.

In both countries, comparing several listings of similar models remains the best way to assess a fair price before committing, particularly for locally built aircraft whose value can vary noticeably from one listing to another depending on history and equipment fitted.

Tax and purchase-related costs

As with any transaction in Europe, the applicable taxation depends above all on the buyer's status (private individual or professional), the country of tax residence and the country in which the aircraft will be registered after the sale. A sale between private individuals is generally not subject to VAT in either country, while a sale by a professional or a company may be subject to it under the applicable national rules.

Since Lithuania and Latvia are both part of the eurozone, transactions between the two countries carry no currency risk, which simplifies price comparisons and negotiation. For a cross-border transaction, it is worth checking any intra-EU VAT formalities applicable between professionals, as well as the administrative fees specific to each destination country.

Registration: the Lithuanian CAA versus the Latvian CAA

In Lithuania, aircraft registration is handled by the Civil Aviation Administration of the Republic of Lithuania, under the LY- prefix. In Latvia, it is the Civil Aviation Agency of Latvia that manages the registration register, under the YL- prefix. Both authorities apply common EASA rules for registration transfers between member states, which makes the process considerably simpler than an import from outside the European Union.

  • Valid certificate of airworthiness and ARC — a precondition for any flight under the new registration, whichever destination country is chosen.
  • Deregistration from the register of origin — required before the new registration if the aircraft changes country.
  • Complete technical file — airframe, engine and propeller logbooks to be submitted to the new authority.
Good to know — Generally allow 2 to 8 weeks for a registration transfer between Lithuania and Latvia depending on how complete the file is, a timeline comparable to that observed for any intra-European transfer.

Maintenance network and parts availability

Lithuania benefits from a maintenance-shop network connected to its local microlight and light aircraft production, which makes parts easier to access for these specific aircraft types. Latvia, for its part, has workshops experienced with gliders and aerobatic aircraft, a specialty that reflects the country's well-established sporting tradition.

The geographic proximity between the two countries often makes it possible to have an aircraft maintained in either country with no major constraint, provided the chosen workshop is properly approved for the type concerned. For a Lithuanian-built aircraft, it can be worth favouring a workshop in Lithuania, closer to the production line and therefore often better informed about any manufacturer feedback and technical bulletins.

Which country to favour depending on your profile

For a buyer interested in a recently built microlight or light aircraft, Lithuania deserves particular attention, as local manufacturers offer a direct supply that is sometimes more favourable than on Western European markets. For a buyer looking for a used glider or aerobatic aircraft, the Latvian market, supported by an active club community, often offers a more relevant choice and sellers familiar with this type of aircraft.

A pilot already based in the Baltic region will find a practical benefit in widening a search to both countries with no currency risk or complex formalities, thanks to their shared membership of the eurozone and the European Union. Overall ownership costs should also factor in ferrying: an aircraft spotted far from your home base will add extra cost and delay before it can first be used, whether it is located in Lithuania or in Latvia.

Good to know — In all cases, a pre-purchase inspection by a qualified independent mechanic remains essential, whether the aircraft is located in Lithuania, Latvia or elsewhere in Europe.

Ultimately, widening your search to both markets often provides access to a broader supply and better negotiating opportunities, especially when the profile you are after matches more closely one country's specialty than the other's. Taking the time to calmly compare listings available in both countries, rather than limiting yourself to the geographically closest market, remains the best strategy for finding the aircraft best suited to your needs.
