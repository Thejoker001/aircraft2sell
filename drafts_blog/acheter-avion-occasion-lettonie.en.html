The Latvian used aircraft market

Latvia is a relatively small general aviation market compared with the large Western European countries, but it has an active community of owner-pilots and a network of aerodromes and microlight strips spread across the country, especially around Riga. As a member of the European Union and the Schengen area, Latvia benefits from simplified registration transfers and customs formalities with other member states.

The supply of used aircraft directly available in Latvia remains more limited than in the large Western European markets, with a significant share of microlights and light touring single-engine aircraft, plus a handful of gliders. Many Latvian buyers also look at listings in neighbouring countries (Lithuania, Estonia, Poland, Finland) to widen their choice, since transferring an aircraft between EU member states remains relatively straightforward.

Average prices by aircraft type

Price ranges observed in Latvia are broadly in line with the rest of the European Union for comparable aircraft, with occasional opportunities on older airframes due to somewhat lower local demand than in the larger Western markets.

Registration with the Latvian CAA

Any aircraft based and operated in Latvia must be registered with the Latvian Civil Aviation Agency (CAA), the country's civil aviation authority, under the YL- prefix. For a purchase between private individuals already registered in Latvia, the transfer is a simple update of ownership with the CAA. For an aircraft imported from another country, deregistration from the original registry is required before the new registration, as with any registration transfer in Europe.

  • Valid Certificate of Airworthiness and ARC — check this before signing anything; the expiry date determines the first flight under your name.
  • Bill of sale — dated, signed by both parties, mentioning the serial number.
  • Liability insurance certificate — required before the first use under the new registration.
Good to know — Common EASA rules govern the transfer between member states and make it simpler than importing from outside the European Union. Expect generally 2 to 8 weeks depending on how complete the file is, a timeframe comparable to any registration elsewhere in Europe.

VAT and taxation

A sale between private individuals in Latvia is generally not subject to Latvian VAT (PVN). However, a sale carried out by a professional or a company may be subject to it, and an aircraft imported from outside the European Union remains subject to import VAT calculated on its customs value. It is recommended to have your tax situation validated before the purchase, particularly if the aircraft has already changed registration country several times.

SituationTax treatment
Sale between EU resident private individualsGenerally VAT-exempt
Sale by a professionalVAT potentially applicable
Import from outside the EUImport VAT, standard national rate

Pre-purchase inspection and documents

Before signing anything, a pre-purchase inspection carried out by a certified mechanic independent of the seller remains the best protection against unpleasant surprises. It covers airframe condition, consistency of the engine logbook, maintenance history and compliance of the avionics with current European requirements (transponder, 8.33 kHz radio).

  • Up-to-date airframe, engine and propeller logbooks, with no gap in chronology.
  • Maintenance invoice history for the last three years.
  • No undeclared structural damage (verifiable via the maintenance file and a thorough visual inspection).
  • For an aircraft that spent long periods outdoors over winter, a thorough corrosion check is essential given the Baltic climate.
Point of caution — Never finalise a purchase without having seen the original logbooks and having their consistency checked by a professional: this is the best indicator of how seriously the aircraft has been maintained, particularly for an aircraft that may have been parked outdoors through Baltic winters.

Pitfalls to avoid

The most common mistakes when buying in Latvia are the same ones seen everywhere in Europe: neglecting to check the deregistration from the previous registry for an imported aircraft, underestimating certificate of airworthiness transfer costs, or signing without having the exact condition of the optional equipment listed in the specification sheet confirmed in writing. Given the smaller size of the local market, it is also worth taking the time to compare with listings from neighbouring Baltic and Nordic countries before deciding.

Another frequent pitfall: underestimating the logistics if the aircraft needs to be transported or flown in from another country. Between simplified customs formalities within the European Union and organising a ferry flight or road transport for a disassembled microlight, it is better to plan for this item in your timeline and budget rather than discover it after signing. It is also worth checking the availability of spare parts and an approved maintenance shop for the chosen aircraft type: some rarer models on the Baltic market may require using a shop located in a neighbouring country for routine maintenance.

Finally, always ask the seller why they are selling and the reason for any extended period of the aircraft sitting unused: an aircraft that has not flown for a long time often needs a more thorough return-to-service process than a simple pre-purchase check, particularly on the fuel system and batteries.
